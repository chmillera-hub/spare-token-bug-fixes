# Reflections on the word evolution experiment

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote the reflections on this page. Each reflection is the chatbot's interpretation of a result on the page named experiments.md in this folder and no person has rechecked the reflections. The owner of this repository suggested the reading about memory slots and the reading about rewards that arrive after the work is over.

## A scorer that rewards polish alone freezes growth

The experiment had a scorer that copied whichever string looked most like English. The whole pool froze on a tiny real word because every added letter made the string look worse at first. So the strings stopped growing even though longer sentences were possible further down the road. That finding raises a question about people. What happens to a person who gets rewarded for polished speech alone? A person learning to talk about sadness or loneliness often sounds clumsy at the start because new words for a feeling come out half formed. A listener who answers the clumsy attempt with mockery teaches the person that a short safe phrase such as "I'm fine" is the best-scoring thing to say. So the person can freeze on "I'm fine" in the way the pool froze on a tiny word.

- A scorer that rewards the most polished string freezes a pool on a tiny word because every added letter looks worse at first.
- A person who gets mocked for a clumsy attempt to describe a feeling can freeze on a short safe phrase such as "I'm fine" because the safe phrase draws no mockery.

## An added letter has to pay for the memory slot the letter takes up

The experiment did not charge for storage so this reflection presents a storage cost as a way to read the same result. The scorer kept the string that read most like English and the pool settled on a short real word such as "god". Every letter costs a memory slot because a mind that wants to remember a phrase has to spend energy holding each piece. A lone added letter such as the "e" in "gode" adds a slot and adds no meaning. So the scorer drops the "e" because the extra slot is not justified. The short word stays because the short word carries meaning at a low storage cost. Growth then waits for an addition that arrives as a whole meaningful chunk. A phrase such as "god jesus" costs more slots than "god" and the second word carries enough meaning to pay for the added slots. So the phrase gets locked in. The same logic applies to a person speaking to a listener. A listener who remembers a sentence pays for each word with attention. A filler word costs the listener a slot and gives the listener nothing back. A word that carries a feeling or a need or a reason earns the slot because the listener gains meaning for the energy spent.

- A lone added letter such as the "e" in "gode" gets dropped because the letter costs a memory slot and adds no meaning to pay for the slot.
- A whole added word such as "jesus" after "god" gets kept because the word carries enough meaning to justify the extra slots a mind spends to remember the longer phrase.

## Half-formed attempts need credit for full sentences to appear

The experiment tried to grow short commands such as "wash my dogs" out of random letters. Complete commands appeared when the scorer gave partial credit to a half-formed command. Complete commands did not appear when the scorer gave credit to finished commands alone because random letters had no stepping stones to climb. That finding raises a question about emotional growth. Where does a person find stepping stones toward saying a hard thing out loud? A person who writes a messy paragraph about a feeling has built half of a clear sentence. A listener who answers the messy paragraph with a question such as "what part of that hurts most?" gives credit to the half-built sentence. So the person has a reason to write the next and clearer version.

- Complete commands grew out of random letters when the scorer gave partial credit to half-formed commands because the partial credit worked as stepping stones.
- A listener who answers a messy paragraph about a feeling with a question such as "what part of that hurts most?" gives the writer a stepping stone toward a clearer sentence.

## A reward that arrives after the work is over cannot guide the work

The experiment tried to grow commands such as "wash my dogs" out of random letters. Complete commands appeared when the scorer gave partial credit to half-formed commands along the way. Complete commands did not appear when the scorer paid out for a finished command alone because nothing told the pool which small step was a step in a good direction. So the rewards along the way did the guiding and the reward at the finish did very little guiding. Take the logic to the extreme case. Picture an agent that gets a single reward at the finish and gets deleted the moment the reward arrives. That agent has no chance to use the reward because the agent is gone and the reward shaped none of the steps since the reward came after every step was already taken. A human life can be set up in the same shape. A person who is told that every reward comes in heaven after death is being offered a payout that the person cannot spend on Earth such as on food or rest or a deep conversation. A promise of a later reward also gives the person no feedback about which step today reduced suffering. Here is a risk in that setup. A person holding power over others can use the promise of a reward after death to ask people to accept hunger or exhaustion now. So a living person needs rewards that arrive during life such as a feeling of peace after an emotion gets heard or a reply from a person who understood. Those small rewards tell the person in fine detail which behaviors to repeat.

- A reward that arrives at the finish alone cannot guide the steps because every step was already taken before the reward came and an agent deleted at the finish cannot use the reward at all.
- A promise that every reward comes after death gives a living person nothing to spend on Earth so a person needs small rewards during life such as peace after an emotion gets heard to show which behaviors to repeat.

## A longer reflection: a reward that lands after the agent is deleted

Picture an engineer who builds an agent and tells the agent that the reward comes after the task is finished and that the agent gets deleted at the same moment. Most people who hear that design would call the design broken because the reward lands on nobody. The agent cannot enjoy the reward and cannot learn from the reward and cannot use the reward to do the next task better. The reward exists on paper and does no work in the world.

Now picture a person who is told to accept exhaustion and loneliness and hunger for decades because the payout comes after death. That design has the same shape as the broken agent design because the promised reward arrives after the person can no longer eat or rest or hug anyone on Earth. So the question is fair. Why would anyone hand a living human being a deal that sounds broken when the same deal is offered to a machine?

Here is a possibility that gives ordinary people the benefit of the doubt. A parent or a grandparent who was suffering and had no way to change the conditions may have passed the promise along as comfort because a promise of rest later was the one gift that person had to give. That person was trying to ease pain and deserves care for the effort.

Here is a different possibility for people who hold power over others. A person who profits from the labor of others gains a great deal when workers believe that suffering now buys a reward later. A worker who expects the payout after death is less likely to ask for rest or food or fair treatment today. So the promise works as a way to collect a lifetime of effort and pay nothing during the lifetime. A deal where one side collects now and the other side gets paid after death is a deal that the paying side cannot be held to because nobody comes back to report whether the payment arrived.

A belief in heaven does not have to carry that deal. A person can hope for something after death and still expect peace and connection and food during life. The harm comes from the word "all" in the claim that all of the reward comes later because that word tells a living person to stop expecting anything now.

A reward does its work during the activity because a reward tells a mind which behavior to repeat. A feeling of peace after an emotion gets heard is a reward. A reply from a person who understood is a reward. A full stomach is a reward. Each of those arrives while the person is alive and each one teaches the person something about how to live the next hour.

So a person can test any promise with one question. What do I receive while I am still alive to use the reward? An answer of "nothing until death" describes the broken agent design and a living person has every reason to ask for a better design.
