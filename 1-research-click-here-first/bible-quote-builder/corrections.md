# Bible quote builder: corrections

This page lists lines that an AI model (Claude) wrote for the Bible quote builder and that Charles flagged as wrong on 11 November 2026 and 12 November 2026. Each entry shows the line before the fix and the line after the fix and the reason for the fix. The page exists because two lines that both sound caring can differ in whether the line helps or hurts a reader and a matched pair shows where the edge is.

The quoted lines keep their own punctuation. The numbers in brackets point to the rule on the page named rules.md in this folder.

## Emotions come first [rule 33]

**Before:** tiredness without reward makes sense  
**After:** tiredness warns that toil without reward wastes human strength  
**Why:** the words "makes sense" give tiredness a pat on the head and never say what the tiredness is telling the worker and a reader could even hear approval for toil that brings no reward.

**Before:** disliking labor which purchases neither house nor travel is understandable  
**After:** dislike of labor which purchases neither house nor travel signals missing reward  
**Why:** the word "understandable" is a bare verdict that leaves the reader guessing what is being approved.

**Before:** joyless work holds no mystery  
**After:** joylessness at work reports something true: effort returns too little  
**Why:** the first line explains the feeling from a distance and the second line says what the feeling points toward.

**Before:** feeling crushed does not prove life is over  
**After:** feeling crushed tells how heavy the load is  
**Why:** the first line argues against the feeling and the second line treats the feeling as a report.

**Before:** fear need not win  
**After:** fear deserves hearing since rent stays due  
**Why:** the first line tells fear to lose and the second line gives fear a hearing and names the reason for the fear in the same sentence.

**Before:** doubt is normal whenever something new appears  
**After:** doubt asks whether something new can be trusted  
**Why:** the word "normal" is a bare verdict and the second line says what doubt is doing.

**Before:** my soul is exceedingly sorrowful  
**After:** my soul is exceedingly sorrowful, since death drew near  
**Why:** an emotion word that stands alone gives the reader a label with no reason.

**Before:** therefore dont be afraid  
**After:** therefore dont be afraid alone  
**Why:** the first line tells fear to stop and the second line offers company to a frightened person.

**Before:** if dread speaks, reply, "get behind me, satan"  
**After:** if cruelty speaks, reply, "get behind me, satan"  
**Why:** the first line sends dread away as an enemy and dread is an emotion that deserves a hearing.

**Before:** now pull grief up by its roots  
**After:** now tend grief gently by its roots  
**Why:** the first line treats grief as a weed to rip out.

**Before:** I hope people near an angry person ask something similar.  
**After:** I hope people near a person who is angry ask something similar.  
**Why:** nearly every human being feels anger at some time and the label "an angry person" makes a passing emotion sound like a fixed quality of one unusual person [rule 50].

## No orders and no hopes aimed at the reader's body [rule 35]

**Before:** Tired bodies deserve sleep between attempts; I hope yours gets some.  
**After:** Tiredness after such nights reports how much got spent; I hope people see that cost.  
**Why:** the reader alone decides when the reader sleeps and a stranger who hands out sleep sounds like a boss or a doctor that nobody asked for.

**Before:** rest your sore leg, then tell everyone how long this took  
**After:** tell everyone how long this took  
**Why:** the first line orders the reader to do something with the reader's body.

**Before:** Go ahead, laugh  
**After:** Whoever wants may laugh  
**Why:** the first line tells the reader to laugh.

**Before:** Visit your neighbor and tell him  
**After:** A neighbor could visit and tell him  
**Why:** the first line tells the reader to go somewhere.

**Before:** Tiredness says: stop pouring strength where nothing stays  
**After:** Tiredness reports strength being poured where nothing stays  
**Why:** the first line gives the body a stop order.

## High-certainty words need evidence in the same sentence [rule 36]

**Before:** perseverance builds proven character, then hope  
**After:** perseverance growing into soul level resonance with God, then hope  
**Why:** the word "proven" makes the reward of long effort sound like a certificate handed out by a judge and the reward is something a person feels inside.

**Before:** size never proves worth  
**After:** I do not count size as worth  
**Why:** the words "never" and "proves" claim evidence that the sentence does not hold.

**Before:** They will build houses and inhabit them  
**After:** One prophet promised they will build houses and inhabit them  
**Why:** the word "will" needs a named speaker so the reader knows who is making the promise.

**Before:** giant projects fade  
**After:** giant projects may fade  
**Why:** the first line states a guess as a fact.

**Before:** Wisdom shows itself through how a tower helps humans speak  
**After:** I look for wisdom in how a tower helps humans speak  
**Why:** the word "shows" claims proof and the second line says what the speaker does.

## No vulnerability labels and no pushing one group down [rules 38 and 39 and 45]

**Before:** When you make a feast, ask poor people, because rich neighbors might also return that favor.  
**After:** When anyone makes a feast, I hope invitations go widely, so each table gets filled.  
**Why:** the label "poor people" turns human beings into a category that waits for pity and the first line keeps wealthy neighbors at the center as the givers of favors.

**Before:** I hope hiring promises reach janitors, not just executives, since lost wages bring hunger.  
**After:** I hope hiring promises reach everyone, since lost wages bring hunger.  
**Why:** the first line builds two groups and lifts one group by standing on the other group and the word "everyone" leaves nobody out.

**Before:** Those voices merit attention before any executive's promise.  
**After:** May job promises reach everyone, since missing wages bring hunger.  
**Why:** the first line ranks one group of people above another group.

**Before:** displaced drafters  
**After:** drafters whom owners replaced  
**Why:** the second wording names the people whose choice caused the loss of work.

**Before:** I want hungry people fed first  
**After:** neighbors lacking bread  
**Why:** the label "hungry people" names a category and the second wording describes a situation.

**Before:** the storyteller praises patient digging over quick fruit  
**After:** the storyteller honors patient digging  
**Why:** the first line ranks slow workers above quick workers.

**Before:** many young workers earn rent only  
**After:** many workers earn rent only  
**Why:** the word "young" adds nothing to the meaning and splits adults into groups and hints that other adults are left out of the care.

## Human beings rank above food and rent [rule 44]

**Before:** Counting rent and bread before babies is care; I hope pay rises for families.  
**After:** A baby outranks rent and food, so whoever sets prices and wages owes families enough.  
**Why:** the first line puts a baby beneath a rent bill and praises people for holding back from having children and takes a shortage that landlords and employers created and hands the shortage to everyone else as a duty on their bodies.

**Before:** Shelter came before children; I hope homes become affordable.  
**After:** The baby came first and the town owed a bed, so I hope landlords open rooms.  
**Why:** the first line ranks a house above a child and the second line says the duty to provide sits with the town.

## Hopes name what other people do and never promise anything for Charles [rules 43 and 48]

**Before:** I hope things get better  
**After:** I hope employers offer wages big enough for homes.  
**Why:** a hope with no action is a person staring into the distance and the second line names who could do what.

**Before:** I hope to bring food to anyone welcoming a child.  
**After:** I hope neighbors bring food to anyone welcoming a child.  
**Why:** the first line makes a promise in the name of Charles who is one person sharing Bible quotes and who does not have food or money or time to hand out.

**Before:** I will keep asking builders to choose that.  
**After:** I hope builders choose that.  
**Why:** the first line commits Charles to a task that Charles never took on.

**Before:** I hope to carry what you wrote to other people so that weight gets heard.  
**After:** I hope someone who sees your words answers you.  
**Why:** the first line still says what Charles will do later and the second line names what another person could do.

## No cliffhangers [rule 47]

**Before:** they publicized widely the saying which was spoken to them about this child  
**After:** and repeated an angel's words, good news of great joy which will be to all the people  
**Why:** the first line points at a saying and then withholds the saying.

**Before:** Then he gave a cruel order about newborns.  
**After:** Then he told the midwives to kill newborn sons.  
**Why:** the first line hides what the order was and the second line names the violence plainly as a report with the ruler as the one who gave the order.

## No compression [rules 32 and 49]

**Before:** Workers feared hunger because one day's harvest left them idle.  
**After:** The whole harvest came in at once, so he stopped hiring. Those workers lost their wages and feared hunger.  
**Why:** the first line squeezes three steps into seven words and leaves the reader to rebuild the missing steps alone.

**Before:** torn gear means no bread  
**After:** If it tore, the catch would escape, and their families would go without bread, so fear rose.  
**Why:** the first line is a clipped phrase that a reader has to decode.

**Before:** A flood of results can break one net or fill every boat.  
**After:** The catch was too big for one boat, so the crew called partners.  
**Why:** the first line leans on a picture and the second line says what happened.

**Before:** a handful, with quietness  
**After:** a handful of people, with quietness  
**Why:** a reader could ask "a handful of what?" and two added words lock the meaning in.

**Before:** let this cup pass away  
**After:** let this cup of suffering pass away  
**Why:** a reader could ask "a cup of what?".

**Before:** the last will be first  
**After:** the last workers hired received full pay too  
**Why:** the first line does not say the last of what and the first line also swaps the rank of two groups.

## General audience [rule 29]

**Before:** The door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed.  
**After:** The door is now shut, and my children are asleep.  
**Why:** the Bible story comes from a one-room house and a present-day reader pictures children in a parent's bed and stops to ask what is going on.

## Story form [rules 40 and 42 and 46]

**Before:** This is my story about hired vineyard workers and my sayings about wages and bread: some landowner found men waiting late and said: Why do you stand here all day idle? They answered: Because no one has hired us.  
**After:** Some landowner found men still standing here all day idle because no one has hired them.  
**Why:** the introduction and the frames with colons sound like a narrator stepping in front of the story again and again.

**Before:** Plainly: the storyteller counts every worker as deserving full pay  
**After:** The storyteller counts every worker as deserving full pay  
**Why:** the starter word is the same narrator chiming in.

**Before:** a body built from six to nine separate sayings where no word at all may repeat  
**After:** one Bible story told from start to finish where small connector words such as "I" and "and" may repeat  
**Why:** a storyteller who cannot say "I" twice cannot say "I did this and then I did that" and the paragraph turns into a row of proverbs with no thread.

**Before:** a body that only names the pain or that jumps straight to comfort  
**After:** a body with a bad beginning and a middle where someone figures out what to do and an ending with a humane and nonviolent action  
**Why:** the three parts carry a reader from the hurt through the thinking to an action that a person can take.

## Grammar [rule 37]

**Before:** He hoped friends may receive him into their houses.  
**After:** He wished friends would receive him into their houses.  
**Why:** the word "hoped" is past and the word "may" is present and a clash in tense makes a reader stop and reread.

**Before:** somebody tells them, "Go in peace," yet nobody gave anything the body needs  
**After:** somebody tells them, "Go in peace," yet nobody gives anything the body needs  
**Why:** the words "tells" and "gave" sat in two different tenses inside one sentence.
