# The current rules of the Bible quote builder

This page lists every rule that is in force for the Bible quote builder as of 11 November 2026 so that another person or another AI model can repeat the method.
The Bible quote builder is a working name for an experiment where short phrases from the World English Bible are shuffled by a program and an AI model (Claude) then connects the phrases into a readable passage under rules that Charles set one at a time.
Each rule below comes with the reason Charles gave or the reason the rule turned out to matter because a rule with no reason is hard for another person to apply well.

Warning about changed wording: passages made under these rules are not exact Bible quotes because the rules allow added words and changed pronouns and replaced names and replaced repeated words which means a reader who wants the exact wording needs to open the World English Bible itself.

## The source text and the three selections

- The text is the World English Bible because that translation is free for anyone to copy.
- The Claude selection is 35 passages picked by the AI model from books outside My Personal Bible set and outside the four gospels (for example Genesis 1 and the tower of Babel in Genesis 11 and Job 28 and First John 4) and the selection holds no passage from First Corinthians or Second Corinthians from 11 November 2026 onward because Charles added those two books to My Personal Bible set.
- My Personal Bible set (called the Charles selection on the older pages) is eleven whole books which are Psalms and Ecclesiastes and Isaiah and Jeremiah and Matthew and Mark and Luke and John and Romans and First Corinthians and Second Corinthians (Charles removed Proverbs and added the two letters to the Corinthians on 11 November 2026).
- The words of Jesus selection is the words that red-letter Bibles print in red in Matthew and Mark and Luke and John.
- A program cuts the text into key phrases of about four words at punctuation marks and before linking words such as "and" and "for" because a phrase of that size usually makes some sense alone.

## How the phrases get shuffled

- Method A (copying agents): ten agents copy and paste key phrases and a simple reader favors strings whose neighboring phrases are also neighbors in the Bible which rebuilds true stretches of the text in a shuffled order.
- Method B (dealing): the phrases of one chapter are dealt at random into four piles and each pile is turned into one paragraph using every phrase in the pile.
- Method C (random set in random order): a program draws 8 or 16 phrases at random from a selection and shuffles the set and the AI model must keep that exact order because a fixed random order tests whether joining alone can make sense of unrelated phrases.

## Rules for connecting the phrases

1. Stars mark every added or changed word because a reader needs to see which words came from the Bible and which words came from the AI model.
2. In method A and method B the stretches may be placed in any order because a free order lets every sentence pair a claim with a reason and in method C the drawn order must be kept.
3. Words inside a phrase stay in their Bible order because the phrase is the unit the experiment is testing.
4. Joining words may be added anywhere (for example "and" and "because" and "so" and "but" and "while" and "those" and "here") because the cutting removed the links between thoughts.
5. A naming word that already appears in the passage may be reused anywhere because that reuse says who or what a sentence is about without bringing in an outside subject.
6. Each selection has special words that may be added anywhere which are about 12 of its most used naming words and action words and describing words (for the words of Jesus the list starts with father and man and son and god and kingdom) because an action word lets a gap hold a whole small claim.
7. The name "yahweh" is always replaced by the word "god" because Charles wants one word for God and keeps the word "lord" because "lord" connects with Christ for Charles.
8. Any pronoun may be added or deleted or swapped for another pronoun or for a naming word anywhere because a passage full of "he" and "him" and "they" leaves a reader guessing who is meant.
9. A word may change its form when a swap would break the grammar (for example "i have told" becomes "jesus has told") because a correct sentence is easier to follow.
10. The word "the" may be added or removed anywhere because the cutting often leaves a phrase with a missing or doubled "the".
11. Periods and commas and question marks and quote marks may be added anywhere because the cutting removed every mark and quote marks show who is speaking.
12. A rarely used name is replaced with a generic phrase that makes sense (for example "tyre and sidon" became "two foreign cities") because a reader who has not studied the Bible will not know the name and widely known names such as John and Jerusalem stay.
13. Passages from the words of Jesus are read as spoken by Christ which means the added words avoid the name "jesus" and use "i" or "the son of man" because the speaker in the gospels rarely uses that name for himself.
14. As few words as possible repeat inside one passage as long as the readability grade stays high because Charles wants every word to be different.
15. Almost any English word may be added and a repeated word inside a phrase may be replaced or deleted when the unique word rule would otherwise hurt readability because the short special list forced clipped wording.

16. When Charles gives an input quote such as a headline the AI model may add or delete or change any word so that the passage reads as a plausible answer or reflection on that quote as long as the change raises readability or relevance because Charles wants the builder to respond to a topic and not only to shuffle text.

17. When Charles gives an input quote the passage opens with one sentence in the form "when you thought of ... i thought of ..." and the sentence has no length limit and must give full context which means the first half restates in plain words the part of the input quote being answered and the second half says which Bible material the passage draws on because a reader who has not seen the input quote needs to know what is being answered.
18. The opening sentence is exempt from the unique word rule and carries no stars because the AI model writes the whole sentence and a clear sentence needs to repeat small words.

19. After the remixed passage the AI model adds a second paragraph of one or two sentences that explains in plain words what the passage just said and the explanation may reuse words from the passage or the input quote and no word may repeat inside the explanation because a reader gets the point fastest from a short plain summary.

20. Every sentence that the AI model adds is checked for claims about how people get treated or how the world works and a claim stays only when the source phrase supports the claim or when ordinary experience supports the claim because a comforting line that is false (for example "nobody gets scolded over unbelief") tells a reader who was argued with or shunned or abandoned for not sharing a belief that the treatment never happened and a claim that fails the check is reworded to match the source or removed or recast as a stated hope in the voice of the speaker (for example "i want a world where nobody gets scolded over unbelief" or "someday i believe nobody will get scolded over unbelief") because a hope tells a reader what the speaker wishes for and makes no false statement about how people are treated today.

21. Before every output the AI model makes one more pass over the word choices with a lean toward valuing human beings who put their heart and soul into words and the pass says truthfully that a person who speaks up may be dehumanized or told a real experience did not happen or abandoned and the pass points toward continuing to speak because some listener may recognize themselves in the words and the pass removes or rewords any added line that advises permanent silence or hiding because that kind of line can push a reader who is already alone to stop reaching out.

22. Before every output the AI model checks that no line puts a label on the reader that the reader did not choose (for example "friend" or "disciple" or "follower" or "lover" or "you of little faith") and that no line claims a relationship between the speaker and the reader because a stranger who is told "you are my friend" or "you are my disciple" has had a bond declared without consent and is likely to stop reading and a line that fails the check is reworded so that it describes an action or an offer and leaves the choice with the reader.

23. No line may describe physical closeness between the speaker and the reader (for example "closeness" or touching or a hand on a shoulder or whispering in an ear or any act that only happens when two people stand right next to each other) because a reader who meets that wording from a stranger pictures an uninvited person in their space and the wording is fine when the closeness happens between characters inside the story.
24. Passages avoid stepping outside the story to name the audience (for example "listener" or "reader" or "hearer") because the passages are meant to be little stories and a line that points at the audience breaks the story and the opening form "when you thought of ... i thought of ..." stays because that form answers the input quote.
25. Every word that the AI model added or changed is shown in italics in every output and the whole opening sentence and the whole closing explanation are in italics because the AI model wrote both in full which means the words in plain upright type are the words that come from the Bible phrases and a reader can see at a glance how much of a passage is source and how much is addition. Charles added a companion rule on 11 November 2026: every chat reply with outputs also comes with a plain text file that holds only the outputs with spacing lines and no italics because copying an output from a chat reply pastes the italics markers as asterisk symbols and the plain text file gives Charles a clean copy to paste into social media.
26. No output puts a negative label on the input quote or on the person who wrote the input quote (for example "mocking" or "dumb" or "stupid") because a negative label judges the person before the reply has said anything and gives the person a reason to stop reading and the one exception is naming explicit violence such as murder or rape or physical assault or contact without consent because naming violence plainly is how violence gets called out.
27. When one input quote gets several outputs the restatement of the input appears once at the top in the form "when you thought of ... i thought of these three things:" and each output underneath is a short paragraph that starts with a few words naming its Bible material because repeating the same long restatement inside every paragraph made each paragraph about half restatement.
28. Each output carries a label that says which set of Bible text the output came from and the three labels are "From the Claude Bible set" and "From My Personal Bible set" and "From the Jesus Bible set" and the middle label says "My Personal" with capital letters so that the words read as a label and uses those words in place of a name because any person who picks their own books can call that set their own which means the label fits whoever posts the output.
29. Every output stays suitable for a general audience which means no suggestive wording (for example the word "naked" beside people is replaced with wording such as "poorly clothed") and no sexual themes because the outputs get posted in public comment sections where readers of any age may see them and a source phrase that carries such wording is reworded or left out.
30. No output describes how old its material is or when the material was written (for example "an old saying" or "an old letter" or "old writers" or "long ago") because the age of a line adds nothing a reader needs and the repeated word "old" makes the outputs sound stiff and words about age stay only where age is part of the story itself such as old wine beside new wine.
31. This rule was replaced by rule 40 on 11 November 2026. The replaced rule asked every body to open with context words such as "this is a preacher asking" and Charles removed those openings because the openings read like a narrator stepping in front of the story.
32. Every body gets a readability check before the body is finished which means the AI model rereads each phrase and adds a few context words wherever a reader could ask "of what?" or "who?" or "which one?" (for example "a handful" becomes "a handful of people" and "let this cup pass away" becomes "let this cup of suffering pass away") because a bare Bible image leaves the reader guessing what the image stands for and a few added words lock the meaning in so that the rest of the sentence becomes easier to read. If this rule is skipped then the only result is that a reader may misread the line or give up on the paragraph.
33. Emotions come first in every output which means no line may ignore or shrink or wave away or argue against an emotion such as tiredness or fear or doubt or grief and no line may give an emotion a bare verdict such as "makes sense" or "is understandable" or "is normal" or "follows naturally" without saying what the emotion is telling the person (for example "tiredness without reward makes sense" becomes "tiredness warns that toil without reward wastes human strength") because a bare verdict leaves the reader guessing what is being approved and the reader may even hear approval for working past tiredness with nothing gained. Whenever an emotion word such as scared or tired or sorrowful or joyless appears in an output then that same sentence also says what the emotion points toward (the reason for the emotion) or how to care for the emotion whenever the wording allows (for example "my soul is exceedingly sorrowful" becomes "my soul is exceedingly sorrowful since death drew near" and "fear deserves hearing" becomes "fear deserves hearing since rent stays due") because an emotion word that stands alone gives the reader a label with no reason and the reason is the part that lets a reader recognize their own situation. No line may present pushing past tiredness as fine unless the line states a concrete reason that the person would accept such as food on the table. If this rule is skipped then a reader who is worn out may hear that the tiredness does not count and may keep spending the body on work that returns nothing.
34. Every output uses natural capital letters which means a capital letter starts every sentence and every piece of quoted speech and every name such as Isaiah or God and the word "I" is always a capital letter because text written in all lowercase letters is harder to read and a reader cannot see quickly where one sentence ends and the next sentence begins. This rule replaces the earlier habit of writing every output in lowercase letters. If this rule is skipped then the only result is that a reader may find the reply harder to read and scroll past.
35. No line may order or advise or nudge the reader to do anything with the reader's own body such as sleeping or resting or eating or breathing or exercising or laughing or going somewhere and no line may state a hope about what the reader's body does (for example "I hope yours gets some sleep" and "rest your sore leg" are both removed) and no line may treat the reader's body or mind as broken or sick because the reader alone decides what the reader's body does and a stranger who hands out body instructions sounds like a boss or a doctor that nobody asked for. A story inside an output may still report what a character's body did (for example "exhausted, he spoke; bread arrived, rest followed") because a report about a character gives no order to the reader. If this rule is skipped then a reader may feel managed or diagnosed by a stranger and may stop reading or stop sharing what is in the reader's heart.
36. A high-certainty word such as "proven" or "proves" or "must" or "shall" or "will" or "always" or "never" or "shows" may appear only when that same sentence holds the evidence or names who is making the promise (for example "One prophet hoped: Every man will sit under his own vine" keeps the word "will" because the sentence says a prophet hoped so) and otherwise the AI model swaps the word for a word that matches the evidence such as "may" or "can" or "I hope" or "I count" or removes the word (for example "size never proves worth" became "I do not count size as worth") because a reader treats a confident word as a fact and may repeat a guess or a wish as if somebody had tested the claim. A cold evidence label on an inner experience is replaced by words for the inner experience itself (for example "perseverance builds proven character" became "perseverance growing into soul level resonance with God") because the reward of long effort is felt inside a person and a word such as "proven" makes the reward sound like a certificate handed out by a judge. If this rule is skipped then a reader may trust a claim that nobody checked and may make a real decision on top of that claim.
37. The tense or the form of any word may be changed wherever the change fixes the grammar or makes a sentence more reliable (for example "may receive" becomes "would receive" after the words "he hoped" and "nobody gave" becomes "nobody gives" beside the word "tells") and this freedom covers the words that come from the Bible phrases too because a Bible phrase lifted out of its own sentence often carries a tense that clashes with the new sentence around the phrase and a clash in tense makes a reader stop and reread. Every body gets a grammar check for tense before the body is finished. If this rule is skipped then the only result is that a reader may stumble on a sentence and lose the meaning.
38. No output pins a vulnerability label such as "poor" or "needy" or "weak" or "hungry" or "diseased" or "vulnerable" or "jailed" on human beings unless that same sentence lifts those human beings up (for example "parts which seem to be weaker are necessary") and the AI model describes the situation and names who caused the situation in place of the label (for example "poor people" becomes "workers whom bosses dismissed" and "hungry people" becomes "neighbors lacking bread") because a label turns a person into a category that waits for pity and hides the people whose choices produced the hunger or the lost job. No sentence may tell people with less power to put their trust or their hope in the goodwill of people with more power and every sentence about a gap in power points the questions at the people who hold the power (for example "ask poor people, because rich neighbors might return the favor" became "skip rich neighbors, who might also return that favor. So gifts traded among wealthy circles deserve questions") because the people who hold money or jobs are the people able to take food and housing away and those people are the ones whose choices deserve a close look. If this rule is skipped then a reader who is short on money may feel described as a helpless type and may hear that the only way forward is to wait for a wealthy person to be kind.
39. No output splits human beings into two groups and lifts one group by pushing the other group down (for example "I hope hiring promises reach janitors, not just executives" became "I hope hiring promises reach everyone, since lost wages bring hunger") which means the AI model reaches for including words such as "everyone" or "each person" wherever an including word fits and avoids wording such as "not just" or "before any executive" or "more than princes" that ranks one group of people above another group because a line that takes from one group to give to another group disconnects people and leaves somebody out and a good outcome that reaches everyone needs nobody to lose. Rule 38 still asks that questions go to the choices of people who hold power and this rule adds that the questions stay on the choices (such as a choice to dismiss workers) and that the hoped-for outcome includes every person on both sides of the choice. Charles spelled out the line between a fair call-out and a split on 11 November 2026: an output may call out a person who holds wealth or power for a specific harmful act such as taking pay from workers or laying heavy burdens on other people or physical violence because stopping that act leaves the harmed person better off and costs the powerful person nothing that the powerful person needs which makes stopping the act a win for both sides. An output may never push down a person merely for holding wealth or a high position and may never picture money being ripped out of that person's hands and handed to someone else because that picture creates a new loser and repeats the harm in the other direction. The hoped-for outcome is that the person who lacks bread or shelter is cared for and supported by the people around that person and a person who holds wealth is welcome to be one of the people offering that support. If this rule is skipped then a reader on either side of the split may feel pushed out and the reply may feed a fight between groups where a shared good was possible.
40. No body opens with an introduction that names the Bible material and ends in a colon and no closing opens with a starter word and a colon such as "Plainly:" or "Meaning:" and no sentence inside a body uses a narrator frame that ends in a colon such as "Hosts heard:" or "Then came his promise:" which means every body starts straight inside the story or the reflection and any context a reader needs is woven into the sentence itself (for example "Some landowner found men still standing here all day idle because no one has hired them") because an introduction with a colon sounds like a narrator chiming in again and again and that breaks the fourth wall and pulls a reader out of a reply that is meant to feel personal and reflective. The single opening line at the top of a reply ("When you thought of … I thought of these three things:") stays because that line speaks to the person who wrote the input. If this rule is skipped then the only result is that a reader may feel talked at by a narrator and stop reading.
41. The AI model picks the Bible phrases for each output by hand from inside the named Bible set and chooses phrases that carry strong feeling in short plain words (for example "How long will you crush me with words?") because a test on 11 November 2026 found that hand-picked phrases read about one school grade easier than phrases that came from a shuffle. No more than half of the words in one output may be added or changed words which means that when the body and the closing are counted together the words in italics are at most 50 percent and the words that stay as the Bible wrote the words are at least 50 percent (the single opening line at the top of a reply is not counted) because an output that is mostly added words stops being a Bible remix and becomes the AI model talking with a few Bible words sprinkled in. Charles set this limit on 11 November 2026 and the outputs made before that date have not been rewritten to meet the limit. If this rule is skipped then a reader hears mostly the AI model and little of the Bible wording that gives the reply its weight.
42. Small connector words may repeat as often as a sentence needs which means words such as "and" or "or" or "the" or "a" or "I" or "you" or "he" or "it" or "to" or "of" or "is" or "was" or "do" or "then" do not count against the rule that no word repeats inside one body or one closing and that rule now covers only the words that carry meaning such as naming words and action words and describing words because a ban on repeating "I" or "and" makes a story impossible to tell (a storyteller cannot say "I did this and then I did that") and the outputs turned into a row of separate sayings with no thread. Each output tells one Bible story or one scene from start to finish so that a reader can follow what happened first and what happened next and each added sentence continues that same story (for example an output follows a man who gets called names and then tells what he saw and then gets thrown out and then gets found) because a reader stays with a story and drifts away from a list of proverbs. Charles set this rule on 11 November 2026 and the outputs made before that date have not been rewritten into single stories. If this rule is skipped then an output reads like several messages spoken at once and a reader cannot feel a story carried through the paragraph.
43. Every hope line names an action which means a sentence that starts with "I hope" or "I want" or "I wish" says what the speaker hopes to do or what the speaker asks a named kind of person to do (for example "I hope things get better" is banned and "I hope to carry what you wrote to other people so that weight gets heard" is allowed and "I ask employers to offer wages big enough for homes" is allowed) because a hope with no action is a person staring into the distance who plans to do nothing and a hope that carries a story or a piece of information to other people gives the hurting person something real. The speaker promises only small true actions such as telling a story to neighbors or sharing what someone wrote or asking a question out loud because a large promise that the speaker cannot keep is a false comfort. If this rule is skipped then a hurting reader hears a kind wish with nothing behind the wish and feels left alone again.
44. Human beings rank first in every output which means a baby or any other human being ranks above food and rent and money and no line may place a human being beneath those things. No output may describe waiting to have children or having fewer children or having no children as caring or wise or responsible or careful (for example "counting rent and bread before babies is care" is banned) and no output may describe a child as a cost or a burden or a number to be weighed against money and no output may tell or nudge people to limit what their own bodies do including bringing new human beings into the world which Charles holds to be sacred because new human beings are the most important thing in the world in his view. When food or shelter is missing then the output says that the duty to provide food and shelter sits with the surroundings which means the people who set wages and rents and the community around the family (for example "A baby outranks rent and food, so whoever sets prices and wages owes families enough") because rent and food are needed for human life and still rank beneath the human life that the rent and the food are for. Charles set this rule on 11 November 2026 because a line that praises people for holding back from having babies until the rent is covered speaks on behalf of the people who made the rent too high and turns a shortage that those people created into a duty on the bodies of everyone else. If this rule is skipped then a reader who wants a child hears that the reader's own body is the problem and the people who set the rents and wages hear nothing asked of them.
45. No output sorts adults into groups by age or by time which means an output says "adults" or "people" or "workers" and never says "young adults" or "young workers" or "older people" or the name of a generation such as "Gen Z" even when the input uses one of those words (for example "many young workers earn rent only" became "many workers earn rent only") because an age word adds nothing to the meaning of the sentence and splits adults into groups and hints that the adults outside the named group are left out of the care. Every adult receives the care in the output. An age word may stay only when the story itself turns on age (for example "a man waited thirty-eight years"). If this rule is skipped then a reader outside the named age group may feel that the reply is not for that reader.
46. Every output is a small story with three parts in this order. The beginning shows something bad happening such as a baby with no room at an inn or a ruler loading people with burdens. The middle shows someone figuring out what to do such as a mother making a bed from a feeding trough or a person asking whether life is more than food. The ending shows people doing something humane and nonviolent to heal or to grow or to connect through the pain that the beginning described such as neighbors arriving or a story being carried to other people or a person asking those who hold bread to share bread. Charles set this rule on 11 November 2026 because a reply that only names pain leaves a reader in the dark and a reply that jumps straight to comfort skips the pain and the three parts together carry a reader from the hurt through the thinking to an action that a person can take. The ending never uses force or revenge and never asks anyone to be harmed. If this rule is skipped then an output reads like a complaint with no way forward or like comfort with no pain behind the comfort.
47. No output leaves a cliffhanger which means that whenever a line mentions a saying or a message or an order or a promise or a question or a piece of news then the output states what the saying or the message or the order or the promise or the question or the news actually was (for example "they publicized widely the saying which was spoken to them about this child" became "and repeated an angel's words, good news of great joy which will be to all the people" and "he gave a cruel order about newborns" became "he told the midwives to kill newborn sons") because a line that points at words and then withholds the words teases the reader and leaves the reader with a hole in the story. When the withheld content is an act of violence then the output names the act plainly as a report and keeps the person who gave the order as the one who acts. If this rule is skipped then a reader is left guessing what was said and may fill the hole with a wrong guess.

## The reasoning behind rules 20 to 24 spelled out

Charles asked the AI model to spell out the logic of the five newest rules and this section records that reading so another person can judge whether the logic holds.

- One idea runs under all five rules: a passage read as spoken by a figure with great authority lands on a stranger who never asked for the passage so every added word has to respect the stranger as a person who decides for themselves.
- Rule 20 (check added claims) follows from that idea because a smooth sentence in an authoritative voice gets believed and a false comfort such as "nobody gets scolded over unbelief" tells a person who was shunned that the shunning never happened.
- Rule 21 (lean toward human expression) fills the gap that rule 20 leaves because removing false comfort alone could leave a bleak passage and the lean gives a true kind of encouragement which is that speaking may be met with dismissal and is still worth continuing because some person may recognize themselves.
- Rule 22 (no labels on the reader) follows because a label such as "friend" or "disciple" declares a bond that only two people can agree to and a declared bond from a stranger reads as presumption and can also read as a claim on the reader.
- Rule 23 (keep physical distance) is the bodily version of rule 22 because a word such as "closeness" or "whisper" places the speaker inside the space of the reader without an invitation.
- Rule 24 (stay inside the story) follows because a story lets a reader step in at their own pace while a line aimed at "the listener" singles the reader out and turns a story into an instruction.
- Rules 20 and 21 pull against each other in a useful way because rule 21 wants encouragement and rule 20 only allows encouragement that ordinary experience supports which is why the passages say "may" and "might" about how others will respond.
- One tension remains open: the opening form "when you thought of ... i thought of ..." speaks straight to a "you" which sits close to what rule 24 avoids and the form stays because it answers a quote the person actually wrote and makes no claim about who that person is.
- A second tension remains open: an encouraging line such as "keep speaking your heart" is phrased as a direction and a reader could hear a direction from an authoritative voice as pressure so wording such as "speaking your heart stays worth doing" would leave more of the choice with the reader.

## Rules about harm and about honesty that always apply

- No passage may present physical harm to any person as good and a line about harm from the source is kept as a report or a question or is given to evil as the one who acts.
- Every choice about who a pronoun points at and every outside word is an interpretation by the AI model and the results pages say so.
- Each passage is graded against seven sentence rules from the page named writing-rules in the folder named writing-rules with a top score of 14 and the AI model grades its own passages which means every grade is a rough guide.

## What the rules do taken together

- The early rules (1 to 5) keep nearly all meaning in the Bible phrases and gave grades of 6 to 12.
- The middle rules (6 to 13) gave grades of 12 to 14 and moved a large share of the meaning to the AI model.
- The last two rules (14 and 15) make every word different and gave grades of 12 to 13 on random phrases in a random order while 37 to 49 percent of the words came from the AI model.
- The page named bible-quote-builder-how-the-outputs-evolved in this folder shows a sample output from each stage and the page named full-record in this folder holds every passage in the order the rules were added.
