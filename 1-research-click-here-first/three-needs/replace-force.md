# Nonviolent replacements for six uses of force

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this page on 14 November 2026 from searches of public sources. No other person has rechecked the page which means every claim here is unconfirmed. The goal of the page is to find ways of providing food and shelter and deep meaningful emotionally and spiritually resonant human connection to all human beings without violence or coercion.

## The common shape of the violence

People in society often answer an unmet need with force because force is the tool already in place. A person with no housing gets arrested or moved along and a person behind on rent gets removed by armed deputies and a person in a mental crisis gets locked in a ward. Each replacement below answers the need itself.

## Six replacements with evidence

### 1. Armed police at nonviolent crisis calls → unarmed health responders

Denver neighborhoods served by a team of one mental health clinician and one paramedic saw reports of lower-level crimes fall about 34 percent which was nearly 1400 fewer offenses in six months. Those teams never had to call for police support on the calls they answered first. The limit is one city and one pilot. Sources: [Denver crisis team study report](https://www.motherjones.com/criminal-justice/2022/06/denver-deployed-mental-health-workers-instead-of-police-and-some-crimes-went-down/) and [Denver crisis team details](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/sending-health-care-workers-instead-of-cops-can-reduce-crime/?amp=true)

### 2. Arresting people who sleep outside → housing with no preconditions

The approach named Housing First gives a person a home without first requiring sobriety or treatment. Randomized trials of Housing First show 73 to 80 percent of participants staying housed while usual care kept roughly 30 percent housed. Substance use and psychiatric symptoms changed about the same under either approach so housing answers the shelter need and other needs still require care. Sources: [Housing First retention review](https://www.homelesshub.ca/node/140290) and [Housing First research handout](https://endhomelessness.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Housing-First-Research-NAEH-NLIHC-Handout.pdf)

### 3. Eviction by armed deputies → required mediation plus rent help

Philadelphia requires a landlord to try a guided talk with the tenant or to seek rent help before filing an eviction. Philadelphia landlords filed fewer than 14000 evictions in 2024 after filing more than 22000 a year before the pandemic and local leaders credit the program. Cases with rental assistance led to a later eviction filing 7.4 percent of the time versus 42.2 percent without assistance so the money matters as much as the talking. Sources: [Philadelphia eviction diversion](https://evictionlab.org/how-local-leaders-are-stemming-the-eviction-crisis/) and [rental assistance and mediation outcomes](https://blog.aboutrsi.org/category/evaluation/)

### 4. Cages after harm → a small face-to-face meeting to repair the harm

The usual name for the meeting is a restorative justice conference and the meeting is a small private gathering and not a large event. The person who was harmed and the person who caused the harm sit down together with a few supporters of each such as relatives or close friends and one trained helper who guides the talk. The person who caused the harm describes what happened and the person who was harmed says how the harm affected daily life and the group agrees on how to repair the harm such as paying for what was broken or answering the questions the harmed person still has. Nobody is forced to attend because the meeting happens only when both main people say yes.

Pooled randomized trials show a modest drop in repeat offending among consenting people assigned to such a meeting and the harmed people's satisfaction with how their cases were handled rose in every trial. The limit is that both people must consent. Sources: [restorative conference trials](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/S10940-014-9222-9) and [restorative conference review summary](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:camsys:v:9:y:2013:i:1:p:1-59)

### 5. Locked psychiatric holds → voluntary peer respite homes

A peer respite is an unlocked home where a person in a mental crisis can stay for a short time with support from trained staff who have lived through crises of their own. One randomized trial sent people facing commitment to either an unlocked peer-run home or a locked facility and the peer-run group reported a greater drop in symptoms and higher satisfaction. Guests in one interview study described a greater sense of belonging and safety which touches the need for deep connection. Research on peer respites is still sparse. Source: [peer respite reporting](https://mindsitenews.org/2021/09/29/a-short-term-home-where-people-in-crisis-can-avoid-the-hospital/)

### 6. Withholding food unless a person works → food with no work condition

A 2026 review of the research concluded that work requirements do not raise employment and do cause a large fall in food assistance enrollment which means the condition mostly produces hunger. Source: [work requirements review coverage](https://sourcenm.com/?p=28689)

## What stays disputed or thin

- One 2019 study did link work requirements to a 1.4 point rise in employment though a federal evidence review gave that study a low rating and supporters of the requirements argue from the dignity of work and control of spending. Source: [2019 work requirements study rating](https://clear.dol.gov/Study/Do-SNAP-work-requirements-work-Harris-2019)
- Earlier pooled studies of restorative justice disagreed about whether repeat offending falls. Source: [restorative justice evidence brief](https://www.justice.govt.nz/assets/Restorative-Justice.pdf)
- Most of the evidence above covers nonviolent situations. The chatbot did not find a tested nonviolent replacement for stopping a person in the middle of serious physical violence and that gap is where critics of these replacements focus.

## Which of the three needs each replacement serves

Housing with no preconditions and eviction mediation serve shelter. Food without conditions serves food. The small repair meeting and peer respite homes and crisis teams serve human connection because each one replaces force with people listening to each other.
