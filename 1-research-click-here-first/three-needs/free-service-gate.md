# An AI model that can switch on free food or shelter for a person: what the design would need

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this outline on 15 November 2026. No other person has rechecked the outline and no such system exists as far as the chatbot knows. The owner of this repository asked what systems and what changes to AI models would be needed so that a person could give an AI model enough proof of need and the AI model could then switch on a government service or another service for free.

The design is built around one rule because the rule shapes everything else: the AI model may say "yes" or "a human will look at this within the hour" and the AI model may never issue a final "no" for food or shelter.

## 1. Money and legal authority

- Lawmakers or agency heads or donors fill a fund and sign a written grant of authority so the AI model can approve spending without a caseworker.
- The grant sets caps such as one hot meal per person per mealtime and one motel night per approval because caps limit the cost of any single mistake.
- Agency officials adopt a "yes now and paperwork later" rule because a person who is hungry tonight cannot wait three weeks for a file review.

## 2. Connections to real services

- The AI model needs live ordering access to restaurants and grocers and motels and bus passes because approval means nothing until a plate or a key reaches the person.
- The AI model needs filing access to benefit applications so one conversation can also start longer-term food and housing help.

## 3. Proof that scales with cost

- A meal needs no proof beyond the person's own words because one wrongly given meal costs a few dollars and one wrongly refused meal leaves a person hungry.
- A motel night could need light proof such as a phone location near a parked car or a photo of the car's back seat with blankets.
- A month of rent could need stronger proof such as a photo of an eviction notice and a phone call the AI model places to the landlord to confirm the amount.
- No step demands a government ID for food because many people sleeping outside have lost their documents.

## 4. Upgrades the AI model itself needs

- Steady judgment so the same story gets the same answer whether the person writes politely or in a panic.
- Resistance to trick instructions hidden in photos or messages because a fund with a spending button attracts people testing for loopholes.
- The ability to state each decision in plain words such as "approved because you said you have not eaten since yesterday."
- A habit of leaning toward yes on survival items whenever the evidence is unclear.

## 5. Guarding the fund without treating people as suspects

- Limits per person and per phone stop one person from draining the fund.
- Human reviewers read a random sample of approvals afterward and adjust the rules without taking back anyone's dinner.

## 6. Privacy

- The AI model deletes photos and location after deciding and shares nothing with police or other officials unless the person asks because fear of exposure keeps hungry people from asking.

## 7. Keeping the powerful accountable

- The rulebook and monthly totals of approvals sit on a public page because officials could quietly tune a hidden gate toward refusal to save money.
- The page names the officials who set the caps so a refused person knows which human made the choice.
