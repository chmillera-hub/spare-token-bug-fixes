# Lessons from editing two reflections line by line

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this page on 14 November 2026. The owner of this repository asked for dozens of small changes to two reflections and this page gathers the patterns behind those changes so that a person or an AI agent can use the patterns when editing reflective writing for public posting. The lessons are the chatbot's reading of the changes and no other person has rechecked the lessons.

The two reflections are in the deep-dives folder inside the social-media-vibes folder. The page named [never-asked-born.md](../social-media-vibes/deep-dives/never-asked-born.md) imagines a prayer built on the words "I never asked to be born". The page named [wish-one-enemy.md](../social-media-vibes/deep-dives/wish-one-enemy.md) reflects on blaming one enemy for a hurt.

Each lesson below gives the reason for the lesson and one example of wording before the change and after the change.

## 1. Swap out a word that has a second meaning

A reader can follow the second meaning of a word and leave the message because the second meaning is often quicker to grab than the idea. Several such words in one post can add up until the replies are about the second meanings and not about the post.

- Before: "the hard part of the same freedom". After: "the other side of freedom".
- Before: "no punishment would come from me". After: "I would not punish you for leaving".
- Before: "the two are tied together". After: "the two are connected through an ecosystem of care" because "tied" can call up a picture of a person bound with rope.
- Before: "a web of causes". After: "an ecosystem of causes" because "web" can call up spiders and spiders can call up fear or disgust.
- Before: "the causes are many and so many places exist". After: "the causes are many and therefore many places exist" because "so many" can be read as one phrase which breaks the grammar.

## 2. Read an off-topic joke reply as information

A joke reply that follows a second meaning of a word shows which word pulled a reader's attention away from the message. The writer does not need to decide whether the person joking is kind or cruel because the adjustment is the same in both cases which is to swap the word in the next post for a word with one clear meaning.

## 3. Keep helpful words free of links to harm

A word that names something useful to a person can lose the useful meaning when a sentence attaches the word to harm.

- Before: "one enemy gives anger a single place to land". After: "one enemy is a single place to focus energy on" because anger can guide a person toward a needed change and the sentence had attached anger to harming an enemy.
- Before: "a harmless action". After: "a useful action that avoids harm" because "harmless" can sound like weakness or like a reason to stay silent.

## 4. Let the narrator say only what can be seen or heard

A narrator cannot see inside another mind and a reader who copies the narrator's habit could start stating guesses about real people as facts.

- Before: "the gardener's hand reached for a stone before the gardener had finished thinking". After: "the gardener's hand reached for a stone" because reaching for a stone does not reveal a thought.
- Before: "the answer that people never got to hear". After: "the answer that people may have never gotten to hear" because the speaker does not know what answer each person received.

## 5. Show one moment causing the next

A scene reads as abrupt when a character acts with no visible link to what was just said. Small linking words show one person's words landing on another person.

- Before: "The other neighbor said that leaves had blocked the ditch and the gardener's hand reached for a rake". After: "The other neighbor said 'Wait, before you grab that stone I believe that fallen leaves have been blocking the ditch' and then hearing this the gardener's hand reached for a rake instead".

## 6. Replace a label with the thing the label points at

A label such as "the sentence" or "the story" makes a reader look elsewhere for the meaning. A reader who sees one line alone should be able to picture what the line describes.

- Before: "understanding the sentence could help". After: "understanding what is behind 'I never asked to be born' could help".
- Before: a paragraph that repeated the word "explanation" many times to define the word. After: a short parable about a gardener and two neighbors that shows an answer working like a quiet order.

## 7. Check the scene against how the real world works

A detail that could not happen pulls a reader out of the message.

- Before: "A gardener woke to find the garden withered". After: "A gardener watched the garden wither over several dry weeks" because plants take days or weeks to dry out.

## 8. Turn helplessness into what a person can do

A sentence that ends in "nothing can be done" allows harm to keep circulating because every person who gives up is one fewer person speaking up.

- Before: "the hurt grows out of a web of causes so wide that nobody controls the web". After: "the causes are many and therefore many places exist where I can help".
- Added line: "Any contribution is greater than zero".

## 9. Do not explain domination or violence with a sympathetic motive

A sentence such as "the person only wanted to matter" or "the person only wanted to feel safe" can work as approval of the violence and the approval can circulate and return to the writer. The safer wording says what a person can do in place of the violence.

- Before: "the wish for one enemy is partly a wish to matter because defeating an enemy would prove that a person's life changed something". After: "No reason makes domination or violence acceptable because the harm lands on a living person" followed by a sentence about putting the same energy into empowering the people nearby toward pro-human nonviolent behavior.

## 10. Put the harm where the harmful choice happens

A reader can blame an innocent starting point when a sentence is loose about where harm begins.

- Example: being born is named as not violence and the treatment a person receives from other people after birth is named as the thing to call out and change.

## 11. Hold nonviolence and speaking up together

Avoiding harm does not mean staying silent. Saying "I am lonely" out loud is a request for support and is not a call toward harm against anyone. A listener who says "I am uncomfortable" is also expressing that listener's own heart and so the speaker can respect the listener's boundary and seek support elsewhere.

## 12. Ask every answer to carry a useful action

A kind sentence with no cause and no action leaves the person where the person started.

- Example added to the parable: an answer such as "that sucks that your garden is withering and anyway have a nice day" hands the listener nothing because the answer names no cause and no action.

## 13. Speak about the person who is making the choice

- Before: "the person who made the choice". After: "the person who is making the choice" because the present tense shows that a person can give input before a choice is final or acted upon.

## 14. Use words that grant what is already present

- Before: "wants to build trust". After: "wants to build even more trust" because "even more" tells the reader that trust is already there and growing.
- Before: "share pro-human ways of living". After: "share more pro-human ways of living" because "more" grants that the listener already carries some.

## 15. Match the certainty of a word to what is known

- Before: "changes what the next person is handed". After: "changes what the next person might engage in".
- Before: "raises the number of people who hold permission to care". After: "raises the number of people who might have the space to care or the bandwidth to care".

## 16. Widen the wording so more readers can stay

- Before: "the Father who represents". After: "the Father which represents a universal pro-human nonviolent framework" because the word "which" lets a reader who does not believe in a deity read the Father as a framework and stay with the message.
