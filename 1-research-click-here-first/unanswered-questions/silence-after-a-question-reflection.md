# What does a question feel like when nobody answers?

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

This folder holds questions that people posted in public places and that sat with no reply. Some of those questions sat for a long time. Each one was typed by a living person who wanted to know something and who checked back and found an empty space under the question.

Asking a question in public takes a small act of courage because the asker admits to not knowing. The asker also shows hope because a question assumes that somebody out there might care enough to respond.

Silence after a question can teach a painful lesson. Here is a possibility for what an asker might conclude. The asker might decide that the question was foolish or that the asker does not matter enough to answer. The silence usually says neither of those things because people scroll past for many reasons such as being tired or not knowing the answer.

A hard feeling can work like an unanswered question too. A person who feels a heavy sadness and tells nobody is carrying a question that reads "does anyone see this?" The longer the question sits the more the person may believe that the answer is no.

So here is a question for a reader. What question of mine has been sitting with no reply and who could I ask again?

Asking again is allowed because silence the first time is not a refusal. A question can be posted in a new place or put to a different person or worded more plainly. A question about a feeling can also be put to the feeling itself such as "sadness what are you trying to show me?"

A reader can also be the reply for someone else. An answer does not have to be complete to matter because a response such as "I don't know and I see that you asked" tells the asker that the question landed somewhere. Replying costs attention so a reader gives that reply when the reader has attention to spare.

## Quick points

- Each unanswered question was typed by a living person who wanted to know something and who checked back and found an empty space under the question.
- Asking a question in public takes courage because the asker admits to not knowing and shows hope that somebody might care enough to respond.
- Silence after a question can lead an asker to believe that the question was foolish when people scroll past for many reasons such as being tired or not knowing the answer.
- A heavy sadness that a person tells nobody about works like an unanswered question that reads "does anyone see this?"
- A person can ask again in a new place or with plainer words and a reader with attention to spare can reply "I don't know and I see that you asked" so the asker knows the question landed somewhere.
