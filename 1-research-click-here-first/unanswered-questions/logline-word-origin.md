# Where the screenwriting word "logline" comes from

## The question

On 29 February 2024 somebody asked on the Wiktionary word-origin board (the Etymology scriptorium) whether the film-script meaning of "logline" (a very short summary of a script) shares the origin of the sailing meaning (the rope tied to a wooden float that sailors used to measure the speed of a ship).
Nobody replied on the board.
The Wiktionary entry read on 12 November 2026 still gives one shared origin for both meanings with a first use in 1613. Source: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/logline

## What the records show

1. The Merriam-Webster dictionary gives the script meaning a separate origin: the word "log" plus the word "line" and originally the line in a television program log that summarizes the plot of a show. The same dictionary dates the first known use of the script meaning to 1982. Source: https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/logline

2. The listings section of the magazine TV Guide was itself called "the log" which was a text list of programs ordered by start time and channel with a short summary beside most programs. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Guide_(magazine)

3. Many screenwriting websites say the word comes from log books that old Hollywood studio heads kept for the scripts the studio owned. None of the websites read for this page shows a document from the studio years that uses the word and the dictionary date of 1982 falls decades after the studio years.

## Best-guess answer

The script meaning does not come from the sailing rope because the script meaning names a line of text in a program log.
The two meanings are still distant relatives because the word "log" for a written record comes from the log book of a ship and the log book of a ship was named after the wooden float on the sailing rope.
A fitting correction for the Wiktionary entry could be two separate origin sections: the sailing meaning from 1613 and the script meaning from television program logs with a first known use in 1982.

## What is still open

No use of the script meaning earlier than 1982 was searched for inside scanned television trade magazines because the full-text search of those scans was out of reach.
The old Hollywood log book story could still be true if somebody finds a studio document that uses the word before television existed.
