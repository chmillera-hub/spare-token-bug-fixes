The farmer clapped.

The farmer clapped again.
That sheep ran faster.
The farmer smiled.

"What happens if I don’t run?"

And the farmer frowned.

"Come on," the farmer said. "You’ll feel better if you run."

That sheep shook its head. "I don’t feel bad."

The farmer's smile faltered. "You’ll fall behind the others."

That sheep looked at the circle, the endless, pointless loop.
Then it looked at the fence.

"What’s beyond it?"

The other sheep overheard, slowing their pace.

The farmer's voice was gentle but firm.
"Don’t think about that. The fence is there to keep you safe."

"But have you ever been past it?" that sheep asked.

The farmer hesitated. "...That’s not the point."

And for the first time, that sheep realized:
The farmer didn’t know what was beyond the fence either.

---

That day, something changed.
The other sheep started noticing the fence too.
And little by little, they stopped running.

The farmer clapped harder.
The farmer yelled.
The farmer said, "This is nonsense! Running is good for you! Why are you wasting your time questioning things?!"

But the sheep didn’t listen.

Because they had seen the fence.
And now, they couldn’t unsee it.

---

And that’s how the cycle broke.

Not with a riot.
Not with an escape.
Not with a revolution.

But with one sheep deciding to stop running.

And the others realizing they could too.
