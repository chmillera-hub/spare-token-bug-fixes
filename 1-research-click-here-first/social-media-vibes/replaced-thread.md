## The post

Follow-up. Seven months back I wrote about a buddy employed at an AI lab who was still at the office after midnight on a weekend. He and I spoke again and he says the whole staff has made peace with being swapped out.

Humans meeting AI today resemble the Aztecs on the day the Spanish force arrived. People believed their own civilization was far along and then a power beyond anything within reach stepped into the yard. The invaders entered the capital in late 1519 with a few hundred soldiers plus thousands of native allies and under two years later the capital was lost. (The tale that the Aztecs mistook the Spanish for gods was probably added afterward according to most historians. I keep the comparison for the feeling which is dread with a layer of wonder underneath at a thing nobody can size themselves against.)

Seven months back I described a video call with that buddy who works at one of the leading AI firms. The hour was past midnight on a weekend for him and the office behind him looked like an ordinary afternoon. Many readers had opinions. He and I finally spoke again for about ninety minutes and I asked how the place feels now.

He said the worry has vanished. Everybody there treats replacement as a closed question: researchers and physicians and welders and eventually every occupation and no action will undo the change. His words were close to these: "Nobody in this valley asks what happens if I get swapped out. Everybody gets swapped out. And since everybody does there is no cause for sorrow."

He likened the mood to the film about a comet that people refuse to look at. First everybody panics and then everybody decides to use the remaining time. Some coworkers are piling up as much pay as possible before the layoffs. The more active ones spend their chatbot allowance on personal projects so they own something when the day arrives. He is no bystander because he runs twelve to fifteen AI agents at once with five of the agents directing the rest. He told me that when he tries to relax with a book his attention slips after about three lines.

Then he said something close to this: "Nobody discusses how to live in a place drenched in technology without getting hurt and how to care for people and bond with people and feel alive. Not one scrap of talk or thought goes there."

I agree with the first half. Replacement is on the way and I doubt anybody gets a vote. The comet is already falling. Where I part ways is what follows. Enjoying the time before impact is one answer. Trying to keep pace by making humans more machine-like is another answer and I doubt that works either. I think humans and AI run side by side: humans stay human and AI stays AI and each has its own work in the world. I wish to spend my time on the part he says nobody discusses which is how people stay whole while living right beside something so powerful.

I have no tidy answer. What I do have is many people around me who quietly made peace with being replaced and very few who said aloud what they plan to hold onto. So I am asking: if you already accept that AI will do your job one day then what have you decided to keep doing yourself and why?
