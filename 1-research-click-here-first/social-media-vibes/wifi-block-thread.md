# What 364 comments asked a homeowner to do about a houseguest and an AI chatbot

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this page from a screen recording of one person's thread on a social media site on 12 November 2026. The comments are anonymized.

Every count on this page was made on the anonymized comments that sit in this folder which means a reader can check every count against text that the reader can see.

- [comments-reworded.txt](comments-reworded.txt) holds every comment in anonymized form.
- [comments-with-labels.txt](comments-with-labels.txt) holds the same anonymized comments with the behavior labels that were counted under each comment.
- A live page shows the same count as a tappable bar graph: https://chmillera-hub.github.io/spare-token-bug-fixes/1-research-click-here-first/social-media-vibes/bar-graph.html

## The situation in the thread

A homeowner asked whether she would be in the wrong for blocking an AI chatbot on her home wifi. A long-time friend is staying with her rent-free during a serious mental health crisis and the friend talks to the chatbot all day as a best friend and therapist. The friend had already told the homeowner that the homeowner talks down to her as if the friend could not make her own decisions.

## What was counted

The recording captured 364 comments from other people plus 27 replies from the homeowner. The thread showed about 2100 comments in total which means the counts describe the top of the thread and not the whole thread.

The counts are counts of comments and not counts of people because the comments are anonymized and so this page does not track which comments came from the same person. One comment can count on several lines because many comments ask for more than one behavior.

## The count of requested behaviors

- 89 comments asked the homeowner to block the chatbot on the home wifi.
- 78 comments asked the homeowner to leave the friend's chatbot access alone.
- 56 comments asked the homeowner to get the friend professional mental health care such as a therapist or a psychiatrist or a doctor.
- 52 comments asked the homeowner to be careful or prepared because a sudden cutoff could set off a breakdown or violence.
- 41 comments asked the homeowner to talk openly with the friend and explain or give warning before any block.
- 40 comments told the homeowner that blocking a website for an adult is the same behavior as a parent putting controls on a household computer.
- 35 comments asked the homeowner to have the friend leave the home (telling her to go or asking her to move out or setting a move-out date).
- 14 comments asked for a house rule (treatment or no chatbot) with the friend free to follow the rule or leave.
- 14 comments asked for other supports to be built first such as human friends or groups or hobbies or public benefits or social workers.
- 13 comments asked for a gradual reduction such as slowing the connection or limiting hours.
- 13 comments asked for AI to be blocked or restricted more widely (in the writers' own homes or by law).
- 11 comments asked the homeowner to get guidance from a professional before changing anything.
- 10 comments asked the homeowner to look after herself (her own support or her own burnout or her own motives).
- 9 comments said the friend should pay her own way or get her own phone data plan.
- 8 comments asked for inpatient care or a psychiatric facility or a psychiatric hold.
- 8 comments asked the homeowner to bring in other people such as the friend's doctor or family.
- 7 comments asked the homeowner to tell the friend to stop quoting chatbot conversations at her.
- 6 comments suggested a secret block or a cover story and 3 comments objected to lying about a block.
- 5 comments suggested sitting with the friend inside her chatbot use or using AI as part of the answer.
- 4 comments asked the homeowner to check tenant and eviction law.
- 4 comments asked the homeowner to read about AI and loss of contact with reality.
- 3 comments asked the homeowner to keep housing the friend.

40 comments also stated that the chatbot use is harming the friend and that statement is a description and not a behavior so the statement sits apart from the list.

## The count of judgments

The site section where the thread sits asks commenters to judge the poster.

- 67 comments said the homeowner would be in the wrong (including soft and qualified versions).
- 55 comments said the homeowner would be in the clear.
- 1 comment said the homeowner would be in the wrong for not blocking and 1 said nobody is in the wrong and 1 said everybody is in the wrong.

## Findings

1. The comments split almost evenly on the block itself because 89 asked for the block and 78 asked for no block.
2. The judgments and the behavior requests do not line up cleanly because 13 comments that said the homeowner would be in the wrong still told her to block the chatbot anyway.
3. The widest agreement across both camps is professional care (56) and a warning about a sudden cutoff (52) since comments that wanted the block and comments that opposed the block both made those two requests.
4. Removal of the friend from the home is a far more common request than continued housing because 35 comments asked for the friend to leave and 3 asked the homeowner to keep housing her.
5. Open conversation outnumbers deception because 41 comments asked for an explanation or a warning first while 6 suggested a secret block or a cover story.
6. Requests that add something to the friend's life are much rarer than requests that take something away or hand the friend to someone else because 14 asked for other supports and 5 suggested sitting with the friend inside her chatbot use while 89 asked for the block and 35 asked for the friend to leave and 8 asked for inpatient care.
7. Exactly 1 comment out of 364 offered personal contact (an invitation to send a private message to talk) which means nearly every comment recommended a behavior for other people to carry out.
8. The homeowner wrote in her replies that she will not block the chatbot after reading the comments and that what she wants most is her best friend back.

## Limits of the count

- An AI chatbot wrote the wording of the anonymized comments and that wording can drift from what the original writer meant. The counts describe the anonymized comments because the anonymized comments are the evidence that the public can see.
- An earlier version of this page counted separate commenters from the original comments and showed slightly different numbers (for example 90 where this page shows 89). The numbers changed because this version counts comments and counts only what the anonymized wording supports.
- The recording scrolled quickly so some comments were cut off and reply chains that stayed collapsed are missing.
- The behavior labels are one AI chatbot's reading of each comment so a second reader could label some comments differently.
- No other person has rechecked the counts which means every count here is unconfirmed.
