# The repository owner's own comments compared with two other comment threads

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this page on 12 November 2026.

## What was looked at

The owner of this repository wrote 9134 comments on a large discussion site before his account was removed. The comments hold about 3.04 million words. 6437 of the comments are different from each other because the owner often posted the same long comment in more than one place.

Two kinds of counting were done.

- A program measured every one of the 9134 comments (length and questions and matches to a list of generic phrases).
- A program picked 60 comments at random and an AI chatbot read an excerpt of each one and labeled the kind of energy in the comment. The 60 excerpts with their labels sit in [owner-comments-sample.txt](owner-comments-sample.txt).
- A live page shows the sample count as a tappable bar graph: https://chmillera-hub.github.io/spare-token-bug-fixes/1-research-click-here-first/social-media-vibes/owner-comments-bar-graph.html

## The count for the 60 sampled comments

One comment can count on several lines.

- 25 comments explain or analyze a situation and ask for nothing.
- 15 comments ask the other person a direct question or invite more conversation.
- 10 comments ask for care to be added such as food and shelter for every person or emotional education or listening to an emotion.
- 8 comments share the owner's own experience or practice or a story.
- 4 comments give a practical instruction or tip.
- 4 comments are a short reaction that asks for nothing.
- 1 comment asks for a person to be kept apart from other people.
- 0 comments refer someone to a paid professional.

8 of the 60 comments open with a line of scripture. About 10 of the 60 are replies that the owner pasted from his conversations with AI chatbots.

## The comparison

The two other threads are the wifi thread (364 comments about blocking an AI chatbot on home wifi) and the executive thread (171 comments about a famous executive who lost his job). Both threads sit in this folder in anonymized form.

Each line gives the owner's comments first and then the wifi thread and then the executive thread.

- Short reaction that asks for nothing: 7% and 7% and 44%.
- Explains or comments and asks for nothing: 42% and 18% and 25%.
- Asks for control or removal or punishment of a person: 2% and 37% and 2%.
- Refers someone to a paid professional: 0% and 17% and 0%.
- Asks for care to be added: 17% and 10% and 8%.
- Contains a question: 56% and 21% and 18%.
- Middle length of a comment: 223 words and 33 words and 14 words.
- Five words or fewer: 1.7% and 2.5% and 10.5%.

The owner's numbers for the first five lines come from the 60 sampled comments. The owner's numbers for the last three lines come from all 9134 comments.

## The generic phrase test

The file [generic-phrases-10000.txt](generic-phrases-10000.txt) holds 10000 interchangeable phrases such as "lol" or "that sucks".

- 54 of the owner's 9134 comments (0.59%) are word for word the same as a phrase on the list. Most of those 54 are the two words "go on" which the owner used 49 times to ask another person to continue.
- 14 more comments (0.15%) are short comments built around a list phrase.
- A public archive of 49525 posts and comments from the same site matched at 0.69% and 1.34% on the same two tests.
- 1.7% of the owner's comments are five words or fewer and 19.9% of the archive rows are five words or fewer.

## Findings

1. The owner's comments are far longer than the comments in either thread because the middle comment runs 223 words against 33 words and 14 words.
2. The owner asks questions much more often because 56% of his comments contain a question against 21% and 18%. In the sample 15 of 60 comments put a question straight to the other person.
3. The owner almost never asks for a person to be controlled or removed (1 of 60) while 37% of the wifi thread comments ask for exactly that.
4. The owner never sends a person to a paid professional in the sample while 17% of the wifi thread comments do.
5. The largest share of the owner's comments (42%) explain or analyze and ask the reader for nothing. So the most common energy is teaching or thinking aloud and not a request.
6. The owner's short comments are rare and the most common one ("go on") is itself a request for the other person to keep talking.
7. The owner's comments are not free of harshness. One sampled comment pairs a tip with a put-down of the person being answered and several sampled comments confront a person with strong language.

## Limits

- The sample is 60 comments out of 9134 so each sample share could be off by ten points or more in either direction.
- The chatbot labeled each long comment from the first 65 words and the last 45 words so a request in the middle of a long comment could have been missed.
- The comparison is uneven because the owner's comments span many threads over many months while each of the other two sets is one thread on one day.
- The same chatbot that works with the owner assigned the labels so a reader should check the labels against the sample file.
- The thread comments are in anonymized wording so the word counts for the two threads are counts of the anonymized wording.
- The folder [owner-comments-full](owner-comments-full) holds 6020 of the owner's comments in full. That folder leaves out repeated comments and 411 comments that a keyword check set aside.
- No other person has rerun the counts which means every number here is unconfirmed.
