# What 458 comments said to a person who was angry with God

An AI chatbot (Claude) anonymized the comments and assigned the labels and wrote this page on 13 November 2026. No other person has rechecked the counts which means every count on this page is unconfirmed.

## What the thread was about

A person posted a video on a social media site. The person in the video describes being angry with God because understanding a life does not give a person authority over that life. Lines from the video that commenters repeated include "knowledge is not sovereignty" and "my capacity to care exceeds my capacity to protect".

## What each comment did

One comment can carry several labels.

- 161 comments said "I feel this too" or thanked the speaker.
- 54 comments reacted or joked or repeated a line from the video.
- 53 comments pointed to scripture or a holy book or a religion. The most repeated passage was the line in Ecclesiastes about more wisdom bringing more sorrow.
- 46 comments told the speaker to accept human limits without leaning on God such as "Life just is".
- 37 comments said the speaker has a flaw such as ego or impatience.
- 35 comments told the speaker to surrender to God or trust God or pray.
- 31 comments offered a different spiritual picture such as "we are all God" or Buddhist detachment.
- 27 comments told the speaker to stop analyzing and act or live or be still.
- 25 comments said the reward or the explanation comes later such as "the next life will be even better".
- 20 comments said the blame does not belong on God.
- 19 comments said that no God exists or that the commenter left religion.
- 17 comments pointed at people and at how people arranged society.
- 14 comments said that anger at God is allowed.
- 12 comments showed care for the speaker such as a prayer or a hug.
- 8 comments talked about the other commenters and 8 comments offered a practical step such as crying or exercise and 6 comments invited a private message or promoted an account.

## Findings

1. Shared feeling was the single most common thing a comment did because 161 of 458 comments said "I feel this too" or thanked the speaker.
2. More comments gave a direction than joined the feeling. 222 comments pointed the speaker somewhere else and did not say that the commenter shares the feeling. 146 comments stayed with the feeling and gave no direction. 31 comments did both.
3. The most common direction was back toward God because 112 comments pointed to scripture or surrender or a later reward or the claim that God is not to blame.
4. 25 comments said the reward or the explanation comes later. The page named reflections.md in the word evolution folder discusses why a reward that arrives after the work is over cannot guide the work.
5. Very few comments invited the speaker to say more. The chatbot's reading found about 4 comments with an open question about the speaker's own view such as "What have you unlearned?"
6. 86 percent of the comments have more than five words (396 of 458) and the middle comment runs 17 words. The same gauge gave 97 percent in the thread where a homeowner asked for advice and 89 percent in the thread about a famous executive who resigned and 80 percent in a large public archive.
7. A name for God sits in more pieces of comment text than any other idea and words about understanding or knowledge or wisdom come second and words about anger come third.

## A narrative built from the twenty most common words

Each of the twenty most common words is in bold. The word "brother" is one of the twenty words and appears as a quoted greeting from the commenters.

A person stood up and said "I am **angry** with **God**". Many **people** answered that the same **anger** lives in them because **knowledge** of how **life** works did not give them **control** over life. Those people said that **understanding** a wound and healing a wound are separate things since a person can **understand** every cause of a pain and still feel the pain. Other people answered with **wisdom** from old books because those books say that more wisdom brings more sorrow. Those commenters asked the speaker to **believe** that God is **good** and to wait for a **time** when the reasons will be shown. A third group said that the **human** **experience** is a mix of beauty and hurt and that **peace** comes when a person stops trying to **change** what one person cannot change. Under all three answers ran **love** for the speaker because strangers from across the **world** greeted the speaker as "**brother**" and said that the speaker is not alone in the feeling.

The twenty words hold no word for blame and no word for disbelief. So the narrative carries the warm middle of the thread and leaves out the comments that placed the problem inside the speaker and the comments that said no God exists.

## Limits of the count

- An AI chatbot wrote the wording of the anonymized comments and that wording can drift from what the original writer meant.
- The labels are one chatbot's reading of each comment so a second reader could label some comments differently.
- Comments that were pictures or unreadable are missing.
- The count of words longer than five was made on the anonymized wording which can differ from the original by a word or two. A count on the collected text before anonymizing gave 80 percent and that count includes broken fragments.
- The word graphs count the commenters' own words in 528 pieces of collected text which includes fragments and quoted scripture.
- The speaker and the commenters are described with gender-neutral words so that a reader of any gender can apply the reflections.
