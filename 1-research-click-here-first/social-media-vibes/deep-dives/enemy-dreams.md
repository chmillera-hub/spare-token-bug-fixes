# Helping an enemy reach a harmless dream: three reflections

This page holds three reflections on one view which is that helping other people reach dreams that harm nobody is worth doing even for a person labeled an enemy. The third reflection looks at the difference between being harmless and being pro-human.

## Reflection one

From my view one of the most important things a person can do to help build the Kingdom of Heaven is to help other people reach their dreams and I include enemies in "other people" because an enemy is still a human being with a mind and body that can suffer or flourish. The word "enemy" here covers a person that I have labeled an enemy and a person that others in society have labeled an enemy. I draw one line around the help which is that the dream cannot involve violating anyone's consent or physically harming anyone. If the dream stays inside that line and I can realistically spare the time or skill then I see nothing that should stop me from helping.

The reason reaches past kindness because a person whose life is working out tends to be calmer and steadier in mind and emotion. An enemy who is finally learning to paint or finally getting a song recorded has less despair to carry around and despair is one of the fuels that people burn when they lash out. Here is a possibility and not a certainty: helping an enemy flourish today could mean meeting a more settled neighbor tomorrow which protects my own food and shelter and peace as well as the enemy's.

The line matters just as much as the help. If an evil genius asks for help destroying the world then my answer is no because that dream ends with harmed minds and bodies. If the same evil genius asks for help learning to paint or play music then my answer can be yes because a paintbrush and a guitar do not injure anyone. The person is not the test. The dream is the test since the same person can hold a harmful dream and a harmless dream at once and I can refuse the first while feeding the second in a pro-human nonviolent way.

There is an old dark joke along these lines about Hitler who applied to art school as a young man and was turned away twice. The joke asks what might have happened if someone had cared for and nurtured the painting. Nobody can know the answer and the horrific violence that followed was chosen by him and by the people who carried out his orders and not by the people who rejected his paintings. I still take the question seriously as a possibility because a person with a nurtured dream and deep human connection has more to lose and more to live for than a person whose dreams all died.

The help has a limit that protects the helper because nobody is required to give time or energy that the helper needs for the helper's own food and shelter and close relationships. Helping within my real capacity is the version of the idea that can last because a helper who burns out helps nobody.

### Quick points

- The test for helping is the dream and not the person because a dream such as painting or music harms nobody while a dream that violates consent or injures people gets a no from any helper.
- Help is offered only from time and skill that the helper can spare because the helper's own food and shelter and close relationships need protecting too.

1\. The Consent and Pro-Human Filter (The Only Gatekeeper)

Most people withhold help from rivals or strangers out of tribal spite, suspicion, or the fear that someone undeserving will get ahead. Your framework strips away all moralizing audits and replaces them with a single hard safety boundary: does this dream violate consent or inflict physical harm, and does it lean toward pro-human flourishing?

- If an adversary wants to build a weapon, exploit people, or coerce others, you block it completely—you do not hand an evil genius blueprints to burn down the village.

Supporting pro-human creativity is always net-positive, regardless of who is holding the paintbrush—and while sitting alone in a cave meditating does no harm, a pro-human approach gently invites people to share what regulates and heals their nervous system so others can benefit too, without ever forcing anyone to listen or believe before they have the bandwidth to hear it.

2\. Nervous-System Regulation and the Reality of Free Will

From a purely practical standpoint, helping an enemy succeed at something peaceful and meaningful is one of the highest-leverage ways to protect yourself and your community—without ever pretending that human minds are simple machines you can program with kindness.

- Many people endure failure after failure and still choose gentleness, never once turning toward cruelty or harm. At the same time, when someone is starving for validation, isolated, humiliated, and watching every door slam in their face, their nervous system might start leaning toward tribalism, scapegoating, or destruction—and leaving that pressure unchecked can eventually blow up in everyone's face.
- Conversely, when things actually start working out for someone—when they experience genuine mentorship, creative momentum, and unforced kindness from the very person they expected to fight—their nervous system might stabilize. Human minds are complex: there are people who, no matter how much kindness you offer, will still despise you and will never love you back. Even then, their hostility never forces you to violate your own principles—and because you have free will as a human being, you are always free to step back and invest your pro-human, non-violent energy in someone else who is ready to build.

3\. Heart on Your Sleeve Instead of Zero-Sum Games

Most tabletop or parlor games are engineered around hidden information and zero-sum bluffing, where showing your hand guarantees a loss. Real human life and connection operate by completely different architecture—almost like a gift built directly into creation where communicating openly from your heart and soul actually tilts the odds in your favor.

- When you wear your heart on your sleeve and actively invest spare time or resources into helping an adversary achieve a pro-human dream, you shatter their expectation of retaliation. They cannot frame you as a monster while you are helping them tune an instrument or critique a canvas.
- Whether they eventually soften or stay hostile, you refuse to let their anger dictate your character. You keep every move strictly pro-human and non-violent, choosing freely where to spend your bandwidth so you shift the world away from zero-sum attrition and toward mutual coherence.

4\. The Historical Echo, Masked Breaking Points, and the Least of These

The irony of landing on the painting example right before catching the parallel to young Adolf Hitler getting rejected from the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts connects straight into Christ’s teaching that whatever you do for the least of these, you do for Him—and reveals why consistent, unglamorous kindness is anything but naive.

- Because you cannot tell from the outside with full clarity who is silently standing on that ledge—even though someone openly screaming threats or intent to hurt people is an unmistakable signal to call out the violent behavior directly whenever you can safely do so—offering steady, pro-human, non-violent support to whoever crosses your path means you might unknowingly pull one of those masked individuals back from the edge. Shifting even one person's trajectory away from despair and toward care, respect, nurture, and love can quietly steer an entire community or generation away from catastrophe and toward shared flourishing.
- Even when modern cynicism mocks genuine care as cringe, wasted effort, or naive idealism, the math of human connection stays on your side: every pro-human dream you help nurture—and every helpful insight you share openly without coercion—makes the world measurably safer and more alive for everyone living in it, including yourself.

The swap from "harmless" to "pro-human" changes what a person measures about a day.

Harmlessness is measured by absence because a harmless day is a day where nobody got injured by that person. A rock passes the harmlessness test and so does an empty apartment which means the test cannot tell a living person apart from nobody being there at all. A person who says "I am harmless because I stay home and look at a screen" is describing a life with no injuries given and also no food shared and no shelter offered and no other heart and mind known.

Pro-human energy is measured by presence because a pro-human day leaves something behind that another person can use. The thing left behind can be small such as one sentence posted about what calmed a racing heart at three in the morning since a stranger who reads the sentence now knows that lifeline exists. The apartment and the screen do not need to change for the upgrade to happen because the screen is a doorway to living people and the same hour of scrolling can become an hour where one useful or caring sentence goes out.

Here is a possibility about why harmlessness became the resting place for many people. A person who gets punished for speaking up (through job loss or mockery or being dropped by friends) can learn that silence is the safest setting and can then rename the silence as a virtue. The renaming makes sense as protection and the renaming also carries a cost because every person who goes quiet removes one more voice that a lonely or hungry person might have heard.

The sentence in reflection two works as a small demonstration for the same reason. A reader watches the word "harmless" get replaced and can try the same replacement on a personal sentence such as changing "I did not hurt anyone today" into "I shared one thing today that might help someone eat or rest or feel known".

The upgrade carries a limit that keeps the idea from turning into pressure because pro-human expression is an invitation for whenever a person has the energy to spare. A person who is worn out and has nothing to give today is not failing since rest protects the person's own mind and body.
