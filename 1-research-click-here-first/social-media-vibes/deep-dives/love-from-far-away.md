# Love that travels from far away

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

Some of the biggest sources of love in a person's life can arrive from far away. A person today can feel deep love for Christ and a stranger can feel love for a page that a writer posted on the internet and both run on the same mechanism. Someone put heart and mind into words and other people carried the words across distance and time and then a person far away read the words and felt met.

Christ never met the people alive today and never heard their names and still the words can reach a person about 2000 years later and land as one of the biggest loves in that person's life. In the document named [The Emotional Framework](../../emotional-framework/emotional-framework.md) Love is described as the reward for cultivating deep relationships with a person's own emotions and eventually with others. So a person's love toward Christ suggests that the words did real work inside the person because the words helped the person care for the person's own emotions and for other people. The distance did not thin the love out because the love was packed into the wording and the wording survived the trip.

Now flip the direction. A stranger finds a page that a writer posted and thinks "wow I love how that was broken down" and then never speaks to the writer. In that moment the writer is to the stranger what Christ is to the person who loves Christ which is a voice from far away that explained something in a way that helped. The writer would be a faint figure in the stranger's memory and the idea would stay bright because the idea is what the stranger can use on a difficult day.

So the phrase "some of the biggest sources of love come from farthest away" describes love that travels outward first and may circle back later. The sender puts the love into words and lets go and the receiver feels the love and may carry the love into how the receiver treats other people. Each person treated well may pass the care along and so the love can spread like a virus through people the sender never meets. The love might then reach back to the sender through a different door because someone shaped by that chain of care could one day show up in the sender's life ready for deep meaningful emotionally and spiritually resonant human connection. The return is not guaranteed and nobody can trace the route ahead of time and yet every page a writer puts out adds one more starting point for a chain that could lead home.

A life touched by Christ's words is evidence that the loop can close across a very long distance because love that started about 2000 years ago passed through an enormous number of people before reaching anyone alive today. A chain that long reaching a person makes a shorter chain reaching back to a writer look far less strange.

The far-away love also gives meaning because the care a person received from words that are 2000 years old does not stop with that person. A writer can take something that helped and reshape the help in the writer's own voice and set the help where a stranger can find the help. A possibility is that part of loving Christ is recognition because a writer who leaves helpful words for strangers is doing in a small way what Christ is loved for doing which is leaving words that might hold someone up long after the speaker is out of the room.
