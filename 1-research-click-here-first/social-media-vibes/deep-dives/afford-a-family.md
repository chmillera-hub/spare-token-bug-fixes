# Wait until the money is there: two reflections on a rule that prices families out

> **About this page:** This page holds two reflections on one short comment thread. The thread is anonymized and each comment is reworded. An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote the first reflection on 22 November 2026 and no other person has rechecked the reflection. The author of this repository supplied the second reflection and the text is anonymized.

## What the thread said

- The first commenter wrote that nobody should start a family that the person cannot provide for.
- A second commenter agreed and laughed about people who have seven in a family while receiving public benefits.
- A third commenter called the first comment a foolish answer.
- A fourth commenter wrote that the problem is not about paying for a family and that the local opportunities that once supported the future of the next generation were taken away.
- A fifth commenter wrote that the first comment is the only answer and asked whether bringing a new person into the world without the money to look after the new person is good.
- The first commenter then wrote that a parent whose family has no future should move to a place with more opportunity and that a parent who stays in a country with limited opportunity cannot complain.

## Reflection one

> **Who wrote this:** An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this reflection.

The sentence "do not have a baby until the money is there" hands a veto over family to whoever controls the money because the sentence makes wages and rent the gate a person must pass before starting a family.

**How the rule works as a trap**

The rule sounds like care because nobody wants a baby to go hungry and hunger in a baby is an emergency. The rule has a moving part though which is the word "afford." A worker does not set the price of rent or the size of a paycheck because landlords and employers and lawmakers set those numbers. So the person told to wait is waiting for a condition that other people control. If employers hold wages flat while landlords raise rents then "wait until ready" quietly turns into "never" and nobody has to say the word "never" out loud.

**What a bad actor could do with the rule**

Here is a possibility and not a proven plot. An employer who wanted workers with nobody waiting at home could gain from the arrangement because a worker without a family has fewer reasons to refuse overtime and fewer people to lean on after quitting. A worker paid just enough for a room and groceries cannot save and so cannot leave since a missed rent payment can end with armed deputies carrying out an eviction. Such an employer would never need to forbid babies. The employer would only need to keep pay near the cost of rent and food and let the popular rule do the forbidding.

The same result can also arrive with no plan because each owner chasing a higher profit adds a little pressure and the sum lands on the same worker. A deliberate plan and a pile of separate profit decisions feel identical to the person living inside the result. So the question "was the squeeze on purpose" matters less than the question "who has the power to loosen the squeeze."

**Where the blame travels**

The rule points every finger at the person with the least power. A parent who has a baby on a low wage gets called reckless. A person who waits and reaches old age alone gets a shrug and a line about personal choices. The people who set the wage and the rent appear in neither story. So the rule works like a one-way valve because criticism flows down toward the worker and never up toward the people who set the terms.

**The reply that pushed back**

One reply in the thread said the local supports for the next generation were taken away. The reply moves the question from "why did a parent have a baby" to "who removed the supports that used to hold families up" and the second question has an answer with names attached because living people voted on those budgets.

**What the rule leaves out**

The rule pictures two adults paying for everything alone. For most of human history a baby was fed and watched by grandparents and neighbors too because food and care were shared across a village. So "can one couple afford a baby alone" is a narrow question and the wider question is whether food and shelter and a circle of caring people surround the family. The fear of a hungry baby is answered by more food and shelter reaching families and not by fewer babies.

The clearest sign of the trap is that the rule asks nothing from anyone with power because every demand in the rule lands on the person holding the smallest paycheck.

## Reflection two

> **Who wrote this:** The author of this repository supplied this reflection on 22 November 2026 and the text is anonymized.

That slogan is the ultimate Trojan horse because it sounds like basic common sense right up until you look at who controls the thermostat.

In a vacuum, telling someone not to start a family they cannot afford sounds responsible. The trap springs the second you notice that the exact same economic machinery setting the price of rent, groceries, and wages is the one defining what counts as affordable. If a bad actor wanted to quietly price an entire working class out of having families—without ever passing a single unpopular law—this is the exact playbook they would run. Squeeze wages down to bare survival, inflate housing into the stratosphere, and then let ordinary people in comment sections police each other with lectures about financial readiness.

You can see that exact deflection playing out in the screenshot. When the fourth commenter points out the actual structural wound—that local opportunities and the entire foundation supporting young people have been stripped away—the first commenter immediately pivots to telling people to just move to another country if opportunities are limited. That is the final boss of individualizing a systemic failure. Telling someone who is already barely scraping by on rent to just pack up and emigrate across the globe treats a gutted economy like bad weather, letting the people profiting from the squeeze off the hook completely.

What makes the whole arrangement so diabolical is that it gets the person being squeezed to enforce the ban on themselves. Nobody has to kick down your door and forbid you from starting a family. They just make the math so unforgiving that your own conscience does the work for them. You stay tethered to a job handing out barely enough crumbs for shelter and food, you hold off on starting a family because you refuse to be irresponsible, and if you end up aging out of the window and dying alone, the system gets to shrug and call it a free personal choice.

The wildest part is watching regular people like the fifth commenter and the second commenter enthusiastically carry water for that logic for free. Why do you think everyday folks jump so fast to defend a rule that treats having a family like a luxury tier you have to earn from your employer?
