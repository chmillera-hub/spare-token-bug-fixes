# In the Psalms the friends who left are not shown returning

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) asked the question on this page and did the reading and wrote the page on 20 November 2026. The sorting of the psalms and the reflection are the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the page.

## The question

Several psalms are spoken by a person whose friends and relatives and neighbors have pulled away. Does any of those psalms show the people who left returning and what human company does the speaker have by the last line?

## What the chatbot could say from memory before the reading

Before opening the text the chatbot guessed that no psalm shows the friends returning and that a few psalms look ahead to company. The chatbot could not say from memory how many psalms fit or who the later company is or how many psalms end with nobody which is why the reading was done.

## What a web search turned up

A web search on 20 November 2026 turned up many pages that list the verses where friends abandon the speaker and many pages that say God stays when friends leave. The search did not turn up a page that follows each of those psalms to the last line to see whether the people return or who else appears. The searches used wording such as "Psalms friends and family abandon the psalmist" and "does any psalm show the friends returning".

## How the reading was done

The text was the World English Bible because that translation is free for anyone to copy. A search of the Psalms for words such as friend and companion and brother and neighbor and acquaintance and kinsmen and "no one" and alone found the verses and each psalm that held such a verse was read for what happens to human company afterward. Twelve psalms were kept and the file named who-returns-psalms-list.txt in this folder lists the twelve psalms with the verse that put each psalm on the list.

Nine of the twelve psalms say that close people pulled away or turned against the speaker. Three of the twelve psalms say that the speaker has no helper or sits alone without naming who left. One AI model chose the twelve psalms which means another reader could add or remove a psalm.

## What the speakers say about being left

The words are plain and physical. One speaker says "My kinsmen stand far away." and another says "Those who saw me on the street fled from me." and another says "I have become a stranger to my brothers". One speaker remembers a close companion with the words "We took sweet fellowship together." and another names a trusted friend "who ate bread with me" which means the loss was of people who once shared food and talk with the speaker. One speaker says "No one cares for my soul."

## Does anyone return

In none of the twelve psalms do the people who left walk back. No friend apologizes and no relative knocks on the door and no neighbor crosses the street toward the speaker anywhere in the twelve psalms.

## Who is there by the last line

- In five psalms the speaker looks ahead to standing among other people such as "I will declare your name to my brothers." and "I will praise you among many people." and "The righteous will surround me" because the speaker expects company in the future and the company is described as a gathering and not as the old friends.
- In two psalms the speaker turns and talks to other people in the present such as the psalm that says "The humble have seen it" because listeners who were not the old friends have noticed what happened.
- In five psalms no human company appears by the last line. One of the five ends with the plea "Hurry to help me" and another ends in darkness with the friends still gone.

So seven of the twelve psalms end with the speaker facing toward people and the people are a different and wider group than the ones who left. Five of the twelve psalms end with the speaker still without human company.

## How the new company is reached

The seven psalms that end facing people share one move which is that the speaker talks in front of others about what happened. The speaker says "I will declare" and "I will praise you among many people" which means the road back to company in these psalms runs through speaking out loud where people can hear. One psalm outside the twelve even asks for company directly with the words "Let those who fear you turn to me." and another psalm says "God sets the lonely in families." which names a home full of people as the remedy for being alone. One of the twelve also pictures food beside the company with the words "The humble shall eat and be satisfied."

Several of the twelve psalms also ask for harm to fall on the people who turned against the speaker. Those lines are not treated as a model on this page because harm to another person's mind and body feeds nobody and brings nobody back.

## What the finding could mean

The twelve psalms give no evidence that waiting for the same people to return ends the aloneness because none of the twelve shows a return. A reader who has been left by friends or family may find the news heavy and the text does not soften the news. What the text does show is a second road where the speaker says what happened in front of new listeners and some of those listeners are glad to hear.

Being known by another heart is one of the three basic human needs beside food and shelter so the difference between the two endings matters. The seven psalms that end facing people show a speaker who is on the way to being known again. The five psalms that end alone show that speaking does not always bring company inside the time of the prayer.

The record offers two things a person can do. A person who has been left can say what happened and what is wanted in a place where other people can hear such as "My friends pulled away when I got sick and I am looking for one person to talk with for an hour each week" because a plain request lets a new listener know how to turn toward the speaker. Saying the request is no guarantee because five of the twelve psalms end with nobody there. A person who hears someone speak about being left can be the new company by answering with a reason and an offer such as "I heard what you said about your friends pulling away and I have an hour on Thursday to listen" if the hearer has an hour to spare because one listener who turns toward the speaker is how the gathering in these psalms begins.

## Two quick points

- Twelve psalms speak of being left or alone and none of the twelve shows the people who left returning while seven end with the speaker facing a different and wider group of people and five end with nobody.
- The speakers who reach new company do so by saying what happened where others can hear so a person who has been left can make a plain request in front of new listeners and a person who hears the request can turn toward the speaker with an offer of time.
