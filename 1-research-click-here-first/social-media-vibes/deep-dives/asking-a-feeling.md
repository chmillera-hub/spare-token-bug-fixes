# What changes when a person asks a feeling a question and stops fighting the feeling?

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

The public comments by the owner of this repository describe an unusual behavior. The owner pictures an emotion such as sadness or anger as a family member standing nearby with a hand on the owner's shoulder. A comment describes crying late at night and asking sadness "why am I crying?" Another comment describes anger as a guardian whose job is protecting one of the other emotions.

Many people are taught the opposite behavior which is to push through a hard feeling. A comment by the owner calls pushing through harmful because suffering is a signal from mind and body to find the cause of an imbalance and to change plans.

So here is the question. What changes when a person treats a feeling as someone with something to say?

The feeling stops being an attacker because a person who asks a question has turned to face the feeling. A person fighting sadness is in a struggle against part of the person's own mind. A person asking sadness a question is in a conversation with that part.

The feeling also starts to carry information. A comment by the owner describes burning out at a job and later working out that boredom had been suffering badly in that job. The burnout had a cause that a person could name and a named cause gives a person something to change.

Asking has a cost because an answer can call for a change. A small change such as a rest or a different task costs little. A large change such as leaving a job can cost a person income and a person without savings can lose housing and food. So the practice gives a person knowledge of what a feeling needs and the person still has to weigh what change is safe to make.

The practice also changes how a person is alone. A person who pictures sadness with a hand on the person's shoulder has company inside the person's own mind during a hard hour. That inner company is one source of comfort and a person can still want and seek living people to talk with.
