# What happens to a worn-out person in the stories of the Bible

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) asked the question on this page and did the reading and wrote the page on 20 November 2026. The sorting of the scenes and the reflection are the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the page.

## The question

The stories of the Bible show many people who are faint or weary or asleep from exhaustion. When a person is worn out in those stories what do the people nearby do and which response shows up most often?

## What the chatbot could say from memory before the reading

Before opening the text the chatbot could name about a dozen scenes from memory and guessed that help would be the most common response and that a few scenes would show a tired person being taken advantage of. The chatbot had no count and could not say what kind of help is given most often or what words are spoken to a tired person which is why the reading was done.

## What a web search turned up

A web search on 20 November 2026 turned up pages that list Bible characters who were exhausted and one reference page that lists five passages where other people give physical help to a tired person. The search did not turn up a tally that also counts the scenes where a tired person is refused or harmed. The search used wording such as "Bible stories exhausted weary person how others respond".

## How the reading was done

The text was the World English Bible because that translation is free for anyone to copy. A search of the story books for words such as weary and faint and tired and exhausted and asleep and "no strength" found 120 verses and a second pass added scenes the chatbot remembered that use other words such as famished. The reading kept the scenes where a person or a group is worn out and somebody else is there to respond. The count was 33 scenes and the file named worn-out-responses-list.txt in this folder lists the 33 scenes in six groups so that any reader can check the sorting.

One AI model chose and sorted the scenes which means another reader could add or remove a scene and the counts below could shift by a few.

## The count

- In 15 scenes the worn-out person is given food or drink or a place to rest or has the load shared or is strengthened.
- In 6 scenes the worn-out or sleeping person is attacked or tricked or bargained with or a plan is made to do so.
- In 4 scenes the worn-out or sleeping person is woken with a complaint or met with a question.
- In 3 scenes food is refused to worn-out people or kept from worn-out people.
- In 1 scene a sleeping person is found by an opponent and left unharmed.
- In 4 scenes something else happens.

Help is the largest group at 15 of the 33 scenes. Harm or refusal adds up to 9 of the 33 scenes which means a worn-out person in these stories is helped more often than harmed and is still at real risk.

## What the help looks like

The help is physical before the help is anything else. Bread or other food is handed over in about half of the 15 scenes and water or another drink in four and a seat or a bed or time to sleep in five. A leader whose arms grow heavy is given a stone to sit on while two companions hold his arms up until sunset. A man found faint in a field is given bread and water and figs and raisins and the text says "his spirit came again to him". A prophet who lies down under a tree and sleeps is woken twice by a touch and finds a cake baked on coals and a jar of water each time and is allowed to sleep again in between.

In four of the 15 scenes the help is a shared load. A leader who judges disputes from morning to evening is told by his father-in-law "You will surely wear away" and "the thing is too heavy for you" and the leader then hands part of the work to others. The same leader later says "I am not able to bear all this people alone" and seventy others are appointed to carry the burden with him.

The words spoken to a tired person in the helping scenes do not blame the person. The messenger who feeds the prophet says "the journey is too great for you" which puts the weight on the road and not on the traveler. One teacher sees friends with no time even to eat and says "rest awhile".

## What the harm looks like

The six scenes of harm show tiredness treated as an opening. A hunter who walks in famished asks his brother for stew and the brother answers "First" and then names the price which is the hunter's birthright. Raiders attack the people at the back of a column who were "faint and weary". A plan is laid to strike a king "while he is weary and exhausted". Two men are killed while asleep and a third is seized after being put to sleep on a lap. Harm done to a tired person is harm to a mind and body that had no strength left to answer and nothing in the text presents the harm as a model.

In three more scenes food is kept from worn-out people. Two towns refuse bread to men who are "faint" and a king forbids his whole army to eat until evening so that "The people were very faint".

In four scenes a sleeper is woken with a complaint or a tired person is met with a question. Friends wake a sleeping teacher in a storm with "don't you care that we are dying?" and the same teacher later wakes his own friends with "Couldn't you watch one hour?" though one account says the friends were "sleeping because of grief".

## What the finding could mean

Food and a place to rest are two of the three basic human needs and the helping scenes meet those needs first and talk second. The third need is to be known by another heart and one line in Ecclesiastes ties that need to tiredness with the words "woe to him who is alone when he falls". Another book says "To him who is ready to faint" and then "kindness should be shown from his friend".

The record offers two things a person can do. A person near someone who is worn out can copy the helping scenes by bringing bread and water and a place to sit before offering any advice if the helper has food and time to spare and can use words that put the weight on the load such as "the journey is too great for you" because those words tell a tired person that the tiredness is no fault. A person who is worn out can say the tiredness aloud the way the leader did with "I am not able to bear all this people alone" because that sentence is what led to seventy others sharing the load. Saying the tiredness aloud is no guarantee because 9 of the 33 scenes show a tired person refused or harmed and the scene of the stew shows that a person who wants something may use the moment to bargain.

## Two quick points

- In 33 scenes of worn-out people in the stories of the World English Bible help is given in 15 and the help is bread and water and rest and a shared load before any advice while harm or refusal shows up in 9.
- A person near someone who is worn out can bring food and a seat and say "the journey is too great for you" if the helper has enough to spare and a person who is worn out can say "it is too heavy for me" aloud because that sentence is what led others to share the load.
