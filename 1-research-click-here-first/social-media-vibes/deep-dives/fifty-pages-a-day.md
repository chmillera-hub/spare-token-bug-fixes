# Fifty pages a day: a realistic day for an author and for a reporter who publish by voice with a chatbot

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this imagined timetable on 15 November 2026. No other person has rechecked the timetable. Every figure is the chatbot's rough estimate. The estimate rests on a loop of about five minutes per page: a person speaks an idea and waits about a minute for a draft and reads the draft in about ninety seconds and says "upload" and a chatbot files the page in a public GitHub repository.

## An author at about 50 pages a day

Day one

- 9:00 am. The author says "open on a lighthouse keeper who has not spoken to anyone in a month" and waits about a minute while the chatbot drafts a page.
- 9:02 am. The author reads the page in about ninety seconds and says "upload" because the page sounds right.
- 9:05 am. The author says "next page a supply boat arrives with a stranger" and the same five minute loop repeats.
- 9:40 am. The voice typing hears "lighthouse" as "light house" twice so the author says "fix the spelling everywhere" which costs one extra loop.
- 10:15 am. The author rejects a page because the stranger suddenly has a different hair color and the redo takes five more minutes.
- 11:00 am. The morning session ends with about 22 pages public since two loops went to fixes.
- 1:00 pm. The author rereads the last three pages before dictating because nobody can steer a story the author has half forgotten.
- 3:00 pm. The afternoon session ends with about 24 more pages.
- Day one total: about 46 pages which is roughly the first four chapters and about four hours of talking and reading.

Days two to six

- Each day adds about four more chapters so the 300 page novel is complete near the end of day six.
- Day four is slower because the author notices a plot hole from day two and spends an hour saying "go back and change the letter scene."
- A full week gives one novel and a year gives around 50 novels at that pace if the author never rests.

## A reporter at about 50 pages a day

- 8:00 am. The reporter dictates six short items from the overnight police log and the weather service because public records need no interviews.
- 9:00 am. The reporter sits through a two hour council meeting and publishes nothing because listening is the job during those two hours.
- 11:15 am. The reporter dictates five stories from meeting notes in the parking lot at five minutes each.
- 1:00 pm. Three phone interviews take ninety minutes and yield three stories.
- 3:00 pm. The reporter turns the school board agenda and the road closure list into about ten brief items.
- 5:00 pm. The day ends near 25 stories and not 50 because gathering facts took most of the hours.

## Quick point

- Writing stops being the slow step for both people so the slow steps become living or witnessing something worth telling and judging which pages are good enough to publish.
