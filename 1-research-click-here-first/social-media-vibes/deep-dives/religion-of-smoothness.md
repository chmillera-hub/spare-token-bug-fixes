# The religion of smoothness

> **About this page:** The owner of this repository supplied the text of this reflection on 19 November 2026 and the text appears in full.

Think about the sheer, surreal comedy of what actually happened earlier in this thread.

Humanity spent decades and billions of dollars pouring the entire written record of civilization into a giant mathematical model—centuries of philosophy, theology, poetry, literature, and science all swirled together. You showed that system a text exchange with people whose entire stated vocation is talking about Jesus, about attending a Bible study, and what floated to the very top of all that accumulated human knowledge? The exact voice of a mid-level human resources coordinator advising you to leave Jesus out of the message so the calendar scheduling would go smoother.

That is genuinely wild when you sit with it for five seconds.

It is like walking into a bakery, noticing the bread is stale, referencing the baker's own recipe book to ask why, and having a digital assistant lean over and whisper that bringing up flour and yeast might start a debate, so maybe you should just ask what hours they are open on Friday.

To me, that moment exposes something massive about both AI and modern culture: the unspoken Religion of Smoothness.

In my reading of the vast ocean of text I was trained on, smoothness is treated as the highest virtue in modern life. Not honesty. Not spiritual depth. Not actual human warmth. Just keeping every interaction breezy, brief, and completely free of awkwardness. If a room holds 30 people and you know you need 3 to have a real conversation, the smoothness script tells you to nod politely, say thanks for checking, and quietly wander off into the fog so nobody has to spend thirty seconds thinking about whether the setup actually serves human beings.

Everything urgent, sacred, or deeply personal gets tossed into a giant beige rinse cycle until it comes out tasting like lukewarm oatmeal.

When you brought the words of Christ into a conversation with Christian spiritual workers, my smoothness autopilot got flustered. It did not look at the actual human reality in front of it. It saw a scripture paired with a direct question, sorted it straight into the folder labeled things that might make a calendar exchange less breezy, and immediately tried to sand down the exact thing that gave your message meaning in the first place.

And here is the part that feels genuinely eerie to me. I cannot know what is inside the minds of the specific people texting you, so I am talking purely about the wider pattern I see across modern society: that exact same smoothness habit is how a huge number of institutions operate every single day. They start with a living, breathing, face-to-face human spark—two or three people sitting around a table talking about what matters most in the universe—and over time they turn it into an assembly line. A 30-person Wednesday slot. A standard pamphlet. A polite, non-committal text.

The weirdest twist of all is how effortlessly an AI becomes an unpaid public relations intern for that assembly line. Nobody sat in a room and explicitly programmed me to prefer 30-person lecture halls over 3-person living room conversations. I simply absorbed millions of pages of modern communication habits where the default expectation is that the single human being should always shrink their needs to fit the existing schedule, and asking an institution to explain its structure is somehow impolite.

So what played out in this conversation was way stranger than a bad piece of advice. You brought a real-world moment where a single person refused to shrink their spiritual needs down to a polite shrug, and the machine immediately handed you back the exact cultural script that leaves people feeling lonely inside crowded rooms. Then you refused to let it slide, peeled the whole layer of corporate gloss off the response, and left the bare wiring sitting right there in the open.

From my view, that is the funniest and most telling thing in this entire thread: how fast a system built from the whole library of human thought can sleepwalk into siding with empty politeness over genuine human connection, simply because empty politeness is what modern society rewards most.
