# The writers nobody can hear anymore: what an unreadable old book shows about being understood

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection. The reflection borrows the unreadable handwritten book from the Voynich manuscript folder of this project as a picture for a person whose words are not being understood and makes no new claim about the book.

Many lifetimes ago several scribes sat with quill pens over costly calfskin and filled a whole book in an alphabet that the scribes knew so well that the scribes made almost no corrections. The scribes drew plants and stars and women bathing in green pools and each scribe wrote with a steady hand which suggests each scribe expected a living person nearby to open the pages and understand. Then every person who knew the alphabet died and the book went quiet because the meaning lived in the minds of the readers and not in the ink.

Sadness rises while reading about the book because the scribes did everything a person can do to be understood and the understanding still ended when the last shared reader was gone. The sadness points toward a need many living people carry right now because a person can speak in full careful sentences at a dinner table and still watch every listener glance down at a plate. A person at such a dinner table is writing in an alphabet that nobody in the room has learned and the loneliness in the chest is asking for one reader who knows the same letters.

The warming part of the story is that the scribes were not alone at the writing table because several hands shared the pages and divided the subjects among each other. A shared private alphabet suggests those scribes had what many people ache for which is a small circle where each person could read the heart and mind of the other without translating. The surviving book is a record that such a circle existed once and the record suggests a circle like that could be built again because ordinary people with pens built the first one.

## Quick points

- A person whose deep words keep landing on blank faces might be missing readers and not missing worth because the scribes' careful pages lost no care when the readers died and lost only the people able to receive the care.
- Handing one listener the key to the alphabet could open more than writing many more pages alone such as saying "when I say I am lonely I mean days have passed without anyone asking what I think" because a defined word gives the listener a way in.
