# The wish for one enemy

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

A gardener watched the garden wither over several dry weeks as the leaves yellowed a little more each day and the gardener finally asked two neighbors "why are my plants dying and what should I do next". One neighbor said "the family across the road has been poisoning your soil" and then hearing this the gardener's hand reached for a stone because the neighbor's sentence already held an instruction inside which was "someone did this to you and so someone must pay". The other neighbor said "Wait, before you grab that stone I believe that fallen leaves have been blocking the ditch that carries water to your rows for weeks" and then hearing this the gardener's hand reached for a rake instead because a blocked ditch asks for clearing and asks for no one to be hurt. The garden was equally withered under both answers and yet the gardener's next action was chosen by whichever sentence the gardener acted on because an answer to "why does my garden hurt" works like a quiet order about what to do. So an answer that includes harm hands the listener a stone and an answer that includes a useful action that avoids harm hands the listener a rake because a person's hands often follow the cause that the person has accepted. An answer such as "that sucks that your garden is withering and anyway have a nice day" hands the listener nothing at all because the answer names no cause and no action and so the garden keeps withering.

One common explanation for a hurt blames one enemy such as one leader or one group of people and blaming one enemy might feel easy because one enemy is a single place to focus energy on and the result might be that the hurt ends when the enemy is punished or goes away or is threatened into silence. The cost of blaming one enemy is that the search for an enemy can slide toward wanting a person harmed because an explanation built around defeating someone leaves little room for caring about that someone's mind and body.

The cost does not end even if the hurt ends when the enemy loses because the explanation that includes harm still exists afterward and can be passed from person to person. An explanation such as "harming a person is acceptable when the person is the cause of my hurt" can travel among the very people a person counts as allies because allies hear the explanation and learn that harm is an allowed answer to hurt. Any ally who later feels hurt can reuse the same explanation with a new name in the place of the enemy and so the harm a person authorized toward someone else could one day return to that person in the same form or in some other form. I think of the returning harm as a kind of mathematical karma because each time a person sends out an explanation that includes harm the number of people who hold permission to harm goes up and a higher number of people holding permission raises the odds that the permission gets used on the sender.

Another common explanation says that the hurt grows out of an ecosystem of causes and the ecosystem explanation holds a real observation because one hurt can have a number of causes that feels almost endless and many of the causes may be ones a person never learns about. Some people finish the ecosystem explanation with "and therefore your contribution is close to nothing and you might as well give up" and that ending allows harm to keep circulating because every person who gives up is one fewer person calling the harm out. A more empowering ending fits the same facts which is "the causes are many and therefore many places exist where I can help" because an ecosystem with countless parts also offers countless places to begin.

An ecosystem of causes is made of living people making choices one at a time and so a person who sees harm can call the harm out to the best of that person's ability in a pro-human nonviolent way which means naming the harmful choice plainly while caring for the mind and body of the person who is making the choice. A person can often give input before a choice is final or acted upon because a choice that is still being made can still take in new information. A landlord chooses a rent and a manager chooses a schedule and a neighbor chooses whether to knock on a door and so each part of the ecosystem has a person who could choose differently. A person cannot change a whole ecosystem alone and yet can speak to the nearest living person about one choice because one choice is small enough to reach.

A person who calls out harm can also offer a replacement whenever possible because removing a harmful action leaves an empty space and a useful action that avoids harm can fill the space the way the rake replaced the stone. Any contribution is greater than zero because one harmful explanation interrupted or one useful action offered changes what the next person might engage in.

Avoiding harm does not mean staying silent because saying "I am lonely" is speaking from the heart and soul out loud while seeking support and care and nurturing from other human beings. Other human beings might feel discomfort at hearing "I am lonely" and a listener who says "I am uncomfortable" is also expressing that listener's own heart and soul and so the speaker can respect the listener's boundary and seek support elsewhere. Stating words out loud is a basic human function and not something that needs to be kept silent because saying "I am lonely" is a request for support and is not a call toward harm against anyone.

A small contribution can end up having a bigger impact because an explanation that includes care can travel the same way an explanation that includes harm travels. So a person who sends a message that says "a choice can be changed while the person making the choice stays cared for" raises the number of people who might have the space to care or the bandwidth to care and the care could one day return to the sender too.

Dominating or controlling one so-called enemy can look like an easy solution and the easy look misleads because domination injures a person's mind and body and hands everyone watching a permission to dominate. No reason makes domination or violence acceptable because the harm lands on a living person and the permission to harm keeps circulating afterward. A person who feels the pull toward an easy solution can put the same energy into empowering the people nearby toward more pro-human nonviolent behavior which means speaking up while a harmful choice is still being made and offering a useful action that avoids harm and helping one person who is hungry or lonely when the helper has food or time to spare.

## Quick points

- An ecosystem of many causes is encouraging news and not a reason to give up because many causes means many places where one person can call out harm and offer a useful action that avoids harm in place of the harm.
- An explanation that authorizes harm toward one person can be packaged and passed around and reused with a different name in the place of the enemy and so harming others to help oneself could end with the same harm aimed at oneself.
- Calling out one specific choice such as a rent increase or a cruel comment keeps a person's energy useful because energy focused on a choice asks for a change and any contribution toward that change is greater than the zero that giving up contributes.
