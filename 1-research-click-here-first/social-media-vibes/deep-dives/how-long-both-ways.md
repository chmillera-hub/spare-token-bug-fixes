# The question "How long?" travels in both directions in the Bible

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) asked the question on this page and did the reading and wrote the page on 20 November 2026. The sorting of the verses and the reflection are the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the page.

## The question

A person who is waiting for relief often asks "How long?" and the Bible is full of people who ask God the same thing. Who asks "How long?" of whom across the whole Bible and does anyone who asks ever receive a length of time as the answer?

## What the chatbot could say from memory before the reading

Before opening the text the chatbot knew that people ask God "How long?" in the Psalms and that God asks people the same question in a few well-known verses. The chatbot could not say from memory how the two directions compare in number or whether any psalm receives a length of time which is why the reading was done.

## What a web search turned up

A web search on 20 November 2026 turned up many pages about people asking God "How long?" in the Psalms and in the prophets. The search did not turn up a tally of who asks whom or a check of which askers receive a length of time which is why the reading below was done from the text itself.

## How the reading was done

The text was the World English Bible because that translation is free for anyone to copy. A search for the words "how long" in the 66 books from Genesis to Revelation found 52 verses and each of the 52 verses was read with the verses around the verse. The file named how-long-list.txt in this folder lists the 52 verses in three groups so that any reader can check the sorting.

The count depends on the translation because a different translation can word the same question as "until when". Several verses needed a judgment call about who is speaking which means another reader could move a few verses between groups.

## Who asks whom

- In 16 verses the question goes toward God such as "How long will you hide your face from me?" in the thirteenth psalm because the speaker feels unseen and wants the hiding to end.
- In 16 verses the question goes the other way and is put to people by God or in the voice of God or by Wisdom or by Jesus such as "How long will this people despise me?" and "How long will you judge unjustly" and "How long will I bear with you?" because the speaker is waiting for people to change.
- The other 20 verses are people asking people or laments with no clear listener or heavenly beings asking one another or plain statements such as the line in the seventy-fourth psalm that says nobody among the people "knows how long".

The two main groups are the same size in this sorting which means the Bible records the divine side waiting on people about as often as the Bible records people waiting on God.

## Does anyone receive a length of time

Nine psalms hold a "How long?" that is prayed to God and not one of the nine psalms records a length of time in reply. A king once asks a servant how long a journey will take and the servant sets a time because the question was a practical one. Two visions in the book of Daniel answer with numbers that readers have argued over ever since. A prophet who asks how long a calling will last is answered with an event and not with a date. A crowd of the dead in the last book of the Bible is told to "rest yet for a while". An angel who asks on behalf of a ruined city receives "kind and comforting words" and the text gives no number with the comfort.

The plain result is that a person in the Bible who asks God "How long?" out of pain is almost never handed a date.

## What follows the question

The words right after the question show what the question is for. In the psalms the next words are usually a request for something to begin now such as "Rescue my soul" and "Satisfy us in the morning with your loving kindness" because the speaker wants the change to start and is not asking for a calendar. When the question is put to people the next words are usually an instruction for something to begin now. The psalm that asks judges "How long will you judge unjustly" goes on to say "Rescue the weak and needy." and Jesus follows "How long will I bear with you?" with "Bring him here to me." and then heals the suffering person who is brought.

Both directions follow the same shape which is a cry about the waiting and then a plain statement of what should start today.

## What the finding could mean

The record does not hide how the waiting can end. The thirteenth psalm closes with "But I trust in your loving kindness." while the seventy-fourth psalm closes with the speaker still pleading and no relief in sight which means the Bible keeps both outcomes on the page and does not promise that every wait ends inside the prayer.

A reader who is waiting for food or a safe place to sleep or one person who truly knows the reader's heart may recognize both questions at once. The person in need is asking "How long?" and the same book is asking the people who hold the bread and the keys "How long will you judge unjustly" and telling those people to "Rescue the weak and needy." so part of the waiting in the first question is waiting on human beings who have not yet answered the second question. One prophet's book even says that God "will wait" in order to be gracious and then says "Blessed are all those who wait for him." which pictures both sides waiting for the other to move.

The record offers two things a person can do. A person who is waiting can say "How long?" aloud and add one concrete request for today the way the psalms do because a request names what help would look like and gives a listener something to act on. Saying the request aloud is no guarantee because one psalm ends with the plea unanswered. A person who holds something another person needs such as a spare meal or a spare hour to listen can hear "How long?" as a question aimed at the holder too and can answer with an act today if the holder has enough to spare because an act is the one kind of answer that shortens the wait.

## Two quick points

- In the World English Bible the words "how long" go toward God in 16 verses and go toward people from the divine side in 16 verses and no psalm that prays the question receives a length of time in reply.
- The words after "How long?" are usually a request or an instruction for something to start today so a person who is waiting can add a concrete request and a person with something to spare can answer the question with an act.
