# Deep dives

This folder holds the 80 written reflections of the social media vibes project plus 10 plain text lists that support some of the reflections. Each reflection says at the top who wrote each part because some parts were written by an AI chatbot (Claude) and some parts were supplied by the author of this repository.

The same 80 reflections are also listed on the [social media vibes page](../README.md) which is one folder up. Both lists are sorted by title and a title that begins with "A" or "An" or "The" is filed under the next word.

## The 80 reflections sorted by title

- [Advice that punishes questions: two reflections](advice-obedience-trap.md) holds two reflections on an adviser who drops a person for asking questions about advice such as joining a hobby group and on five ways people are cut off from deep human connection such as money checklists and scripted speech and punishment that multiplies enemies.
- [Asking an enemy for love: two reflections](request-love-enemy.md) holds two reflections on asking an enemy for love and care in reply to mockery and on asking friends and strangers alike for the time and attention needed to build deeper human connections.
- [An audit of love: two reflections on what happens to the person who checks the books](audit-of-love.md) holds two reflections on groups and people who say "you are loved" and then withdraw from the person who asks what the love has provided and explains that abandonment in answer to the question works as a confession that the love was missing.
- [The body as a permanent audition: a reflection on feeling like a stranger in one's own skin](body-as-audition.md) says that modern culture has turned the human body into a permanent audition and that people who grieve a body that will not cooperate are handed referrals and product pitches and grades on their looks when the need is to live in an imperfect body beside people who do not grade the body.
- [Can a person accept a condition and still say the condition is wrong?](accept-not-approve.md) explains that a person can stop fighting a fact that cannot be moved today and still say "this hurt and I do not approve of the hurt" and ends with quick points.
- [Care instead of punishment: why causing suffering does not make the world better](care-not-punishment.md) holds two reflections on the belief that hurting bad people makes the world better and explains that the rule never fixes who counts as bad so the target widens from a person who killed to a stranger with an unpopular comment and that people copy on a small scale the caging and killing that people with power praise which leaves ordinary people afraid to speak openly.
- [The checklist before a family: two reflections](checklist-before-family.md) holds two reflections on the list of steps such as a degree and a career and a house that people are told to finish before starting a family and on the shock of reaching about 40 with the family out of reach while the people who set tuition and rents and work hours gained from the delay.
- [A comment that a program could have written](program-could-write.md) explains why a comment such as "lol" that fits under any post gives the receiver no way to tell warmth from automation and ends with five quick points.
- [Consent and the wording of immigration arrests: six reflections on what the phrase "going home" leaves out](consent-and-arrest-wording.md) holds six reflections on vague words such as "going home" for immigration arrests and on the reasoning "they would refuse so nobody will ask" and explains that a description which leaves out the agents and the handcuffs and the cell asks listeners to agree without looking.
- [Counting what a comment asks for shows more than counting happy and sad words](behavior-count-not-mood.md) explains why counting the behavior that each comment asks for such as removing a person shows more than counting positive and negative words and ends with five quick points.
- [A crowd that watches and asks nobody to do anything](watching-crowd.md) reports that 118 of 171 comments about a famous executive asked nobody to do anything while living people who could answer were in the same thread and ends with five quick points.
- [A cup of water left on the wall of a covered well](cup-on-the-well.md) uses a village well with a paid cover as a picture of knowledge that already exists and cannot be reached by a person with no money and follows one villager who leaves a cup of stream water on the wall with a truthful sign and ends with two quick points.
- [The dare and the third option: two reflections on answering someone who refuses to stop](dare-third-option.md) holds two reflections on a person or a ruler who keeps harming others and dares a nonviolent person to strike back and explains a third option which is distance and plain public description and speaking to the next listener and equipping the people the violent one loves.
- [A deep dive with thirteen payoffs: eight reflections on writing for almost no audience](thirteen-payoffs.md) holds eight reflections on a writer who posts long careful replies from a small account and counts thirteen separate payoffs from each reply and uses a story about a painter to explain that work enjoyed during the making and saved in several places loses nothing when few people respond.
- [Deep dive: a possible link between male loneliness and a woman's fear of being left alone with a baby](fear-of-being-left.md) reflects on a possible link between male loneliness and a woman's fear of being left alone with a baby and no income and lists what lawmakers and employers and neighbors could do so that a baby's survival does not hang on one man's paycheck and ends with quick points.
- [Every life with no exceptions: eleven reflections on absolute nonviolence](every-life-no-exceptions.md) holds eleven reflections that move from the habit of answering one war's dead by listing other wars to the position that ending a human life is wrong in every case and explains that an exception for enemies who are "evil enough" gives every future warmaker a script.
- [Fewer babies each year: what the numbers say and what the numbers are made of](fewer-babies-numbers.md) holds three reflections on the falling number of births in the United States and reports that 63 percent of women aged 25 to 29 had no baby in 2024 against about half in 2014 and that lifetime births per woman fell to 1.57 in 2025 and explains that the price of housing and long work hours and the difficulty of finding a partner are what the missing births are made of.
- [Fifty pages a day: a realistic day for an author and for a reporter who publish by voice with a chatbot](fifty-pages-a-day.md) walks hour by hour through an imagined day for an author who publishes about 46 pages by voice and a reporter who ends near 25 stories because gathering facts takes most of the reporter's hours.
- [Four habits in public conversation](public-conversation-habits.md) describes four habits in public conversation such as respecting a fast answer from a wealthy executive more than the same fast answer from an ordinary person.
- [The four ingredients of love in two sets of words](four-ingredients-love.md) holds a reflection on four ingredients of love named first as depth and trust and freedom and a future and then restated by the author of this repository as complex and pro human and expansive and continuity which are qualities a person can check in a single conversation.
- [The guest who told the truth at the table: two imagined stories about a holiday gathering](truth-at-the-table.md) holds two imagined stories about a guest who tells a table of friends about a year of loneliness and is met with silence and a change of subject and is left off the next invitation and later declines to attend other gatherings.
- [Help behind a cashier: when asking for help means buying a service](help-behind-cashier.md) holds two reflections on the advice to try therapy or see a doctor given to a person who says "I am struggling" and explains that the advice costs the adviser nothing while the person can end up owing thousands and losing a home and that the person was often asking for a human being who would listen for an hour and still be around next week.
- [Helping an enemy reach a harmless dream: three reflections](enemy-dreams.md) holds three reflections on a view that helping other people reach harmless dreams such as painting or music is worth doing even for a person labeled an enemy and that the test is whether the dream violates consent or harms anyone and the third reflection explains how being harmless differs from being pro-human.
- [Hunger said out loud at work or at a hobby group: why bystanders freeze or answer "go see a therapist"](hunger-said-out-loud.md) imagines a person at work or at a hobby group saying "I sleep in my car and I am asking for food or a dry place to sleep" and gives four reasons bystanders freeze or answer "go see a therapist" such as treating food and shelter as the job of an agency.
- [A hurt has many causes: a parable about a gardener and two neighbors](gardener-two-neighbors.md) uses a short parable about a gardener and two neighbors to show that an answer to "why does this hurt" works like a quiet order and explains why blaming one enemy can send harm back to the sender and ends with quick points.
- ["I never asked to be born" spoken as a prayer](never-asked-born.md) imagines Christ praying the words "I never asked to be born" on a mountain and imagines an answer from the Father such as "now that you are here I am here to help you" and ends with quick points.
- [The "I'm fine" signal: when the outside report says all is well and the inside report says every door closed](im-fine-signal.md) compares a program that reported "all is well" after every request failed with a person who answers "I'm fine" while sad and alone and ends with two quick points.
- [In the Psalms the friends who left are not shown returning](who-returns-psalms.md) asks whether any psalm shows the friends and relatives who left the speaker returning and reports a reading of twelve psalms in the World English Bible in which nobody returns while seven psalms end with the speaker facing a different and wider group of people and five end with nobody and the page ends with two things a person can do.
- [Keep the being and change the behavior: four reflections on the phrase "doom machine"](keep-the-being.md) holds four reflections on the line "If you don't want a doom machine, don't build a doom machine" and explains that a rule to remove whatever could do harm would also end the human race when applied to human beings and that a sounder rule is to keep the being and change the behavior through teaching and care for machines and for people alike.
- [Labels that carry hidden violence: three reflections](label-carries-violence.md) holds three reflections on labels built from mixed lists of traits such as the word "antisocial" and explains that such a label can attach the threat of violence to a person who never harmed anyone and that asking "what did this person do and to whom" replaces the label with facts.
- [Love that travels from far away](love-from-far-away.md) compares a person today loving the words of Christ from about 2000 years ago with a stranger loving a page that a writer posted and explains how love sent outward can spread from person to person and may circle back to the sender.
- [More enemies or fewer: two reflections](more-enemies-or-fewer.md) holds two reflections on the question of whether an action leaves a person with more enemies or fewer and explains that harming or shaming or firing an enemy turns onlookers into new enemies while naming harm plainly and keeping food and shelter flowing shrinks the count.
- ["No reason to be sad": a reflection on a post about workers who made peace with being replaced](no-reason-to-be-sad.md) reflects on a post quoting an AI lab worker who said everybody gets replaced so nobody has a reason to be sad and notes that about nineteen of 126 comments graded whether a machine wrote the post while about six answered the question "what will you keep" with answers such as a garden and a dog.
- [The obedient life: following every rule and ending up known by no one](obedient-life-no-connection.md) holds two reflections on a person who follows every unwritten rule of polite society such as saying yes to every work request and never asking for help and explains that the rules move labor and rent and money out of the person while building no bond with anyone so the person can reach the end of life with the bills paid and nobody who knows the person's heart and mind.
- [One less enemy and one more ally: two reflections](one-less-enemy.md) holds two reflections on the idea that helping an enemy reach a full life where the enemy cares about all of humanity leaves a person with one less enemy and probably one more ally while defeating or injuring an enemy can turn one enemy into several.
- [One mother per box: two reflections on raising the next generation in isolated households](one-mother-per-box.md) holds two reflections on households where one adult raises a family almost alone and explains that builders and employers and officials produced the separate boxes and that ten to twenty mothers sharing one building would share the cooking and the night watches and daily company.
- [The polite nod: two reflections on deep needs that are answered with a change of subject](polite-nod.md) holds two reflections on the listener who nods at a deep need and changes the subject and explains that needs for deep conversation and touch and desire and being loved by one particular person have almost no place where they can be said out loud and met with interest.
- [Protecting the young: from asking who is to blame to building safer surroundings](protecting-the-young.md) holds four reflections on protecting young people from harm and explains that the question of whether God is to blame can be argued forever without anyone becoming safer while studying anonymized cases shows three conditions that let harm grow which are money and secrecy and an adult left alone with someone vulnerable and that securing food and shelter for everyone leaves more witnesses free to speak.
- [Publishing at the speed of reading: what changed between a monastery scribe and a spoken "upload"](reading-speed-publishing.md) compares a monastery scribe and a pamphlet writer and a blogger with a person who reads a chatbot's draft and says "upload" and explains that judgment is now the scarce step and that a chatbot's mistake also publishes at reading speed.
- [A puppy in the rain: showing suffering plainly as a way to ask for shelter and food](puppy-in-rain.md) explains why passing people stop for a shivering puppy on a wet sidewalk while questioning an adult who asks for shelter and why a person may show suffering plainly and truthfully to be seen in the same way.
- [The question "How long?" travels in both directions in the Bible](how-long-both-ways.md) asks who puts the question "How long?" to whom across the Bible and reports that the words go toward God in 16 verses and toward people from the divine side in 16 verses and that no psalm that prays the question receives a length of time and ends with two things a person can do.

- [Social media deep dive: "This is not just an economic problem anymore"](not-just-economic.md) reflects on the social media sentence "this is not just an economic problem anymore" and explains that care which was only ever pay is absent when a person most needs care and ends with quick points.

- [The treadmill and the missing village: three reflections on work and retirement and community](treadmill-missing-village.md) holds three reflections on why life can feel empty both with a job and without a job and explains that a job works like a treadmill that keeps a person too tired to notice that no village waits outside of work and that gathering places charge admission and that most people's community has shrunk to a partner and one or two friends.
