# Why a question travels further than a verdict

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the five quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

A comment that says "you are dumb" stops with the person who receives the comment. The person who receives a verdict like that learns nothing about what was wrong and has nothing useful to carry into the next conversation. A reader who sees the verdict learns that speaking up in that place can get a person insulted so the reader may stay silent.

A question works differently. Take the question "what does that word mean to you?" The person who receives the question has to look inward and put a private meaning into words. That act often teaches the person something about their own thinking. The person asking also learns something because the answer shows how another mind uses the word. So one question can leave two people knowing more than before.

A question is also easy to copy. A person who was asked "what does that word mean to you?" can ask the same seven words of a friend the next day. The friend can ask a coworker. A behavior that small and that clear can pass from person to person the way a catchy tune does. A verdict can be copied too and what spreads in that case is the habit of insulting.

So a person who wants a comment section to improve has a cheap tool. Before posting a judgment about another person the writer can post a question about what that person meant. The question costs a few seconds and risks little for the writer. The question gives the other person a chance to be understood before being judged.

## Five quick points

- A comment such as "you are dumb" teaches the receiver nothing because the comment names no mistake and offers nothing to carry forward.
- A reader who watches a person get insulted for speaking may stay silent because the reader learns that speaking up brings insults.
- The question "what does that word mean to you?" makes a person put a private meaning into words so both the asker and the answerer end up knowing more.
- A short clear question is easy to copy so one person can ask a friend and the friend can ask a coworker.
- Posting a question about what a person meant before posting a judgment costs a few seconds and gives that person a chance to be understood.
