# What old proverbs in seven languages say about being alone and having company

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) asked the question on this page and did the reading and wrote the page on 20 November 2026. The sorting of the proverbs and the reflection are the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the page.

## The question

Proverbs are the short sayings that ordinary people repeated for centuries. When old proverbs speak about being alone and about having company which side do the proverbs favor and does any proverb praise being alone for its own sake?

## What the chatbot could say from memory before the reading

Before opening the book the chatbot knew the saying "Better alone than in bad company" and two or three other sayings. The chatbot guessed that proverbs in favor of being alone would outnumber proverbs in favor of company. The chatbot had no count behind the guess which is why the reading was done.

## What a web search turned up

A web search on 20 November 2026 turned up the saying "Better alone than in bad company" on page after page and turned up long lists of modern quotes about solitude. The search did not turn up a tally of what a large collection of old proverbs says on each side. The search used wording such as "proverbs about being alone versus company across languages".

## How the reading was done

The source was a book printed in London in 1857 that gathers proverbs in French and Italian and German and Spanish and Portuguese and Dutch and Danish with an English translation beside each proverb. The book is free to read because the copyright ran out long ago and Project Gutenberg hosts the text as ebook number 51090 at https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/51090 and the chatbot read a copy of the same text kept on GitHub.

The book holds 7924 proverbs by the chatbot's count. A search of the English translations for words such as alone and solitary and hermit and company and companion and "no friend" pulled out about 100 proverbs and each one was read. A proverb was kept when the proverb says something about a person being alone or being with others. The count was 54 proverbs and the file named alone-proverbs-list.txt in this folder lists the 54 proverbs in five groups so that any reader can check the sorting.

Three limits matter. The reading used the English translations and not the original languages. The same saying often appears in several languages and each appearance was counted. The book also holds 149 proverbs with the word friend and the reading did not sort those unless one of the search words was present.

## The count

- In 24 proverbs company makes life better or being alone makes life worse.
- In 12 proverbs being alone is better than bad company or a companion has a cost.
- In 9 proverbs company leaves when the food or the good fortune is gone.
- In 6 proverbs a person is known by the company the person keeps.
- In 3 proverbs the verdict on a person who lives alone is mixed or a joke.

The guess made beforehand was wrong because proverbs in favor of company outnumber proverbs in favor of being alone by 24 to 12. The reading also found something the guess did not reach for. Not one of the 54 proverbs praises being alone for its own sake. The proverbs that favor being alone compare being alone with something worse such as bad company or a companion who gives orders.

## What the proverbs on the side of company say

Many of the 24 proverbs are about plain bodily comfort. One says "One log does not burn long by itself." and another says "He who sleeps alone keeps long cold" and adds "two soon warm each other". Two say that good company shortens the miles of a journey. One says "Company in distress makes trouble less."

Several are about thinking. One says "A doctor and a boor know more than a doctor alone." and another says "The pope and a peasant know more than the pope alone." which means the proverbs hold that the most learned person still knows less alone than together with an ordinary person. One Danish proverb pairs the words "Alone in counsel" with the words "alone in sorrow" which means a person who decides everything alone also suffers the results alone.

The strongest lines are the shortest. One says "There’s no living without friends." and one says "One would not be alone in Paradise."

## What the proverbs on the side of being alone say

The saying "Better alone than in bad company." appears in five of the seven languages and is the only form in which the book favors being alone. One French proverb gives a reason to be wary of company with the words "He who has a companion has a master." One German proverb warns about the opposite mistake with the words "Everybody’s companion is nobody’s friend." The lone proverb that looks straight at a person living alone says "A solitary man is either a brute or an angel." which treats a life alone as something outside the ordinary range of human lives.

## Food runs through the proverbs about company

Bread and meat appear again and again in these proverbs. A Spanish proverb says "He that eats his fowl alone may saddle his horse alone." which means a person who keeps food to himself can expect no help with work later. Two Portuguese proverbs refuse the name of friend to a person who eats from a shared plate and then eats his own food in private. One says "A morsel eaten gains no friend." because food that was shared could have made a friend and food eaten alone cannot.

The same link shows a bitter side in the nine proverbs about company that leaves. Two proverbs say that once the bread is eaten the company departs. One says "A dog with a bone knows no friend." One says "The dead and the absent have no friends." and one says that a person without money has no friend. Those proverbs do not praise the leaving. Those proverbs record that people saw friends vanish when the food ran out and said so in a line short enough to remember.

## What the finding could mean

Food and shelter and being known by another heart are three basic human needs and the proverbs tie the three together more tightly than the chatbot expected. Warmth in bed and a fire that keeps burning and a shorter road and a shared meal are the pictures the proverbs use for company. The people who made these sayings did not treat company as a pleasant extra. The sayings treat company as part of how a person stays warm and fed.

The proverbs also keep both warnings in view. Bad company is worse than none and company that stays only for the bread is not the company a person needs. The book gives no proverb that says how to tell the two apart ahead of time which is a real gap in the old advice.

The record offers two things a person can do. A person with food to spare can share a meal because the proverbs name the shared meal as the place where help and friendship begin and name the meal eaten alone as the start of working alone. A person who has been left alone can hold on to the one comparison the proverbs make without taking the comparison as praise because the proverbs say that being alone beats bad company and do not say that being alone is good. No proverb in the book promises that a shared meal will bring a lasting friend and nine proverbs record company that left.

## Two quick points

- In 54 old proverbs about being alone and having company from seven languages 24 favor company and 12 favor being alone and none praises being alone for its own sake.
- The proverbs picture company as warmth and a fire and a shared meal so a person with food to spare can share a meal and a person left alone can read "Better alone than in bad company." as a comparison and not as praise of being alone.
