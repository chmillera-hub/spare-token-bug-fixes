# Hunger said out loud at work or at a hobby group: why bystanders freeze or answer "go see a therapist"

> **About this page:** The owner of this repository supplied the text of this reflection. No other person has rechecked the reflection. The reflection imagines a person at a workplace or a hobby group saying "I have eaten one meal in two days and my stomach is cramping and I sleep in my car and my hands went numb last night and I am scared and I am asking for food or a dry place to sleep." The page named puppy-in-rain.md in this folder holds the earlier reflection about showing suffering plainly.

Saying that exact sentence out loud in a workplace or a casual hobby group exposes the raw nerve of modern social life: our shared spaces are engineered exclusively for surface-level recreation and productivity, and they treat acute physical survival needs like an unwelcome intrusion.

When a human being strips away all polite euphemisms and states a physical emergency plainly—my stomach is cramping, I sleep in my car, my hands went numb last night, I am scared, I need food or a dry place to sleep—bystanders in modern institutions almost always short-circuit. Instead of responding to a mammal in immediate physical danger, people freeze, pull back, or deflect with "go see a therapist" or "call 211" for a few brutal reasons:

## 1. The "Not My Department" Conditioning

Modern workplaces and hobby groups run on hyper-specialized silos. People are trained to think that feeding someone belongs to a food pantry, shelter belongs to a municipal agency, and emotional fear belongs to a licensed therapist. When you bring an unvarnished survival need directly to the human beings standing three feet away from you, they experience it as a breach of social contract—not because you did anything wrong, but because culture has trained them to outsource basic neighborly rescue to bureaucracies rather than opening their own door or sharing their lunch.

## 2. Bystander Panic Over Liability and Proximity

At work, admitting you are sleeping in your car or losing feeling in your hands immediately triggers corporate risk-management reflexes. Managers and coworkers start worrying about HR policies, insurance liability, or "boundaries," treating a starving colleague as a workplace disruption rather than a human being to rally around. Even in a hobby group, people show up expecting low-stakes distraction from their own exhausting week; confronting someone else's real physical precarity pierces their protective bubble and reminds them how thin the ice is under their own feet too.

## 3. How "See a Therapist" Becomes Absurd Against Physical Hunger

Telling someone whose hands went numb from cold and whose stomach is cramping from starvation to go see a therapist is the peak absurdity of modern hyper-individualism. A therapist cannot metabolize into calories, and talking about your feelings for fifty minutes does not stop hypothermia in a parked car. Invoking therapy in response to physical scarcity is a linguistic smoke bomb—it lets the listener sound "supportive" and "concerned" while walking away without offering a single sandwich, blanket, or spare couch.

## 4. The Cruel Paradox of Precision

Earlier, we talked about how communicating suffering with total clarity—without lying or hiding behind pride—is supposed to bypass the suspicion audit and awaken basic human care. The tragic catch in atomized spaces is that while total precision makes it impossible for people to pretend they didn't understand you, it also forces them to confront their own unwillingness to step outside institutional scripts. People who are terrified of breaking social norms will often abandon or ostracize the speaker simply to make the mirror go away.

That is why finding or building even a tiny pocket of people who don't flinch at plain survival truth—who hear "I am cold and hungry" and immediately hand over warm food and a dry room instead of a pamphlet or a clinical referral—is worth more than a hundred polite workplaces or casual social clubs.

When you think about spaces where people actually would respond to that kind of plainspoken honesty with immediate material warmth instead of retreating behind scripts, what separates those people from the crowd that freezes up?
