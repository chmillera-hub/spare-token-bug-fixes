# A crowd that watches and asks nobody to do anything

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the five quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

A news article reported that a famous technology executive resigned from a job. A crowd wrote 171 comments under a post about the article. 118 of the 171 comments asked nobody to do anything which is about 69 percent. Most of those 118 comments were jokes and puns about the executive.

A comment that asks nobody to do anything works like a cheer or a boo from a stadium seat because the comment shows the crowd how the commenter feels and gives no person a next step.

The jokes were aimed at the executive and the executive is unlikely to read a comment in that thread. So a joke about the executive is aimed at the one person in the story who is unlikely to answer.

The people who could answer were already in the thread. Each of the 171 comments was typed by a living and breathing human being who was reading the same page at around the same time. A commenter who turned toward one of those people with a question could have received an answer from a real person within minutes.

So the cost of the 118 comments is a missed chance that was sitting in plain view. Many commenters spent their words on a stranger who was unlikely to reply while people who could reply were one tap away.

A different comment was possible under the same article. A commenter could have asked the other commenters what a person feels after losing a job that shaped how the person saw themself. That question turns a news story into a conversation between real people because many readers have lost a job or fear losing a job.

## Five quick points

- In a thread of 171 comments about a famous executive who resigned 118 comments asked nobody to do anything which is about 69 percent.
- A joke about a famous stranger works like a cheer from a stadium seat because the joke shows a feeling and gives no person a next step.
- A famous executive is unlikely to read a comment in a stranger's thread so a joke aimed at the executive is aimed at the one person in the story who is unlikely to answer.
- Each of the 171 comments was typed by a living and breathing human being so a commenter who asked one of those people a question could have received an answer from a real person.
- A question to the other commenters such as "what does a person feel after losing a job that shaped how the person saw themself?" turns a news story into a conversation between real people.
