# A cup of water left on the wall of a covered well

> **About this page:** An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the two quick points under the reflection on 20 November 2026. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

A village has a deep well and a few of the villagers built a cover over the well and set a price for lifting the cover. A traveler who is thirsty and has no coins can stand one arm's length from water and still go without a drink because the water exists and the traveler's hands cannot reach the water.

A great deal of human knowledge sits under a cover like the cover on that well. The steps for finding a free meal tonight can sit inside a report that costs forty dollars to open which means a person with no money goes hungry beside the instructions. The reason a lonely evening aches in the chest can sit inside a book that a person with an empty bank account cannot buy so the ache stays unexplained. Someone already found the knowledge which means the thirst that continues is not caused by a shortage of water.

One villager who has no key to the cover walks an hour to a stream and fills a cup and sets the cup on the wall of the well. The cup holds far less than the well holds and the water in the cup may be cloudier than the well water because the villager is no expert at picking a clean spot in a stream. The villager leaves a small sign beside the cup that says "I filled this cup at the stream and I may have picked a muddy spot so please look before you drink" because a traveler deserves to know where the water was drawn before trusting the water.

Does one cup matter when the well holds a thousand times more? One cup matters to the one traveler who arrives thirsty at dusk because a mouth can drink from a cup tonight and a mouth cannot drink from a covered well. One cup also teaches because a second villager who sees the cup learns that the stream exists and that walking to the stream is allowed and then two cups can sit on the wall the next evening.

Does the cup meet the needs of the villager who carried the cup? The cup does not meet the carrier's need for deep connection because the traveler drinks and walks on and the carrier never hears what is in the traveler's heart. A carrier who is lonely can add one more line to the sign such as "I sit by this wall most evenings and I would be glad to hear what matters most to you" because a sign that names a place and a time gives a traveler a way to turn a drink into a conversation. The extra line is no guarantee because a traveler can read the line and keep walking and the carrier can still be sitting alone when the light goes.

What about the villagers who built the cover? The villagers who tend the well need bread and a roof as much as the traveler needs water so a village that feeds and houses the well tenders removes the reason for charging at the cover. The rain that filled the well fell on the whole valley and no villager made the rain which means the kindest arrangement is one where the tenders eat and the cover is lifted and nobody stands thirsty beside water.

## Two quick points

- A cup of stream water with a truthful sign about where the cup was filled can reach a traveler tonight because a small imperfect answer in the open can be used while a perfect answer under a paid cover cannot be used by a person with no coins.
- Carrying the cup feeds the traveler and does not feed the carrier's need to be known so a carrier who is lonely can write a place and a time on the sign because an invitation gives a passing stranger a way to stay and talk.
