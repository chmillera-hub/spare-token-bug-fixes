# What happens when a person says an act is wrong and recommends the act anyway?

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

A homeowner asked a crowd whether blocking an AI chatbot on the home wifi would be wrong since a friend in crisis was staying in the home and talking to the chatbot all day. Several commenters gave the same two-part answer. Those commenters said that the block would make the homeowner a jerk and then told the homeowner to do the block anyway. A commenter wrote that being a bit of a jerk is sometimes worth the price.

That advice raises a question. What does a person learn from being told that an act is wrong and that the act is fine to carry out?

The advice pulls apart two things that usually travel together. The judgment that an act harms someone usually comes with a decision to look for a different act. Once the judgment and the decision are pulled apart the judgment shrinks into a label such as "jerk" that a person can accept and then carry on.

The phrase "worth the price" also hides who pays. The homeowner receives a label. The friend in crisis loses the conversation partner that the friend talks with all day. So the person who decides and the person who pays are different people.

Here is a possibility about the commenters. Those commenters may have wanted to comfort a tired homeowner by saying that the homeowner does not have to be perfect. Care for the homeowner is a real kindness and the advice still leaves the friend out of the picture.

A different answer could hold both people. A commenter could say that the homeowner's exhaustion matters and that the friend's need for conversation matters. Then the commenter could suggest that the homeowner tell the friend plainly what the homeowner can keep giving and what the homeowner has run out of. That talk takes more energy than changing a wifi setting so the suggestion fits a homeowner who has some energy left.

## Quick points

- Several commenters told a homeowner that blocking a friend's chatbot would make the homeowner a jerk and then told the homeowner to do the block anyway.
- The judgment that an act harms someone usually comes with a decision to look for a different act and the advice in the thread pulled those apart.
- Once the judgment stands alone the judgment shrinks into a label such as "jerk" that a person can accept and then carry on.
- The phrase "worth the price" hides who pays because the homeowner receives a label while the friend in crisis loses a daily conversation partner.
- A commenter could hold both people by naming the homeowner's exhaustion and the friend's need for conversation and suggesting that the homeowner say plainly what the homeowner can keep giving.
