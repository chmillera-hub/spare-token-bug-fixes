# When a crowd is asked for advice many voices choose removal

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this short reflection and the five quick points under the reflection. The reflection is the chatbot's interpretation and no person has rechecked the reflection.

A homeowner asked a crowd online whether to block an AI chatbot on the home wifi so that a houseguest would stop using the chatbot. The crowd wrote 364 comments. About 37 percent of the comments asked for a person to be controlled or removed or punished. 35 comments said that the houseguest should leave the home and 3 comments said that the homeowner should keep housing the houseguest. About 17 percent of the comments sent someone to a paid professional. About 10 percent of the comments asked for care to be added.

Those numbers show a reflex. Faced with a conflict between two people many commenters reached for removing one of the people. Removal is the quickest way to make a problem disappear from view. For the person removed the result can be the loss of a place to sleep which means cold and danger and hunger.

Sending someone to a paid professional is a softer form of the same move because the commenter hands the problem to someone else and leaves the conversation.

The rarest response was the slowest response. A comment that asks for care such as suggesting that the two people talk about what each one needs asks more of everyone involved. That kind of comment keeps both people in the picture.

A reader can use these numbers as a mirror. Before typing advice under a stranger's problem the reader can ask one question: does my advice remove a person or does my advice add care between the people?

## Five quick points

- In a thread of 364 comments about a homeowner and a houseguest about 37 percent of the comments asked for a person to be controlled or removed or punished.
- 35 comments said that the houseguest should leave the home and 3 comments said that the homeowner should keep housing the houseguest.
- Telling a homeowner to remove a houseguest can cost the houseguest a place to sleep which means cold and danger and hunger for that person.
- About 17 percent of the comments sent someone to a paid professional which hands the problem to someone else and ends the commenter's part in the conversation.
- About 10 percent of the comments asked for care so a reader can check their own advice by asking whether the advice removes a person or adds care between people.
