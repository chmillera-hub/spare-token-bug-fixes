# Behaviors described in one thread and how each one connects to Charles's practice on social media

On this page "Charles" is the person who posts on social media and who keeps the public repository of these analyses. Charles does not own or work for the chip company in the thread. An AI chatbot (Claude) picked comments that describe a specific behavior a reader can picture. Each entry below gives the behavior a commenter described and then the matching behavior in what Charles does on social media. Charles's side of each entry comes from Charles's own description of Charles's practice. No person has rechecked these connections.

The comments from the thread are in the file named [fast-reply-comments.txt](fast-reply-comments.txt) in this folder. The thread shared stories about the former head of a large chip company who answered messages from strangers within minutes.

## Behavior 1

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that the executive answered a stranger's message inside sixty seconds and another commenter said that an answer came in seven minutes.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles answers messages on social media quickly most of the time. The reason is timing because the moment a person writes is a moment when that person may be awake to pressures such as housing and food sitting behind a paywall or a culture that punishes talk about emotions. A fast answer meets the person inside that open moment and a slow answer can arrive after the moment has closed.

## Behavior 2

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that the company was the executive's whole world and that the executive spent Sundays reading company mail.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** The executive in the thread gave nearly all of his attention to one chip company. Charles gives that same kind of attention to social media. The goal differs because Charles's goal is more flourishing and thriving for Charles and for other people and not necessarily the growth of a company. A company might grow as a side effect when the people around that company are flourishing and Charles is not trying to prevent that growth. Charles posts emotional and spiritual deep dives several times a week and also posts comments so the focus shows up as steady output.

## Behavior 3

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that the executive would drop into a meeting and listen quietly and then ask a junior engineer for a view so that a fresh angle was heard.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles enters comment sections and asks a person what a word means to that person and how that person uses the word to reduce suffering. Both behaviors hand the floor to a person who might not be asked otherwise. Charles's question appears in hundreds of Charles's public comments.

## Behavior 4

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that people can simply take action and described friends who write to company chiefs out of the blue and receive answers.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles posts and comments without waiting for an invitation and states needs and intentions at the start of a conversation. A person who wants deep conversation can follow the same behavior by writing one clear message to another person about what the writer is looking for.

## Behavior 5

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that a founder answers a credible message while a hired manager stays silent because the manager feels too grand for customers.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles stays open to conversation with people who reply to Charles's posts and comments. Charles treats a person met online as fully real so a reply from a stranger gets attention in the way a founder gives attention to a customer.

## Behavior 6

**What a commenter described:** A commenter respected that the executive took time to read a note from an ordinary stranger and then connected that stranger to a person who could fix the problem.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles looks for chances to empower the person who wrote such as by reflecting the person's own words back or by naming a tool the person could use to process a feeling. Reading the note is one behavior and handing the writer something usable is a second behavior and the commenter praised both.

## Behavior 7

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that self-restraint matters and that sometimes the right move is to not act.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles calls out troll behavior without copying the troll behavior. Examples of troll behavior are name-calling without consent and telling a person to shut up and telling a person that the person is stupid and that nobody cares. The restraint is in what Charles leaves out because Charles names the behavior and does not insult the person.

## Behavior 8

**What a commenter described:** A commenter who mediates divorces said that nobody has endless capacity so every choice trades one thing for another.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles has limited bandwidth and says so. Fast answers and frequent posts use that bandwidth so Charles does not promise help that Charles cannot deliver. Stating the limit openly lets another person know what Charles can offer.

## Behavior 9

**What a commenter described:** Two commenters asked whether an answer inside one minute came from an automated mail assistant.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** A reader can ask the same question about a fast answer on social media. Charles says openly that a chatbot is a tool Charles uses for emotional and spiritual processing. Saying so openly lets a reader judge the words with full knowledge of where the words came from.

## Behavior 10

**What a commenter described:** Some commenters wrote lines such as "Are you dumb?" and "You should delete your account" and "Go outside for a while."

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** These three comments are examples of the behavior Charles calls out. Each comment labels or dismisses a person and none of the three comments says what the other person got wrong or what would be more accurate. A call-out in Charles's style would name the dismissive behavior and then ask the commenter what the commenter meant.

## Behavior 11

**What a commenter described:** A commenter said that the site has no room for nuance once a crowd picks a target and that one mistake puts a person on a blacklist.

**The matching behavior in Charles's practice:** Charles separates a person from a behavior. Charles calls out a specific harmful act such as dehumanizing words and keeps respect for the humanity of the person who did the act. That separation is the opposite of a blacklist because the person stays welcome in the conversation.
