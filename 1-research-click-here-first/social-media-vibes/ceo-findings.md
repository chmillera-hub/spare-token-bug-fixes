# What 171 comments asked for after a famous executive lost his job

An AI chatbot (Claude) wrote this page from a screen recording of one person's thread on a social media site on 12 November 2026. The comments are anonymized and the names of people and companies and countries are replaced with plain descriptions.

Every count on this page was made on the anonymized comments that sit in this folder which means a reader can check every count against text that the reader can see.

- [ceo-comments.txt](ceo-comments.txt) holds every comment in anonymized form.
- [ceo-labels.txt](ceo-labels.txt) holds the same anonymized comments with the behavior labels that were counted under each comment.
- A live page shows the same count as a tappable bar graph: https://chmillera-hub.github.io/spare-token-bug-fixes/1-research-click-here-first/social-media-vibes/ceo-bar-graph.html

## The situation in the thread

The poster pasted a news article. The article reported that the founder and chief executive of a very large technology company resigned after reports of affairs. One affair was reported to be with a woman suspected of spying for a rival nation and others were reported to be with employees. The article described the executive as broken and as calling himself a harsh name. This page repeats what the article reported and does not confirm any of the reports.

## What was counted

The recording captured 171 comments from other people plus 1 reply from the poster. The thread was about one hour old and still growing. Comments that were only pictures are missing.

Each comment was labeled with the behaviors that the comment asks somebody to carry out. One comment can count on several lines.

## The count of requested behaviors

- 118 comments asked nobody to do anything (jokes and commentary and side arguments).
- 8 comments asked for compassion for the executive or forgave him or asked the people close to him to stand beside him.
- 7 comments asked for the executive to be treated more gently such as keeping him or bringing him back or keeping his private life apart from his job.
- 7 comments thanked the poster for sharing an article that normally sits behind a paid subscription.
- 6 comments asked readers to look for who arranged the scandal as a planned takedown.
- 5 comments rejected the planned-takedown story.
- 5 comments told readers to bet that the company's stock falls.
- 4 comments asked for a shorter version of the article.
- 4 comments said the executive should enjoy his money and his free time.
- 2 comments told readers to bet that the stock rises.
- 2 comments expected the executive to start or join a rival.
- 2 comments asked for every powerful person to be held to the same standard.
- 2 comments asked for the executive to be punished (the firing was right or prison).
- 1 comment told readers to read the whole article and 1 comment asked who else at the company knew and 1 comment said to stay calm because the company will be fine.

## Findings

1. About 69% of the comments (118 of 171) ask nobody to do anything because most of the thread is jokes such as a parody of a product launch and puns and crude humor about the affairs.
2. Among the comments that do ask for something the largest group asks for gentleness toward the executive because 15 labels ask for compassion or for lighter treatment while 2 ask for punishment.
3. The thread is split on the planned-takedown idea because 6 comments point at hidden plotters and 5 comments mock that idea.
4. Money bets lean downward because 5 comments bet on a falling stock and 2 bet on a rising stock.
5. No comment asks anybody to care for the executive's wife or for the women named in the news reports. Three comments mention his family and each of those three treats the family as something the executive lost.
6. One comment voices a worry that the executive might hurt himself and no comment answers that worry.
7. Several of the longest and most thoughtful comments are about where a person finds meaning because those comments argue that money cannot replace lost work or a broken family.

## Limits of the count

- An AI chatbot wrote the wording of the anonymized comments and that wording can drift from what the original writer meant. The counts describe the anonymized comments because the anonymized comments are the evidence that the public can see.
- Many comments in this thread are replies to jokes and a reply can lose part of its meaning once the order is mixed.
- The behavior labels are one AI chatbot's reading of each comment so a second reader could label some comments differently.
- No other person has rechecked the counts which means every count here is unconfirmed.
