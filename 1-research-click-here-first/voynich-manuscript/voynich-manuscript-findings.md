# The Voynich manuscript: who made the unreadable book and how the book was built

The Voynich manuscript is a handwritten book from the early 1400s that nobody has been able to read because the writing uses an alphabet that matches no known language or code.
The first half of this page tells the human story of the book from published research and the second half reports spelling-pattern tests that an AI model (Claude) ran on 11 November 2026.
No other person has rerun the tests in the second half yet which means every number in the second half is a single unconfirmed analysis.

## The people and the place

- Laboratory workers at the University of Arizona dated samples of the pages in 2009 and the dates fell between 1404 and 1438 which means the animal skins come from a time before printed books existed in Europe.
- The pages are made of calfskin and the whole book used the skins of at least fourteen or fifteen calves which suggests a person with money paid for the book because calfskin was costly.
- One tiny drawing of a castle has wall tops shaped like the forked tail of a swallow and builders used that shape most often around the city of Verona in northern Italy which is one reason researchers guess the book was made in northern Italy.
- An archer drawn on the page for the star sign Sagittarius wears a hat in the style of the city of Florence which is a second clue that points toward northern Italy.
- The plant drawings resemble a family of plant books that copyists in northern Italy made in the 1400s for physicians and pharmacists which suggests the makers lived close to working healers.
- The names of the months beside the star-sign drawings are written in ordinary Latin letters with spellings that fit France or northwest Italy and a few margin words look like German which means the place of origin is a best guess and not a settled fact.
- A handwriting study published in 2020 sorted the writing into five different hands which means five different scribes probably shared the work and the book was probably a team project and not the doodling of one person.
- The five scribes divided the work by subject because several scribes shared the long plant section and one scribe wrote the star and sky pages and another scribe wrote the bathing pages and two scribes wrote the pages of short starred paragraphs.
- The scribes made very few corrections which suggests every scribe already knew the strange alphabet well before starting.

## What the pictures show

- The drawings show plants that mostly match no known species and star charts and star signs and hundreds of small naked women.
- Many of the women stand in pools of green or blue liquid joined by pipes and tubes which is why researchers call that part of the book the bathing section.
- One research team proposed in 2024 that part of the book concerns sex and conception and the health of women because a physician of the same period wrote about plants and women and baths and the sky and advised hiding such knowledge in secret letters.
- The colored paint was put on rather roughly and possibly by a later person which means the ink outlines are the more trustworthy record of what the first makers drew.

## How the book was built

- The book is made of large calfskin sheets folded in half so each folded sheet gives two leaves and four pages and the folded sheets are nested inside each other in bundles like a stack of folded newspapers.
- Gaps in the old page numbers suggest the book once had at least 272 pages and about 240 pages survive today which means some sheets went missing over the centuries.
- The cover is goatskin and the cover was added centuries later which means the present binding is not the binding the makers knew.
- In several bundles of the plant section the sheets of one scribe sit nested between the sheets of another scribe (for example one bundle runs scribe 1 then scribe 2 then scribe 1 four times then scribe 2 then scribe 1) which means each scribe wrote whole folded sheets and somebody later interleaved the sheets.
- A painted stream of water leaves a bath on the back of leaf 78 and continues on the front of leaf 81 and those two pages are far apart in the bound book but sit side by side on one unfolded sheet which suggests the painter worked on single sheets before anyone bound the book.
- A study published in October 2026 proposed that the book began as loose folded sheets that were never meant to be nested and that a person who could not read the writing later bound the sheets in the wrong order.
- The same study pointed to a large water stain in the upper margin of some pages as more evidence because the stain can show which sheets once lay on top of each other.

## The journey of the book

- The earliest probable owner was a physician and alchemist in the German city of Augsburg and the first confirmed owner was an alchemist in Prague in the 1600s.
- A book dealer bought the manuscript in 1912 from a Jesuit college near Rome and the manuscript carries the name of that book dealer today.
- The rare book library of Yale University has held the manuscript since 1969 and the library put pictures of every page online in 2020 so anyone can look at the pages.
- Code breakers who cracked military codes in both world wars tried to read the manuscript and failed.

## Could the words be meaningless

- A researcher showed in 2026 that a cipher worked by hand with tools from the 1400s can turn Latin or Italian into text that resembles the manuscript which means a real hidden message is possible.
- Volunteers who were asked to write pages of invented nonsense produced text with similar statistics because the volunteers kept copying and slightly changing words they had already written which means meaningless filler is also possible.
- Nobody knows yet which of the two possibilities is true.

## What the spelling-pattern tests on this page add

The tests below support three parts of the human story with a different kind of evidence which is the drift of spelling habits from page to page.

- The bound order of the pages is probably not the order in which the scribes wrote the pages.
- Each scribe probably wrote one folded sheet at a time from the first page of the sheet to the fourth page.
- Nearly every page ends exactly where a paragraph ends which could mean the scribes fitted the text to each page.

## Method

The main test gives a "next page" score to a pair of pages by comparing three-letter chunks in the last four lines of one page with the first four lines of another page because the spelling habits of a writer drift slowly and pages written back to back share habits.
The score ranks the true candidate page among all candidate pages by the same scribe in the same section so 0.50 means chance and 1.00 means the top match.

## Results

- The back of a leaf scored 0.67 as the continuation of the front of the same leaf across 68 leaves which shows the test detects real back-to-back writing.
- The facing page in the bound book scored 0.54 across 46 pairs which is near chance and suggests the bound order is not the writing order.
- The step from the back of the first leaf of a folded sheet to the front of the second leaf of the same folded sheet scored 0.79 across 26 sheets and the sheet partner was the top match in 9 of 26 cases where chance predicts about 2.
- The same sheet partner in the reverse direction scored 0.54 across 31 cases which suggests the scribe's hand moved one way through each folded sheet.
- A different sheet in the same bundle scored 0.51 across 83 comparisons which suggests the effect belongs to the single folded sheet and not to the bundle.
- The pattern held in three transcriptions (ZL and IT and RF) and under 36 measurement settings.
- 176 of 177 pages with running text end exactly at a paragraph end which means almost no paragraph runs across a page break.
- The 24 folded sheets by scribe 1 in the plant section differ along one main word-habit contrast and sheets in the same bundle sit close together on the contrast (about 1 chance in 800 under shuffling) which could mean the binder kept piles of sheets together.
- Sheets by different scribes matched in word mix at 0.30 and sheets by the same scribe matched at 0.53 while two halves of one sheet matched at 0.56 which argues against a separate cipher for every sheet and leaves a per-scribe difference open.

## What is not new and what might be new

A study published in October 2026 in the journal Digital Medievalist ("Singulion Structure and the Voynich Manuscript") had already reported that the two halves of a folded sheet resemble each other more than facing pages do.
The direction test and the same-bundle control and the paragraph-end count might be additions but the author of this folder could not open the full journal article to check.

## Limits

- The test tracks the order in which a scribe wrote and cannot show the order a reader was meant to follow.
- The order of the sheets remains unknown because sheet-to-sheet scores were only slightly above chance.
- Several versions of the analysis were tried before the ranking version which raises the risk of a lucky result.
