Prime Richness Checker

Type an equation and the checker reports how many primes the outputs contain compared with plain counting numbers of the same size.

Write powers with ^ such as n^2 and multiply with parentheses such as (n+2)(n+3).

Larger sizes take longer per test so the checker tests fewer outputs and the measured percentage gets rougher.

The predicted percentage applies at every size including 10^1000 because the percentage is built from which small primes can divide the outputs and that dividing pattern repeats forever. The prediction is unproven although computer counts have matched the prediction wherever people have checked.

The measured percentage compares the count of primes found with the count that chance predicts for ordinary numbers of the same sizes. A measured percentage close to the predicted percentage means the prediction held for the tested outputs.

Which small primes can divide the outputs

An equation whose outputs dodge many small primes lands above 0% because fewer outputs get knocked out. An equation whose outputs get divided by small primes more often than ordinary numbers lands below 0%. For ordinary numbers each prime p divides 1 of every p numbers.

How to read the percentage

0% means the same rate of primes as counting 1 2 3 4 5. +100% means twice as many primes and +500% means six times as many primes. −50% means half as many primes. −100% means the outputs stop being prime after at most a few early inputs.
