# Prime percentages grouped by type of equation

> **About this page:** One AI model (Claude) ran the computations and wrote this page in a single chat on 21 November 2026. No other person has rerun the work yet which means the results are unconfirmed by a human reviewer. Each percentage comes from a computer count and not from a written proof.

## What the list shows

The list sorts 293 equations into 16 groups by type of equation and ranks the equations inside each group from the most primes to the fewest primes. Each percentage compares the equation with plain counting numbers of the same size so +859% means 859% more primes than the number line and −100% means no primes.

The page [ranked-list.md](ranked-list.md) holds the same equations in one ranking from the highest percentage to the lowest percentage.

## The rules behind every line

An output with decimal places has the decimal places dropped with no rounding (1.99 becomes 1) unless the line names a different rounding rule.

"Bump the last digit" means the last digit of each output moves up to the nearest of 1 or 3 or 7 or 9 so 0 becomes 1 and 2 becomes 3 and 4 or 5 or 6 becomes 7 and 8 becomes 9.

A prime that an equation outputs more than once counts once so an equation that keeps repeating the same prime gets a low percentage.

Each equation was tested on 300000 inputs with outputs under 1 trillion unless the line says otherwise. A line says "rough" when chance predicts fewer than 50 primes because luck can then move the percentage by a large amount.

## Calculus (slopes and areas)

A slope or an area is another equation so the percentage follows the equation that the slope or the area turns into such as 2n + 1 for the slope of n² + n + 41.

The highest in the group is slope of n² + n + 41 which is 2n + 1 at +100% and the lowest in the group is slope of n³ + 2 which is 3n² at −100%.

1. slope of n² + n + 41 which is 2n + 1: +100%
2. area under x² + x + 41 from 0 to n with decimals dropped: +6% (14421 inputs tested)
3. area under ln(x) from 1 to n with decimals dropped: +1%
4. area under x × sin(x)² from 0 to n with decimals dropped: 0%
5. area under 1 ÷ ln(x) from 2 to n with decimals dropped: −1% (repeated primes counted once and 20000 inputs tested)
6. slope of n³ + 2 which is 3n²: −100%

## Sums

Sums of whole numbers or squares produce no primes because the totals split into factors such as n × (n + 1) ÷ 2. Sums of primes behave like the plain number line.

The highest in the group is the first n primes added up then plus 1 for even totals at +99% and the lowest in the group is √1 + √2 + √3 up to √n with decimals dropped in each piece at −100%.

1. the first n primes added up then plus 1 for even totals: +99%
2. n÷1 + n÷2 + n÷3 up to n÷n with decimals dropped in each piece: +1%
3. n × (1 + 1/2 + 1/3 up to 1/n) with decimals dropped: 0%
4. the first n primes added up: −1%
5. 1 + 2 + 3 up to n: −100%
6. 1² + 2² + 3² up to n²: −100% (14421 inputs tested)
7. 1² + 2² + 3² up to n² then plus 1: −100% (14421 inputs tested)
8. √1 + √2 + √3 up to √n with decimals dropped in each piece: −100%
