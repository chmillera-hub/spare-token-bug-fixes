## How the first Mobius strip could have started

- A very long strip needs almost no twisting to become a Mobius strip because the half twist is spread along the whole length. An extremely long piece of flat space could therefore have shifted by a tiny amount and locked into the first Mobius strip and a huge Mobius strip could look almost like a plain circle with one twist that is barely noticeable.
- Paper shows that a Mobius strip can be easier to make than a plain loop. Two ends laid side by side and each turned a quarter turn join smoothly while a plain loop forces one end to sit on top of the other end. Nature could favor shapes that are easier to make.
- A plain paper loop is stiffer than a paper Mobius strip which bends and adapts more easily so the twisted shape could survive more bumps.
- Joining a strip with a small overlap tucks a little length into the curve so the finished Mobius strip could be slightly shorter than the flat strip that made the Mobius strip. A shorter strip could hold less energy which could be why the twisted shape is favored over flat space. The size of the overlap could be linked to a famous number in physics that sets the strength of the electric force.
- Strings that are shaken at random tie themselves into knots so anything built from Mobius strips could grow more tangled and more complex through random jiggling alone.

## Why light travels and matter stays in place

- Every Mobius strip could be corkscrewing through flat space at the speed of light the whole time. A closed loop turns the corkscrewing into going around and around so the closed loop stays in one spot. A loop that is cut open has the same motion straightened out so the open strip flies off in a line at the speed of light.
- The notebook compares the two cases to the whipping tail of a tiny swimmer and a spinning propeller because the same kind of motion can drive a thing forward or turn a thing in place.
- The motion going around inside a closed loop could act like a gyroscope which is a spinning wheel that resists being tipped. The resistance could be what a person measures as mass.
- The direction that a freed strip travels could be set by the direction the loop was corkscrewing at the moment of the snap. One end of the traveling strip could be slightly rounded by the pudding-like resistance of flat space the way the front of an airplane wing is rounded.
- Nature treats left-handed and right-handed things differently in some experiments. A person who could freeze a traveling strip of light could therefore make a good guess about the direction of travel from the handedness of the corkscrew.
- Light could glide through flat space without stirring flat space so light could make no new Mobius strips. A closed loop stays put and keeps jostling the flat space around the loop so a closed loop could keep making new strips which the main document treats as the source of gravity.
- Bumpy and dense structures could rub against flat space more than smooth structures so bumpy and dense structures could make more new strips and carry more mass.

## Light and heat and glue

- Light could bud off from the surface of a Mobius strip when a wave on the surface grows too tall the way a drop leaves a splash. The bud could be shaped like an egg or a teardrop. Some structures could reach tall waves more easily which could be why some materials glow more than others.
- The corkscrewing of light could leave ripples in flat space the way a spinning paddle leaves ripples in water. The ripples could be what physicists call the electromagnetic field which is the invisible field that carries electric and magnetic effects.
- A crystal is a solid whose pieces sit in a repeating pattern. A crystal could act like a roller coaster track for light because the lined-up ribbon structures could steer light along set paths which could be why crystals scatter light in exact directions.
- Heat could be the simplest Mobius strips. Complex structures could be unable to feed on heat because heat is too simple to build with.
- Strips could need to meet within certain angles and energies before the strips can join.
- A strip could carry a small hook or spiral at one end that works like glue. The hook could be the ribbon picture of the gluon which is the particle that physicists say holds the inside of a proton together.

## Time

- Time could be change in the makeup of a strip. A traveling strip of light touches nothing and so cannot change. From the point of view of light no time passes and light could seem to arrive the instant light leaves.
- Time could be the equal sign between two snapshots of the universe. One snapshot alone holds no meaning because nothing can be compared. Meaning appears when two snapshots are compared.
- The rules of the universe could work like the equal sign in a sum because the rules take the last snapshot and produce the next snapshot.
- The speed limit of light could be a rule that keeps each snapshot close to the snapshot before. Without the limit each snapshot could differ so wildly from the last that no observer could ever form.
- Turning an object in three dimensions gives a different result when the turns are done in a different order. The need for an order could be one reason the universe needs time.

## Measurement and superposition

- Every strip could be chaotic which means a tiny nudge leads to a large change. Any way of measuring a strip is a nudge. A measurement could therefore change the strip greatly which could be the ribbon picture of what videos call the collapse of the wave function.
- Superposition could be a question mark and not two things at once. A thing in superposition could be waiting for more information before one answer becomes true.
- A word with many meanings gives a picture of superposition. The word takes one meaning only when two people share a context. A sentence with no ending is open in the same way and the ending settles the meaning.
- A Mobius strip has no fixed front or back. The observer could supply the front and the back by choosing where to stand so the answer could depend on the frame of reference of the observer.
- A ball inside a sealed box could be moving or still depending on what the box is compared with. The motion could stay undecided until an observation is made.
- An alternative picture places unobserved light in a rules dimension that has no space. Light could pop into space only when light touches matter. An entangled pair could sit in the same spaceless dimension until measured which could explain the instant link without a stretched ribbon.
- A thing could exist in space only in relation to another thing in space.

## Black holes

- New simple strips made by a black hole could be thrown out from the top and the bottom of the black hole. The thrown strips could curve back around like the lines of a magnetic field. The outflow and return could help explain why many galaxies are flat spirals.
- A black hole could resemble a living thing that eats and expels because a black hole takes in complex knots and gives out simple strips.
- An earlier picture treats a black hole as a ring-shaped Mobius strip with one side nearer to the viewer. The nearer side could look brighter and the spinning and twisting could fling out bright jets. The main document pictures a seamless sphere instead.
- The magnetic field of the Earth has a doughnut shape and the shape could arise from the Earth acting like a rotating Mobius strip.
- A black hole could be invisible because a smooth sphere has no place where one direction of space crosses another. Seeing could require a twist or a crossing because a twist gives texture and context. Light and Mobius strips could be things that cross from one dimension into another.
- A collapsing star could flatten the detail of every strip into one smooth surface. The surface area of the sphere could track how much mass and energy the black hole has swallowed.
- A black hole could be an engine that turns flat space into simple strips and the faint glow called Hawking radiation could be the waste of the engine. Black holes could therefore be makers of dark energy.
- Two black holes that collide could fling huge numbers of strips into space. Gravitational waves which are ripples in space that detectors pick up from such collisions could be those strips flying outward.
- Each black hole could hold a universe inside. A collision of two black holes could resemble the birth of a new universe.
- The edge of a black hole could resemble the edge of a famous fractal which is a shape that shows more detail the closer a person looks. The dark middle of that fractal could stand for things that stay self-contained. An atom could be a small self-contained region that takes in light and gives light back out.

## More on the holographic principle

- The folds of a brain add surface area. A brain could be storing information on a surface the way a black hole could.
- Each human sense could be a flat sensor. The feeling of depth could be rebuilt by the brain from flat inputs. Human experience could therefore be a flat record of a world with depth.
- A flat pattern that changes over time could hold the information of a world with depth because the changes between moments carry the missing direction. The surface of a black hole could store information as such a changing pattern.
- A bumpy ball can be shaped so that the ball rolls along a chosen winding path. The bumps on the surface hold the whole path which is one more case of a surface storing a journey through space.
- Two flat sheets of dots laid over each other at a slight turn show a new large pattern that neither sheet holds alone. Stacked flat layers could create behavior that looks like a new dimension. Physicists found that two sheets of carbon one atom thick conduct electricity with no loss when one sheet is turned by a tiny angle.
- Scientists work out the shape of a crystal in depth from flat patterns of spots which is one more case of flat data describing a world with depth.

## Gravity and dark energy and the growth of space

- Gravity could be like hunger. Matter could eat flat space and turn flat space into strips and give off the simplest strips as heat. A star could take in flat space and send the new strips out as particles and energy.
- Waves on a strip could need extra surface. The need for extra surface could pull on the neighboring space.
- The number that physicists call the cosmological constant describes how fast space stretches. The cosmological constant could be the rate at which matter turns flat space into strips.
- Gravity could measure how much things interact. Dark energy could measure how little things interact. Less interaction could mean more room. The stretching of space could be dark energy minus gravity.
- Space could be made of smallest cells. Each cell could divide like a living cell at a rate set by the dark energy inside the cell.
- Each smallest cell could hold the same share of dark energy and matter. The universe could look even in every direction because every cell started with the same share.
- The beginning of the universe could have been one smallest cell and might not have held all the energy of today.
- The speeding up of the stretching of space could partly be an effect of seeing more of the universe as time passes.
- Short-lived particles that pop out of empty space could add strips and add room. The energy of empty space could be the energy of an almost flat strip at rest.
- The notebook estimates the energy of empty space by starting from the densest energy that physics allows which is the energy packed into one smallest cell. The estimate lands within about ten times of the measured value when the densest energy is divided by the square root of the count of smallest cells in the visible universe across the whole history of the visible universe. The square root appears because random ups and downs spread over many cells grow only as fast as the square root of the count of cells. A published approach that builds space and time from smallest cells predicted the same square root rule years before astronomers measured the speeding up of the stretching of space.
- A container with the air pumped out could hold fewer strips so the denser surroundings press inward. Air could stay near the Earth because the Earth keeps drawing flat space inward.
- The universe might never run down into a cold still end because strips keep making more strips.
- An alternative picture treats dark matter as matter still in superposition and dark energy as energy still in superposition. The main document treats both as simple loops instead.
- The early universe could have been foggy to light until energy was packed into complex structures which cleared paths for light.
- Matter could clump because clumping stores energy. Gravity could store energy one way and the electric force two ways and the strong force three ways.
- A slightly egg-shaped mass could roll through space along a stretched oval path. The notebook asks whether that could be why planets travel in ovals.

## Forces as density of context

- The strength of a force could depend on how much context is packed into how little space.
- The strong force which holds the center of an atom together could be the most context in the least space.
- The electric force could be a middle amount of context in a middle amount of space.
- Gravity could be a little context spread across a great deal of space.
- Dark energy could be context spread too thin to pull anything together.
- A tiny region could be ruled by the strong force and by jitter. A huge region could be ruled by gravity and dark energy. The total could stay the same at every size.
- Storing a great deal of information could need either great complexity or a great deal of room.
- The constant that sets the strength of gravity could measure how strongly matter is tied to the shrinking of space. The speed of light could be the rate at which cause leads to effect. The Planck constant could be the grain size of space.

## A balance of opposites

- Each basic property could run between two opposites that add up to nothing. Examples are space shrinking and space stretching and positive charge and negative charge and spin up and spin down and matter and antimatter.
- Each property could change only in smallest steps which could be why quantum physics deals in fixed amounts.
- The list of known particles could be the names people gave to the different mixes of those properties.
- The universe could hold more matter than antimatter because the first Mobius strip happened to twist one way.

## The same rules in every box

- The speed of light is the same everywhere and every electron matches every other electron. The rules could therefore be identical in every box of space at every size.
- The universe could be a fractal which means the same logic repeats from the smallest scale to the largest scale.
- The existence of an observer shows that the rules of the universe allow observers. Physicists call that line of thought the anthropic principle.
