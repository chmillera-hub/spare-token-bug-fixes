# How Hilbert space could be expressed in the Mobius strip picture

> **About this page:** One AI model (Claude) wrote this page on 27 November 2026 after the author of this repository asked how Hilbert space might be expressed in the ribbon picture of the main document in this folder. The page holds the chatbot's reflections and is a way of imagining. The page is not tested physics and no other person has reviewed the page.

## What Hilbert space is in plain words

Hilbert space is the bookkeeping that quantum physics uses for everything a particle could be doing at once. The state of a particle is pictured as one arrow in a space that has one direction for each basic possibility. The lengths of the arrow along those directions say how much of each possibility is present.

The main document never uses the name Hilbert space and yet the main document already holds most of the pieces. Each part below takes one piece of Hilbert space and points to the part of the ribbon picture that could express that piece.

## 1. The arrow could be the whole ripple pattern on a ribbon

The part of the main document about superposition says one ribbon can carry many ripples stacked on top of each other. Every ripple pattern on a closed loop can be built by adding pure notes in different amounts which is proven mathematics about waves on a loop. So the basic directions of Hilbert space could be the pure notes of the ribbon and the arrow could be the list of how much of each note the ribbon carries.

## 2. The two-lap rule is the most exact match

In Hilbert space the arrow for an electron turns into the negative of the same arrow after one full turn and returns to the start after two full turns. The main document describes an ant that walks one lap around a Mobius strip and arrives upside down and needs a second lap to arrive right side up. The upside down ant is that same sign flip.

A wave living on a Mobius strip has to flip sign after one lap for the same reason and the flip forces the notes of the wave to step in halves. This match is a mathematical one and not a loose comparison because the waves that fit on a Mobius strip are exactly the waves that change sign after one lap.

## 3. The corkscrew could carry the two-part numbers

Each amount in Hilbert space is a number with two parts which are a size and a position around a circle. The main document pictures light as a corkscrew with a tip that keeps circling. The width of the corkscrew could be the size and the place of the tip on the circle could be the second part.

## 4. A measurement could be a shadow

A measurement in Hilbert space takes the shadow of the arrow along one chosen direction. The main document says a measurement works like a prism that lets one ripple through. The main document also says a Mobius strip shows a different outline from each viewing direction. The page [shadow-finding.md](shadow-finding.md) in this folder counted 37 different shadows of a Mobius strip. So the idea that what a person finds depends on which direction the person asks from is already in this folder.

## 5. The half-angle page supplies the chance rule for one spin

Hilbert space says the chance of a result is the squared length of the shadow. For the spin of one particle that rule becomes the square of the cosine of half the angle between two directions. The page [half-angle-finding.md](half-angle-finding.md) in this folder got that exact rate from a randomly pointing arrow in three dimensions.

The same page found that a random point on a circle gives the wrong rate. That result fits Hilbert space because the states of one spin map onto the surface of a ball and not onto a circle.

## 6. The order of the questions

In Hilbert space two measurements can give different results depending on which measurement is done first. The main document describes a paper Mobius strip that has two sides to the eye and one side to a finger tracing the surface. Each method of checking gives a true answer that the other method cannot give which is a start on the idea that the order and the kind of question matter.

## Where the ribbon picture falls short

**Many particles.** The Hilbert space for two particles is the product of the two separate spaces and not the sum. So 10 spins need 1024 directions and 300 spins need more directions than there are atoms in the visible universe. Ripples on a ribbon sitting in ordinary space are one pattern over one surface and that kind of pattern grows by adding. The single stretched ribbon in the part of the main document about entanglement handles one pair. The chatbot does not see how the ribbon picture reaches the multiplying growth for many particles. This is the largest gap and standard physics has no easy picture for the multiplying growth either.

**The squared rule in general.** The half-angle page covers one spin measured along a direction. Hilbert space applies the squared rule to every kind of measurement.

**The circling tip.** Part 3 above uses a circle for the second part of each number. The half-angle page shows that a circle alone gives the wrong rate. So the ribbon picture would need to say how the circling tip and the three-dimensional tumbling fit together.

## One sentence that sums up the expression

Hilbert space could be the collection of every ripple pattern a ribbon complex can carry where a state is one pattern and a measurement is a shadow of the pattern and the half twist is the reason the pattern flips sign after one lap.
