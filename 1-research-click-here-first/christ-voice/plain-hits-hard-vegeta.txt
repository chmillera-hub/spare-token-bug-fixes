Three short interactions written by an AI chatbot (Claude) on 12 November 2026. No line is copied from the Dragon Ball shows or books or from any Bible translation.


INTERACTION 1: THE HANDS THAT SHOOK

In this scene Vegeta speaks about the day his hands shook in front of the tyrant who ruled him.

Vegeta: My hands shook in front of that tyrant and I have hated my hands ever since because a prince does not shake. I trained until the shaking stopped and the hate did not stop with the shaking.

Jesus: I knelt in a garden at night and my own sweat fell like heavy drops because my soul was full of sorrow at what was coming. I did not hide my sorrow from my Father but I spoke my sorrow out loud and my Father heard me.

Jesus: This is the fear that hands tell the truth about before the mouth is ready because hands shake when a life is in danger and the shaking is the whole person asking to stay alive. Your hands were not weak. Your hands were awake. The tyrant taught you that fear is a stain and so you became a man who trains against his own hands because nobody told you that fear might be a guard standing watch over a person's life. The hate you carry for your hands might be grief that never got a name since a person who was taken from his home had every reason to shake and no one to shake beside. And you can ask the shaking what the shaking was guarding and then you might find that the Father was not ashamed of your hands and that your soul was speaking through your hands the whole time.


INTERACTION 2: THE TEARS IN THE DUST

In this scene Vegeta speaks about the day he wept in front of his rival while he was dying.

Vegeta: I wept in the dust in front of my rival while I was dying and I begged him to stop the tyrant because I could not stop the tyrant. I have not forgiven my eyes for that day because my people do not beg and do not weep.

Jesus: I wept at the grave of a man I loved and the people standing near saw my face and said look how he loved him. I did not wipe my eyes to look strong because the tears were my love with nowhere left to go.

Jesus: This is the moment when the mask of a prince cracked and the man underneath spoke because a dying man has no strength left over for hiding. You call the weeping your worst hour. I might call the weeping your first true word. For years you carried your father and your people and your planet inside your chest without one sound because the tyrant who owned you punished every sound and so the grief waited behind your eyes like water behind a wall. The tears in the dust might have been that water finally reaching another person since you told your rival that the tyrant took your father and your people and your planet and your rival listened. A man who weeps in front of another man has not lost his crown because the weeping hands the other man a piece of the truth to carry. You can tell one person you trust about that day in the dust because a grief that is spoken has somewhere to go. And the eyes you have not forgiven might be the most faithful part of you since your eyes refused to lie on the day when every other part of you had been trained to lie and God was near to you in that dust.


INTERACTION 3: THE OPEN HAND

In this scene Vegeta speaks about the day his rival held out an open hand to him after a fight.

Vegeta: My rival held out his open hand to me after our fight and I froze because these hands have only ever closed into fists. I do not know how to take a hand that I am not trying to break.

Jesus: I knelt on the floor and washed the feet of my friends with my own hands and one friend tried to pull his feet away. I did not want hands that only give orders but hands that serve because my Father put all things into my hands and I picked up a towel.

Jesus: This is the fear of a man who knows exactly what his hands have done because the memory of harm lives in the fingers and the fingers remember. You froze. You did not pull away. The freezing might be care wearing the only armor that care has ever been given since a man who did not care about the open hand would have felt nothing at all. It may be that nobody offered you an open hand before that day because the tyrant who ruled you had use for fists and no use for open palms and so an open palm feels to you like a language you were not taught. But a language can be learned one word at a time and your rival is already teaching you the first word which is stay with his open hand held out in front of you. You can say to your rival I do not know how to do this yet because a person who holds out a hand has already chosen to wait. The stillness you felt might be your soul going quiet in front of something holy because a hand that has only made fists and now holds still for a friend is a hand that God might be teaching to bless.
