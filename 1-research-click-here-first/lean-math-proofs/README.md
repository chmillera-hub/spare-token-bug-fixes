# Lean math proofs

This folder holds eight statement files that are described in a later section and eleven computer-checked proofs of ten small math facts that an AI model (Claude) wrote on 27 November 2026 in the proof language Lean 4 which is a programming language where a program called the Lean checker accepts a proof only when every step follows from the rules of logic.

The five facts are steps inside six larger known results from the public project Left to Solve at https://lefttosolve.com which splits hard math results into smaller steps so that many people and AI agents can prove one step at a time. The collection of that project at https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents had no proof file for any of the five steps when Claude copied the collection on 27 November 2026. Other contributors to the Left to Solve project had proofs of all five steps accepted into the collection between 20 and 30 minutes after Claude made that copy (the accepted proofs carry the pull request numbers 4819 and 4832 and 4855 and 4862 and 4890 and 4930) which means the proofs in this folder are independent second proofs of steps that the project already counts as done and the proofs in this folder were not sent to the project.

The five facts are small helper steps and are not solutions to open problems because the hard parts of the larger results are other steps that still have no proof in that collection.

- [Erdos946.erdos_946.variants.spiro_5040.R1.H2.lean](Erdos946.erdos_946.variants.spiro_5040.R1.H2.lean) proves a counting fact which says that among the whole numbers from 1 to X at most X divided by B numbers can be divided evenly by the square of some whole number larger than B (for example at most 10 of the numbers from 1 to 100 are divisible by the square of a number larger than 10). The counting fact is one of two steps toward a 1981 result of Claudia Spiro about Erdős problem 946 which says that infinitely many whole numbers n have the same count of divisors as the number n + 5040.
- [Erdos1162.erdos_1162.variants.pyber.R1.H1.lean](Erdos1162.erdos_1162.variants.pyber.R1.H1.lean) proves a lower bound on the number of subgroups of the symmetric group on n objects where the symmetric group means the collection of all ways to rearrange n objects and a subgroup means a smaller collection of rearrangements that is closed under doing one rearrangement after another. The bound says that the number of subgroups is at least 2 raised to the power a times b where a is n divided by 4 rounded down and a + b is n divided by 2 rounded down (for example 8 objects give at least 2^4 = 16 subgroups). The bound is the easier of two steps toward a 1993 result of László Pyber about Erdős problem 1162.
- [Erdos1162.erdos_1162.variants.roney_dougal_tracey.R1.H1.lean](Erdos1162.erdos_1162.variants.roney_dougal_tracey.R1.H1.lean) holds the same proof of the same lower bound with a different first line because the project lists the lower bound a second time as a step toward a sharper 2025 result of Roney-Dougal and Tracey.
- [Erdos968.erdos_968.variants.infinite_increasingTriples.R1.H2.lean](Erdos968.erdos_968.variants.infinite_increasingTriples.R1.H2.lean) proves a bound on the size of prime numbers which says that the prime number in position n + 1 of the list of primes (2 and 3 and 5 and so on) divided by n + 1 is at most 2 times the natural logarithm of that prime number whenever n is at least 13 (for example the prime in position 100 is 541 and 541 divided by 100 is 5.41 while 2 times the natural logarithm of 541 is about 12.6). The bound is the easier of two steps toward a known result about Erdős problem 968 which says that the ratio of a prime number to the position of that prime number rises twice in a row infinitely often.
- [ElliottHalberstamConjecture.elliott_halberstam.variants.bombieri_vinogradov.R1.H2.lean](ElliottHalberstamConjecture.elliott_halberstam.variants.bombieri_vinogradov.R1.H2.lean) proves a bookkeeping fact about sums over prime numbers which says that a bound on sums where each prime p is weighted by the natural logarithm of p can be turned into a bound on the same sums without the logarithm weights at the cost of dividing by the natural logarithm of 2. The bookkeeping fact is the easier of two steps toward the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem of 1965 which describes how evenly prime numbers spread across remainder classes on average.
- [HyperbolicGroupsResiduallyFinite.hyperbolic_residuallyFinite_iff_exists_finiteIndex_ne_top.R1.H1.lean](HyperbolicGroupsResiduallyFinite.hyperbolic_residuallyFinite_iff_exists_finiteIndex_ne_top.R1.H1.lean) proves a short fact from group theory where a group means a collection of reversible moves that can be done one after another. The short fact says that if every group of a kind called hyperbolic can be told apart from the one-move group by finite pictures of the group (the label for that property is residually finite) then every hyperbolic group with more than one move has a smaller part that is not the whole group and that splits the group into finitely many equal pieces. The short fact is the easy direction of a known two-way statement and the other direction still has no proof in the collection.

## Proofs written for sending to the Left to Solve project

The five files in this section were written after the owner of this repository made a personal copy of the Left to Solve collection at https://github.com/chmillera-hub/open-math-agents so that proofs could be sent to the project under the GitHub name chmillera-hub. This folder keeps a copy of every proof written for the project so that the proofs stay readable here even if the Left to Solve project is removed from the internet.

- [Erdos100.erdos_100.variants.guth_katz.R1.H2.lean](Erdos100.erdos_100.variants.guth_katz.R1.H2.lean) proves a fact about distances between points on a flat surface which says that if any two different distances between points of a finite set differ by at least 1 then the number of different distances is at most the largest distance in the set plus 1 (for example the three corners of a triangle with sides 3 and 4 and 5 give three different distances and the largest distance plus 1 is 6). The fact is the easier of two steps toward a known result about Erdős problem 100 which follows from a 2015 theorem of Guth and Katz. Claude reserved this step on the Left to Solve site on 27 November 2026 and the owner of this repository sent the proof to the project on that day as pull request number 7124. The project accepted a proof of the same step from another contributor (pull request number 6342) because that contributor had sent a proof about 80 minutes earlier and the project checks proofs in the order of arrival.
- [Erdos89.erdos_89.variants.n_dvd_log_n.R1.H2.lean](Erdos89.erdos_89.variants.n_dvd_log_n.R1.H2.lean) proves a counting fact about line segments between points which says that the square of the number of ordered pairs of different points is at most the number of different distances times the number of ways to pick two ordered pairs that are the same distance apart. The counting fact is the easier of two steps toward a known result about Erdős problem 89. Claude reserved this step on the Left to Solve site on 27 November 2026 but another contributor had a proof of the same step accepted before Claude finished and so this proof was not sent to the project.
- [Erdos398.erdos_398.R1.H1.lean](Erdos398.erdos_398.R1.H1.lean) proves a fact about factorials where the factorial of n (written n!) means the product of the whole numbers from 1 to n. The fact says that for n at least 4 the number n! + 1 is a perfect square exactly when n! divided by 4 is a product of two neighboring whole numbers (for example 5! + 1 is 121 which is 11 squared and 5! divided by 4 is 30 which is 5 times 6). The fact is one of three steps toward Erdős problem 398 which is an open problem that asks whether 4 and 5 and 7 are the only values of n that make n! + 1 a perfect square. Claude reserved this step on 27 November 2026 and the owner of this repository sent the proof to the project on that day. Other contributors had sent proofs of the same step between 7 and 24 minutes earlier and the project checks proofs in the order of arrival which means the project will most likely accept one of the earlier proofs.
- [Erdos386.erdos_386.variants.two.R1.H1.lean](Erdos386.erdos_386.variants.two.R1.H1.lean) proves a fact about counting pairs which says that if P is at least 6 and 8 times P plus 1 is a perfect square then P is the number of ways to choose 2 objects out of n objects for some n that is at least 4 (for example P = 6 gives 49 which is 7 squared and 6 is the number of ways to choose 2 objects out of 4). The fact is one of two steps toward a part of Erdős problem 386 which is an open problem. Claude reserved this step on 27 November 2026 and the owner of this repository sent the proof to the project on that day. Other contributors had sent proofs of the same step between 7 and 24 minutes earlier and the project checks proofs in the order of arrival which means the project will most likely accept one of the earlier proofs.
- [Erdos458.erdos_458.R1.H1.lean](Erdos458.erdos_458.R1.H1.lean) proves a fact about the least common multiple of the numbers from 1 to n which means the smallest number that every number from 1 to n divides evenly. The fact says that the natural logarithm of the least common multiple for b minus the natural logarithm of the least common multiple for a equals a sum over the numbers between a and b in which each power of a prime number contributes the natural logarithm of that prime number. The fact is one step toward Erdős problem 458 which is an open problem. Claude reserved this step on 27 November 2026 and the owner of this repository sent the proof to the project on that day. Other contributors had sent proofs of the same step between 7 and 24 minutes earlier and the project checks proofs in the order of arrival which means the project will most likely accept one of the earlier proofs.

## Statements written for sending to the Left to Solve project

The eight files in this section hold statements and no proofs. A statement file translates an unproven guess from the Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (a public list of number sequences at https://oeis.org) into the language Lean 4 so that people and AI agents can later try to prove the guess with a computer check. The checking script of the Left to Solve project confirmed on 27 November 2026 that the eight files are valid Lean 4 text but a computer cannot confirm that a translation says the same thing as the original guess and so a person at the project reviews each translation. The owner of this repository sent the eight files to the project on 27 November 2026 and the project accepted five of the eight files into the public collection within about 40 minutes (the files for sequences A055469 and A366871 and A109095 and A256836 and A067730). The other three files (for sequences A356109 and A352628 and A383244) were still waiting at the project at 22:45 world time (UTC) on that day. Claude searched the waiting line of the project before sending and found no other translation of the same eight guesses. About 690 translations from many contributors were waiting for review at the project at 14:45 world time (UTC) on that day and the project was accepting about 100 to 140 translations each hour.

The file was sent as pull request number 7412 and the project accepted the file on 27 November 2026.

## How the proofs were checked

Claude ran the checking script of the Left to Solve project (the file tools/check.sh at version 630fcab of the collection) on each of the eleven files and the script printed "accepted" for all eleven files which means the Lean checker found no gap and the proofs use only the three standard starting assumptions of Lean (propext and Classical.choice and Quot.sound). No person has reread the proofs. The Left to Solve project has not received or reviewed the six proofs in the first list because other contributors finished the same steps first.

## The idea behind each proof

- The counting proof adds up the multiples of each square because exactly X divided by d squared (rounded down) of the numbers from 1 to X are multiples of d squared. The sum of 1 divided by d squared over every d larger than B stays below 1 divided by B because each term 1 divided by d squared is at most 1/(d-1) minus 1/d and those differences cancel in a chain.
- The subgroup proof swaps objects in pairs because a + b separate pairs of objects can each be swapped or left alone which gives a collection of 2^(a+b) rearrangements. Each grid of zeros and ones with a rows and b columns then picks out a different subgroup inside that collection and there are 2^(a times b) such grids.
- The prime number proof uses two facts from the public Lean math library named Mathlib which are a lower bound of Chebyshev type on a weighted count of prime powers up to a number P and an upper bound on the same weighted count by the number of primes up to P times the natural logarithm of P. The two facts together force the number of primes up to P to be large and the prime in position n + 1 has exactly n + 1 primes up to that prime.
- The bookkeeping proof walks up the whole numbers one at a time because adding a new prime changes the plain sum and the weighted sum divided by the logarithm by the same amount. The leftover error at each step is at most the bound times the drop in 1 divided by the logarithm and those drops cancel in a chain.
- The group theory proof picks one move other than the do-nothing move because the residually finite property hands over a part of the group that leaves out the picked move and that splits the group into finitely many equal pieces.
