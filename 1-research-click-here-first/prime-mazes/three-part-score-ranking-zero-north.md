## The score

Each digit of a prime opens one door of a room and the digits fill a square grid row by row starting at the top left room. This page uses the version of the door rule where 0 opens north: 0 and 4 and 8 open north. 1 and 5 and 9 open east. 2 and 6 open south. 3 and 7 open west.

The three part score adds three numbers.

- **Largest region:** the most rooms that are joined together by open doors.
- **Longest walk:** the most rooms one line can pass through without entering any room twice.
- **Door points:** picture a player who walks around the outside of the grid and may open a door from outside at any spot on the outer wall. The player earns 1 point for each spot where the player can enter and then walk through joined rooms to the opposite side of the grid and leave there. A spot on the top wall needs a path to the bottom row and a spot on the left wall needs a path to the right column. A corner room has two spots because a corner room touches two outer walls.

A grid of 3 by 3 rooms has 12 spots on the outer wall (3 on each side) so the door points stop at 12. A grid of 2 by 2 rooms has 8 spots and a grid of 4 by 4 rooms has 16 spots.

## The highest possible score

One hallway through every room that reaches all four sides earns the most: every room for the largest region plus every room for the longest walk plus every spot on the outer wall for the door points.

- 2 by 2 rooms: 4 + 4 + 8 = 16
- 3 by 3 rooms: 9 + 9 + 12 = 30 (the program checked all 262144 door patterns and the highest score found is 30)
- 4 by 4 rooms: 16 + 16 + 16 = 48
- 5 by 5 rooms: 25 + 25 + 20 = 70

No grid can beat these totals because the largest region and the longest walk cannot hold more rooms than the grid has and the door points cannot exceed the number of spots on the outer wall.

Separate hallways score lower than one full hallway because separate hallways are short. Four hallways side by side on a grid of 4 by 4 rooms earn 4 + 4 + 8 = 16 because the 4 top spots and the 4 bottom spots each lead across while no left spot leads to the right side.

## Weak points

- Thousands of primes tie at each score so the order inside a tie is just smallest prime first.
- The lists for grids of 4 by 4 rooms and 5 by 5 rooms rank a random sample and not every prime of that size.
- The score is one choice among many. On this score a full hallway always wins because the largest region and the longest walk both grow with the same hallway.
- The ranking uses one version of the door rule and a different choice of which number opens north gives a different ranking.
