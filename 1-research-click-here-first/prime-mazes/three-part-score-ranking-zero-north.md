## The score

Each digit of a prime opens one door of a room and the digits fill a square grid row by row starting at the top left room. This page uses the version of the door rule where 0 opens north: 0 and 4 and 8 open north. 1 and 5 and 9 open east. 2 and 6 open south. 3 and 7 open west.

The three part score adds three numbers.

- **Largest region:** the most rooms that are joined together by open doors.
- **Longest walk:** the most rooms one line can pass through without entering any room twice.
- **Crossing points:** 2 points for each crossing. A crossing is a group of joined rooms that reaches from one side of the grid to the opposite side (top to bottom or side to side). One group that reaches all four sides counts as two crossings and earns 4 points.

A grid of 4 by 4 rooms can hold at most 4 separate crossings (for example four hallways side by side) so the crossing points on that grid stop at 8.

## The highest possible score

One hallway through every room that reaches all four sides earns the most: every room for the largest region plus every room for the longest walk plus 4 crossing points.

- 2 by 2 rooms: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12
- 3 by 3 rooms: 9 + 9 + 4 = 22 (the program checked all 262144 door patterns and the highest score found is 22)
- 4 by 4 rooms: 16 + 16 + 4 = 36
- 5 by 5 rooms: 25 + 25 + 4 = 54

The program also checked all 256 door patterns for 2 by 2 rooms. The figures for 4 by 4 rooms and 5 by 5 rooms follow from the same reasoning and were not checked pattern by pattern.

Many separate crossings score lower than one full hallway because separate hallways are short. Four hallways side by side on a grid of 4 by 4 rooms earn 4 + 4 + 8 = 16.

## Weak points

- Thousands of primes tie at each score so the order inside a tie is just smallest prime first.
- The lists for grids of 4 by 4 rooms and 5 by 5 rooms rank a random sample and not every prime of that size.
- The score is one choice among many. On this score a full hallway always wins because the largest region and the longest walk both grow with the same hallway.
- The ranking uses one version of the door rule and a different choice of which number opens north gives a different ranking.
