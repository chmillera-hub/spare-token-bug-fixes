The rule. Every room is a hexagon with six closed doors. Each room reads one number and opens one door. 0 opens the north door and 1 opens the northeast door and 2 opens the southeast door and 3 opens the south door and 4 opens the southwest door and 5 opens the northwest door. The cycle then repeats so 6 is north and 7 is northeast and 8 is southeast and 9 is south. A door opened from either side joins the two rooms. A door that points outside the grid joins nothing. The numbers fill the rooms row by row starting at the top left room.

How the hexagons are laid out. The grid has the same number of rooms in each row and in each column. Every second column sits half a room lower so the rooms fit together like a honeycomb. Each room touches up to six neighbors where a square room touches up to four.

Which digits open which doors. The digits 0 and 6 both open north and 1 and 7 both open northeast and 2 and 8 both open southeast and 3 and 9 both open south. Only 4 opens southwest and only 5 opens northwest. So a plain row of digits opens the two western doors half as often as the other four doors. An odd digit opens northeast or south or northwest and an even digit opens north or southeast or southwest.

The colors. Every region with two or more rooms is filled with one of six colors and the six largest regions each get a different color. A room that is joined to no other room stays white.

The dashed outer walls. A dashed wall on the outside of the grid marks an outside door that the player can open. The top row has one outside door on the north wall of each room and the bottom row has one on the south wall of each room. The left column has one outside door at the west point of each room and the right column has one at the east point of each room so a corner room counts for two sides. A dashed wall turns green with a green arrow when the player can enter there and walk through joined rooms to the opposite side of the grid and leave. Each green door is worth 1 door point. A dashed wall that stays dark leads to rooms that never reach the opposite side so that door earns no point. A solid outer wall has no outside door.

The small arrows inside the rooms. Each room shows a small gray arrow next to the wall where the number in that room opened a door. An open wall with no small arrow beside the wall was opened from the neighboring room.

The scores. A region is a group of rooms joined by open doors. A top to bottom crossing is a region that touches both the top row and the bottom row. A side to side crossing is a region that touches both the left column and the right column. The longest walk is the most rooms a walker can visit without entering a room twice. The maze score is the longest walk plus the number of rooms in one row for each crossing. The three part score is the largest region plus the longest walk plus the door points. The highest possible three part score is every room for the region plus every room for the walk plus every outside door.
