The rule. Every room starts with all doors closed. Each room reads one number and opens one door. The doors of a room are counted clockwise starting from the door that points most nearly north so 0 opens that first door and 1 opens the next door clockwise. When the number is larger than the last door the count goes around again. A door opened from either side joins the two rooms. A door that points outside the grid joins nothing. The numbers fill the rooms row by row starting at the top left.

The Einstein hat tiles. The hat is a tile with 13 sides that can cover a flat surface only in patterns that never repeat. One side is twice as long as the short sides and holds two doors so a hat room has 14 doors. The hat choice works differently from the other grids. The picture starts with one hat. Each number picks a door of the current hat by counting clockwise from the door that points most nearly north and going around again after 14. If another hat already sits behind that door the door opens and the walk steps into that old hat. If the space behind the door is empty the program lists every way a new hat can sit against that door without overlapping any hat and the rest of the number (the number divided by 14 with the remainder dropped) picks one way from the list. The door opens and the walk steps into the new hat. If no hat fits behind the door nothing happens. A plain hat is drawn in a light color and a mirrored hat is drawn in a darker color. Numbers with two or more digits are used so that all 14 doors can be reached.

Growing from one room. The second menu switches any shape from a fixed grid to a picture that grows. The picture starts with one room. Each number picks a door of the current room. If the space behind the door is empty a new room appears there and the walk steps into the new room. If an old room already sits behind the door the door opens and the walk steps into the old room. The walk reads the next number from whichever room the walk is standing in. The slider sets how many numbers are read.

The score of a growing picture. A growing picture is scored by the largest region plus the longest walk. The largest region is the largest group of rooms joined through open doors. The longest walk is the longest route through open doors that never visits a room twice and the picture draws that route as a red line. A room that was visited many times still counts as one room in both parts. A square that reads no numbers counts as a room of the region and does not count as a step of the walk. The words at least appear when the search for the longest walk ran out of time.

Visit counts in the growing pictures. A room in a growing picture can be visited many times because the walk often steps back into an old room. A menu sets how the visits are shown. The stacks setting draws one more outline for each visit so a room visited five times looks like five plates on top of each other. The landscape setting tilts the picture and draws each room as a tower whose height is the number of visits. A stack stops rising after 8 visits and a tower stops rising after 40 visits and a number on top then shows the true count where the room is large enough to hold a number. Pinching a growing picture with two fingers enlarges the picture up to 40 times. A check box keeps the room where the walk stands in the middle while the picture builds and dragging the picture with one finger moves the view. Two quick taps return to the whole picture.

Squares that read no numbers. The eight sided grid and the ten sided grid have squares between the large rooms. A third menu sets how the squares behave. With the first setting the numbers go only into the large rooms and every square starts with all doors closed so a square joins a region only when a large room opens a door into the square. On a fixed grid a square that reads no numbers is not a step of the longest walk. A walk passes from one large room straight to another large room when a square joins the two rooms and only the large rooms are counted. The highest possible score then counts every room once for the largest region and every large room once for the longest walk. In the growing pictures with the first setting a door that leads into a square places the square and the walk stays in the large room because a square reads no number. A further setting makes a square disappear when a large room picks a door into the square. A square that has disappeared has all doors open from then on so the large rooms around the square are joined through the empty space. In the growing pictures the disappearing setting behaves like the open setting because a square appears only when a door is picked into the square. With the open setting every square starts with all doors open which joins the whole fixed grid into one region before the first number is read and in the growing pictures the walk passes straight through a square into the large room on the far side. With the third setting a square reads a number like any other room.

The extra number sequences. The menu of numbers also holds the terms of several sums that go on forever and the digits of several famous numbers. Some sequences such as the factorials grow past what the program can hold exactly. A term that large is replaced by the remainder after dividing by 10090080 because that remainder picks the same door in a room with any number of doors from 1 to 16. In a fixed grid the label inside a room then shows the remainder and not the full term.

The dashed outer walls. A dashed wall on the outside of the grid marks an outside door. A room on the edge counts once for each side of the grid that the room touches (top or bottom or left or right). A dashed wall turns green with a green arrow when a player can enter there and walk through joined rooms to the opposite side of the grid and leave. Each green door is worth 1 door point.

The colors. Every region with two or more rooms is filled with one of six colors and the six largest regions each get a different color. A room joined to no other room stays white.

The scores. A region is a group of rooms joined by open doors. The longest walk is the most rooms a walker can visit without entering a room twice. The three part score is the largest region plus the longest walk plus the door points. The highest possible score is every room for the region plus every room for the walk plus every outside door. The grids hold different numbers of rooms so the percent is the fair number to compare between shapes.

About this page: One AI model (Claude) wrote this program on 27 November 2026 after the author of this repository asked for the door maze on other shapes of room and for one program that switches between the shapes. No flat grid can give every room ten neighbors so the ten sided grid mixes large rooms with ten doors and plain squares. Two of the four slanted sides of a large room hold two doors each which is how an eight sided outline gets ten doors. Both doors on such a side lead into the same square so a square has six doors. The outline of the hat tile is the published shape and the growing rule for the hats was proposed by the author. No other person has reviewed the program yet.
