The rule. Every room starts with four closed doors. Each room reads one number and opens one door. A door opened from either side joins the two rooms. A door that points outside the grid joins nothing. The numbers fill the rooms row by row starting at the top left room.

The openings on the outer wall. A green arrow with a green piece in the outer wall marks a spot where a player could open a door from outside and walk through joined rooms to the opposite side of the grid. Each green arrow is worth 1 door point. Two green arrows on opposite sides that sit on the same colored region are the two ends of one crossing. A thin gray piece in the outer wall marks a door that a number opened onto the outside when no path leads from that door to the opposite side.

The small arrows inside the rooms. Each room shows a small gray arrow next to the wall where the number in that room opened a door. A room can also be entered through a door that a neighboring room opened so an open wall with no small arrow beside it was opened from the other side.

The scores. A region is a group of rooms joined by open doors. A top to bottom crossing is a region that touches both the top row and the bottom row. A side to side crossing is a region that touches both the left column and the right column. The longest walk is the most rooms a walker can visit without entering a room twice. The maze score is the longest walk plus the number of rooms along one side for each crossing. The three part score is the largest region plus the longest walk plus the door points.
