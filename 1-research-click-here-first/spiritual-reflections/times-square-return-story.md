# The return at Times Square

> **About this page:** The owner of this repository supplied this story on 19 November 2026 and the story appears in full. The story imagines Jesus returning in a chariot of fire and refusing to use violence and then finding that every way of feeding one hungry person is blocked by parking rules and store guards and armed officers.

The sky splits clean open over Times Square at 2:14 PM on a Tuesday. A chariot of literal blinding fire descends between the M&M's store and a digital billboard for a crypto exchange, scorching the asphalt as three bootleg Spider-Men scatter for cover.

Jesus steps out in dusty sandals, squints at a giant LED screen advertising a new burger, and immediately kills the money-making mood.

Half the crowd drops to its knees expecting him to vaporize Congress, melt the Pentagon, or start flipping Wall Street like a wooden table. Instead, he holds up both hands and tells everyone to relax. He is not overthrowing the government. He is not smiting the rich. He refuses to use violence, even to stop violence. He begins proclaiming his actual strategy—radical presence, honest conversation, and helping people solve their loneliness, fear, and hunger one by one—when a parking enforcement officer in a neon vest steps directly into his aura, taps a clipboard, and tells him he has to move the chariot because it is blocking a commercial loading zone.

Jesus pauses mid-revelation, looks at the officer, looks at the flaming celestial vehicle, and sighs. Fine.

He climbs back in and spends two excruciating minutes trying to parallel park a chariot of divine fire between a box truck and a Honda Civic while tourists film from the curb. He finally gets it lined up, steps out, and stares blankly at the parking meter before realizing he doesn't have any quarters—and definitely doesn't have the city parking app downloaded. Defeated by the sheer, soul-crushing friction of municipal red tape, he snaps his fingers, vanishes the chariot into thin air, drags a hand down his face, and asks the crowd where he was. It is the ultimate bureaucratic stall tactic: bury anyone trying to heal the world under so much petty paperwork and parking logistics that they lose their train of thought before they even get started.

Before he can recover his momentum, a guy in a torn jacket steps forward and says he hasn't eaten in two days.

Easy layup. The Son of God looks around, spots a massive fluorescent retail fortress a few blocks away, and points right at it. There is a mountain of food sitting in that building called Walmart. Go inside, take what you need, and eat.

The guy stares at him like he just fell off a turnip truck from the first century. He explains that if he walks out of there with a six-dollar rotisserie chicken, a loss prevention officer and two armed cops are going to tackle him before he clears the automatic doors.

Jesus blinks, slightly thrown off, and pivots. Alright, bad plan. Physical safety first. What about the local food pantry?

Only open once a month, the guy says. Every other day of the week you are legally required to just sit there and be hungry.

Now the Messiah is visibly sweating. He rubs his temples and tries again. Okay, what if we walk right down to City Hall or the nearest police precinct, look the public servants in the eye, and just tell them a human being is starving?

The guy laughs directly in the face of the Almighty and tells him to try it first and report back.

And right there, standing under a blinking advertisement for a Broadway musical, the Creator of the Universe realizes he has been completely soft-locked by municipal bureaucracy.

Rome was amateur hour. Crucifying a guy on a hill was a PR disaster because it gave everyone a spectacle they couldn't ignore. The modern system is way smarter. If Jesus walks into City Hall demanding a sandwich, nobody nails him to a piece of wood. They hit him with a trespassing citation, slap him with a 72-hour psychiatric hold for claiming to be the Son of God, and tuck him into a concrete room with no Wi-Fi.

Sure, he is an omnipotent being. He could phase straight through the steel bars like morning mist and teleport back to Manhattan. Except he runs the math in his head and realizes what happens ten seconds after a prisoner literally vanishes out of a locked holding cell. The NYPD declares a citywide anomalous threat, scrambles three tactical units, and floods six blocks with assault rifles. Somebody who looks like him is probably going to get tackled on the sidewalk instead. A stray bullet meant for him could hit a pedestrian.

Because he refuses to risk collateral damage, his own commitment to nonviolence severely nerfs his ability to share the divine salvation of a radically nonviolent pro-human framework with others. The armed state doesn't even have to beat him in a fight; it just holds bystanders hostage by existing, forcing him to remain largely quiet so nobody gets hurt accidentally.

Even the classic miracles are dead on arrival. He thinks about just duplicating loaves and fishes right there on the pavement, until he realizes the Department of Health will shut him down in twenty minutes for running an unpermitted pop-up fishery, followed immediately by a fine for obstructing pedestrian traffic.

So there he stands. Infinite love. Zero coercion. No chariot. Most people are keeping their distance, terrified of what happens if they associate with him and draw the ire of a potentially psychopathic power structure with massive amounts of power and money—especially knowing that if the authorities lock them up in a concrete cell, Jesus is not going to break them out by hurting anyone and refuses to use violence to help anyone. A few brave souls film from afar while the comment sections explode into a fierce debate over whether the city should lock him up because he didn't pay that parking ticket for parking in the loading zone.
