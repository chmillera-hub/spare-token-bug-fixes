# The Mobius Strip Picture of Physics

November 11 2026

## What this document is

This document explains strange behaviors in physics by picturing space as being built from twisted paper-like ribbons called Mobius strips and the document uses no numbers and no equations because the goal is for a reader to be able to imagine each idea the way a person imagines a strip of paper. Each physics part first describes one behavior that popular science videos call weird such as two far-apart particles seeming to change at the same instant and then offers a what-if picture built from things that paper strips really do when a person twists or cuts or tapes the strips.

The pictures are offered as ways of imagining and are not offered as proven physics because no experiment has yet tested whether space is built from twisted ribbons. The later parts of the document move from physics toward biology because the later parts ask whether survival behaviors seen in living things such as defending the body or moving toward food could also appear in a simple form inside complex ribbon structures.

## The building block: a particle as a twisted ribbon of space

What if the smallest piece of matter is not a tiny ball and is instead a flat ribbon of space that was given one half twist and then joined end to end which is the shape called a Mobius strip? A Mobius strip has only one side and only one edge which means an ant walking along the middle of the strip would cross every part of the surface and return to the starting spot without ever stepping over an edge. A Mobius strip is flat like a sheet of paper and yet the half twist needs depth to exist so a Mobius strip is the simplest flat thing that reaches into three directions of space.

Popular videos say a particle such as an electron has to be turned around twice before the electron looks the same as before the turning started and the videos often say the double turn cannot be pictured. An ant on a Mobius strip gives a picture of the double turn because one lap around the strip brings the ant back to the starting spot upside down and only a second lap brings the ant back right side up.

A Mobius strip cannot be turned into a plain untwisted loop by any amount of bending or stretching because removing the half twist requires cutting the ribbon and taping the ribbon back together. The need for a cut could explain why the smallest particles last for such a long time since ordinary bumps can bend a twisted ribbon and cannot remove the twist.

Videos also ask why an electron does not spiral inward and crash into the center of an atom even though the center of the atom attracts the electron. A paper Mobius strip gives one possible reason because a paper strip that is made too short compared with the width of the strip cannot hold the half twist without tearing or passing through the surface of the same strip. A particle made of ribbon would therefore have a smallest possible size which means the ribbon particle would stop shrinking at that smallest size instead of collapsing down to a point. A ribbon particle also has no center point to fall into because the ribbon particle is the whole loop and is not a dot circling around a middle.

A spinning loop behaves like a spinning bicycle wheel because a spinning bicycle wheel pushes back when a person tries to tip or shove the wheel. A spinning ribbon would likewise take effort to move and the effort needed to move the spinning ribbon could be what a person experiences as mass which is the heaviness and resistance to pushing that matter has.

## Entanglement: two particles as one stretched ribbon

Entanglement is the name for a behavior in which two particles that were created together can be carried very far apart and a measurement on one particle seems to settle the result for the other particle at the same instant. Videos call entanglement spooky because the result appears to be shared faster than any signal could travel between the two particles.

Picturing the two particles as one ribbon removes the need for a signal because the two particles would be the two far ends of one single Mobius strip that has been stretched very carefully across the distance. A person measuring at either end would be touching the same ribbon so no message would need to cross the gap. The two ends would only look like two separate particles because a person sees a small patch of ribbon at each location and does not see the long stretch of ribbon running between the two patches.

Measuring an entangled pair can be pictured as cutting the ribbon because a measurement forces the one stretched ribbon to become two separate things. Paper shows what a cut can do because a Mobius strip cut lengthwise at one third of the way in from the edge falls into two separate loops that are still linked through each other like two links of a chain. The two paper loops are created by one single act of cutting which means the shape of one loop is decided at the same moment as the shape of the other loop and neither loop has to send news to the other loop.

A twisted ribbon also has a handedness which means the twist can turn to the left like a left-handed screw or to the right like a right-handed screw. What if the two loops born from one cut always carry opposite handedness so that one loop twists left whenever the other loop twists right? Opposite handedness would match what videos describe when one entangled particle is measured as spinning one way which physicists call spin up and the partner particle is measured as spinning the opposite way which physicists call spin down.

## Wave and particle: closed loop versus unfurled ripple

Videos say light and tiny particles such as electrons behave like spread-out waves in some experiments and behave like small solid dots in other experiments and the videos call the double behavior wave-particle duality.

Picturing matter and light as two states of the same ribbon gives a reason for the double behavior because one material can sit still in one state and travel in another state. A particle of matter would be a ribbon whose two ends are joined into a closed loop and the closed loop would stay in one place because the joined ends hold the twist inside the loop. A hard enough knock such as a collision could snap the closed loop open and an open ribbon cannot stay in one place so the twist would travel outward through space as a rippling corkscrew. The traveling open ribbon would be what a person sees as light which means matter would be ribbon in a closed state and light would be the same ribbon in an open traveling state.

Light can also be pictured as a small piece of ribbon that gets pinched off from a larger strip the way a drop pinches off from a dripping faucet. The pinched-off piece would corkscrew through flat space the way a screw turns through thick pudding because the pudding-like resistance of flat space would give the corkscrew something to push against.

Light being absorbed by matter can be pictured as the reverse of the snapping open because an open ribbon can close again. A traveling ripple that meets a closed strip with a matching shape could wrap around the closed strip until the two ends of the ripple touch and join which would turn the ripple back into a closed loop that stays in one place.

A traveling ripple could also ride along a closed strip the way a roller coaster car rides along a track. At the moment the ripple leaves the closed strip the two ends of the ripple would be very close together so the two ends could join easily into a new closed strip shaped like the first closed strip. A closed strip would therefore work like a mold which is a shape that helps make more copies of the same shape.

The corkscrew picture also offers a reason why two experiments that are set up identically can give different results which is a behavior videos call quantum randomness. A corkscrew flying in a straight line has a tip that keeps circling the whole time so the tip may be on the left side of the circle or on the right side of the circle at the moment the corkscrew strikes a target. A target struck while the tip is on the left side would be pushed a different way than a target struck while the tip is on the right side even though the corkscrew as a whole flew along the same straight line both times.

## Superposition: waves stacked on top of waves

Superposition is the name videos give to the claim that a particle exists in several states at once such as spinning both ways at the same time until someone measures the particle and finds only one state.

Picturing the particle as a ribbon carrying ripples gives a more ordinary meaning to superposition because the surface of one ribbon can carry many ripples at the same time. Some ripples could run clockwise around the ribbon while other ripples run counterclockwise much like white light carries every color of the rainbow at the same time. Ripples add together where the high part of one ripple meets the high part of another ripple and ripples cancel where a high part meets a low part so the visible surface shows only the combined total. A small swell on the ocean can be the combined total of a very tall wave and a nearly as deep dip which means a small visible swell can hide two large waves inside.

A measurement can then be pictured as a filter such as a prism that separates white light into single colors because the measuring device lets one simple ripple through and blocks the other ripples. The particle would never have been in several realities at once because the particle would have been one real ribbon carrying stacked ripples that the measuring device sorted apart.

The direction a person looks from matters too because a Mobius strip shows a different outline from different directions. A ball looks like a circle from every direction while a Mobius strip looks like an eye shape from one direction and like a figure eight from another direction and like a flat line from a third direction. A world built from Mobius strips would therefore show a different face to each observer depending on where the observer stands which could be why videos say the observer plays such a large part in quantum experiments.

## Where matter comes from: flat space crystallizing into twists

Videos say empty space is not truly empty because an invisible field called the Higgs field fills all of space and gives particles their mass. Some videos add that empty space might be resting in an unstable state called a false vacuum which means a state that looks settled and could still tip over into a more settled state.

Picturing empty space as a flat sheet held under tension gives a way to imagine both claims because a stretched flat sheet stores energy the way a stretched rubber sheet stores energy. A twisted loop would be a more relaxed arrangement than a flat sheet because the twist tucks a little of the stretched sheet away. Flat space would therefore tend to relax into twisted loops whenever something such as a collision gives the flat space a nudge.

Very pure water gives an everyday example of the same tipping over because very pure water can stay liquid below the freezing temperature until a single ice crystal is dropped in and then the whole glass freezes outward from the crystal. The first twisted loop would play the part of the ice crystal because the edge of the first loop would bend the neighboring flat space until the neighboring flat space snaps into loops too. Matter in the ribbon picture is therefore not something sitting inside space because matter would be space that has crystallized into twists.

## Gravity: flat space flowing toward twisted space

Videos explain gravity by saying that mass curves space and the explanation is hard to feel as a pull because nothing visible is tugging on a falling object.

Picturing space as flowing gives gravity a more physical feel because a flow is something a person can imagine carrying an object along. A region packed with twisted loops such as a planet or a star would be a region where space has already relaxed so the packed region would sit at lower pressure than the flat space surrounding the packed region. The higher-pressure flat space would flow inward toward the lower-pressure region the way water flows toward a drain. The inward flow would never stop because flat space arriving at the planet would keep relaxing into new simple loops which would keep the pressure near the planet low.

A falling apple in the flowing-space picture is not being pulled by an invisible rope because the falling apple would be carried along by the inward flow of space the way a leaf is carried along by a stream. A kitchen version of the same idea treats twisted loops as bubbles in pudding because bubbles are less dense than pudding so the pudding keeps pressing inward toward the bubbles.

## Magnets and forces: ribbons humming in tune

A magnet held under a wooden table can drag a piece of iron across the top of the table without touching the iron and the wood between the magnet and the iron is not moved at all which videos often present as a puzzle about how a force crosses empty space.

Picturing every ribbon as vibrating extremely fast gives a way to imagine the magnet because vibrations can add together and travel outward. The ribbons inside a magnet would be lined up and vibrating in step like a choir holding one note so the vibrations would add up into one strong hum that spreads outward through space.

A piece of iron would be built from ribbons that naturally ring at the same note as the magnet so the iron would behave like a tuning fork which is a metal fork that starts ringing when a matching note is sung nearby. Two things humming the same note would drift together because moving closer would ease the strain in the space between the two humming things.

Wood would be a jumble of many different ribbon arrangements which is like pressing every key on a piano at the same time. The pure note from the magnet would find no single matching note in the wood so the hum would pass through the wood and reach the iron on the other side.

## Simple loops and knots: atoms and dark matter and dark energy

Videos describe two unseen things in space which are called dark matter and dark energy. Dark matter is the name for extra unseen stuff that seems to surround galaxies because galaxies spin as if more matter were present than telescopes can see. Dark energy is the name for the unknown cause that makes the whole of space stretch faster and faster.

Picturing two kinds of twisted space gives a possible account of both unseen things because one kind would be invisible and the other kind would make atoms. The first kind would be the simple loop with one half twist which has no knot and nothing for other ribbons to grip so the simple loop would not catch light and would not build chemistry and would only take up room. The second kind would be the knot in which several ribbons are braided together so tightly that the braid cannot come undone without a cut. The particles inside atoms such as protons can be pictured as knots of the second kind because protons last an extremely long time.

A knot would be very unlikely to tie by accident so ordinary matter such as a planet could turn nearby flat space only into simple loops. The simple loops answer the question of why the Earth does not grow heavier even though the Earth keeps turning flat space into loops because the new simple loops would add room to space and would not add atoms to the Earth.

Dark energy in the ribbon picture would be the steady pile-up of simple loops because every new simple loop adds a little more room to space and the added room carries galaxies away from one another. Dark matter in the ribbon picture would be simple loops crowded thickly around a galaxy because a thick crowd of simple loops would still draw a gentle inward flow of flat space which acts as gravity. The crowd of simple loops would stay invisible because simple loops have no knots for light to catch on.

Knots would need a forge which means a place violent enough to mash ribbons together until some ribbons braid. The birth of the universe and the collapse of giant stars can be pictured as such forges because both events squeeze matter with extreme force which is where the knots that make atoms would come from. Among all the random tangles made in a forge the shapes that hold together best would last the longest and would nudge neighboring ribbons toward the same shape. The stable shapes would therefore become the common shapes which resembles natural selection among living things where the forms that survive best become the most common forms.

## Black holes: knots melting into one seamless sphere

Videos say three strange things about black holes which are that nothing can leave a black hole including light and that the middle of a black hole is an endlessly small point and that a black hole slowly evaporates over an enormous span of time.

Picturing a black hole as a smooth sphere of space offers a reason for all three claims because a smooth sphere has no loose ends and no middle point. A collapsing star would crush the knots of atoms so hard that the knots unbraid and fuse into one smooth sealed sphere of space with no edges and no seams. The endlessly small point would disappear from the story because the black hole would be the surface of the sphere and would not be a dot in the middle. A strip that touches the sphere would melt into the sphere the way a raindrop melts into the ocean so the sphere would grow a little larger with every strip swallowed.

Light could not leave the sphere because light in the ribbon picture is an open ribbon that snaps loose from an edge and a seamless sphere has no edge and no loose end to snap free. The sphere would not be dragging light back because the sphere would have no way to make light in the first place.

Everything swallowed would leave a ripple on the sphere the way every tap leaves a vibration on the skin of a drum. The record of swallowed things would therefore be kept on the surface as a pattern of ripples which matches a popular idea in physics that the information about everything a black hole swallowed is stored on the outside surface of the black hole.

The ripples on the sphere would be violent enough to shake the flat space just outside the sphere and the shaking would knock flat space into new simple loops. Making new loops costs energy and the energy would come out of the ripples so the sphere would slowly calm down and shrink. A person watching from far away would see the slow loss of energy as a faint glow which would be the ribbon picture of a black hole evaporating.

## Experimental reflection: primitive emotions in ribbon structures

This part of the document is an experimental reflection and is not a claim that Mobius strips feel anything because a ribbon of space has no nervous system and does no reasoning. The question being explored is whether the survival work that emotions do for living bodies such as pushing a body away from danger could show up in a much simpler automatic form inside structures built from ribbons.

An emotion in a living body can be described as two linked parts because the body first detects a condition such as an injury or a shortage of food and then shifts behavior in a way that raises the odds of staying alive. A non-living structure can link the same two parts without any sensing when the shape of the structure makes the condition itself cause the helpful shift. A spinning top is an example because a nudge that tips the spinning top is answered by a wobble that brings the top back upright and nobody would say the spinning top decided anything.

Natural selection is the process in which forms that survive and copy themselves become more common than forms that do not and natural selection does not need life in order to work. Selection only needs many different structures and surroundings that break some structures faster than other structures. Ribbon structures that happen to respond to disturbance in self-preserving ways would last longer and would seed more copies while ribbon structures without such responses would fall apart into simple loops. After enough time the surviving ribbon structures would look as if the surviving structures had primitive emotions because only structures with protective responses would still exist.

Nineteen emotions are sorted below into four groups according to the survival work each emotion does. Each emotion is described first as the work the emotion does in a living body and then as a possible automatic version in a ribbon structure.

### Protecting the structure

Anger in a living body is the surge of energy that follows being hurt or violated and the surge pushes the body to defend against the source of the hurt. A ribbon structure held under tension could do an automatic version because a dent would release the stored tension at the dented spot and the released tension would spring the intruding strip back outward.

Annoyance in a living body is a milder response to small repeated irritations such as being poked and the mild response sets a boundary without a full fight. A flexible ribbon structure could do an automatic version because flexibility would let interlocked twists slip past a small bump the way gear teeth slip so the ribbon structure would shrug off the small bump while still staying near neighboring strips.

Fear in a living body is the alarm that goes off before harm arrives such as when a shadow passes overhead and the alarm prepares the body to hide or run. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because violent regions of space would send ripples ahead of the violent regions. A ribbon structure that tightens or drifts away when the early ripples arrive would avoid the violent region that would have torn the ribbon structure apart.

Wellness is used here as the name for the body-wide alert that damage might happen or is happening or has happened such as the ache that makes a person rest an injured leg. A closed loop could do an automatic version because a closed loop is one continuous surface so a damaged spot would change how the whole loop vibrates and the changed vibration would shift strain away from the damaged spot.

### Managing energy

Hunger in a living body is the drive that grows when fuel runs low and the drive pushes the body to go and find food. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because a ribbon structure that keeps converting nearby flat space into new simple loops would keep the surrounding pressure low which would keep fresh flat space flowing inward as a steady supply.

Frugality in a living body is the tendency to guard stored energy such as an animal resting instead of running when food is scarce. A closed or knotted ribbon structure could do an automatic version because closed paths would keep ripples circulating inside the ribbon structure instead of letting the ripples leak away as light.

Efficiency in a living body is the tendency to notice wasted effort and find an easier way such as an animal taking the shorter path to water. A tangle of ribbons could do an automatic version because random jostling would shake a tangle toward the most relaxed arrangement the same way a knotted paper strip loosens when shaken in the hands.

Tiredness in a living body is the signal that pulls energy away from activity and toward rest and repair because a worn-down body that keeps working breaks down. A layered ribbon structure could do an automatic version because loosely attached outer loops would slow down or drop away first when energy runs low which would leave the remaining energy holding the inner knot together.

### Growth and getting unstuck

Boredom in a living body is the restless discomfort that appears when nothing new is being learned or built and the discomfort pushes the body to go looking for richer surroundings. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version if every complex ribbon structure slowly loses complexity whenever nothing is feeding the structure. A ribbon structure that starts to drift when the surroundings are too quiet would be more likely to reach a richer region than a ribbon structure that stays in the quiet place and decays.

Happiness in a living body is the pleasant state that appears when life is going well and growing and the pleasant state encourages the body to stay with whatever is working. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because a ribbon structure that vibrates in step with incoming ripples would absorb the ripples most easily. The matched vibration would hold the ribbon structure in the place where building outpaces decay.

Love in a living body is the lasting commitment to keep another being or a shared life growing even when keeping going is hard. Two complementary ribbons could do an automatic version because two matching strips could seal together along shared edges so that each strip holds the shape of the other strip under strain that would break either strip alone.

Impulsivity in a living body is the sudden urge to act that breaks a stuck situation such as jumping up after sitting frozen in indecision. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because rings of interlocked twists can jam the way an odd number of gears in a ring jam against one another. Tension would build in the jammed ring until one strip snaps open and shoots away as light which would free the jammed ribbon structure to move again.

Overwhelm in a living body is the shutdown feeling that appears when more is arriving than the body can handle and the shutdown signals that the current plan cannot continue. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because too much incoming energy could pull the ribbon flat. A flattened ribbon would let further ripples pass by instead of absorbing more ripples than the ribbon structure can survive.

### Relating to other structures

Loneliness in a living body is the ache that appears when connection with others is missing and the ache pushes the body to seek company because bodies in groups tend to survive better. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because structures humming the same note would drift toward one another as in the magnet picture earlier in this document. An isolated ribbon structure that decays faster alone would therefore tend to end up inside a group.

Guilt in a living body is the discomfort that follows harming someone in the same group and the discomfort pushes the body to repair the harm. Linked ribbon structures could do an automatic version because a motion that distorts a linked neighbor of the same kind would send strain back through the link. The returning strain would push the first ribbon structure back toward the earlier arrangement that did not distort the neighbor.

Sadness in a living body is the heaviness that follows a loss such as the death of a companion and the heaviness slows the body down and draws others close. A group of ribbon structures could do an automatic version because the breakdown of one member would change the shared hum of the group. The changed hum would slow the remaining members and draw the remaining members inward around the gap.

Embarrassment in a living body is the flush of discomfort that appears when the body notices that others reacted badly to something the body just did and the discomfort stops the behavior. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because neighbors disturbed by a motion would send echoes back to the source of the motion and the echoes would damp the motion that caused the disturbance.

Humor in a living body is the burst of pleasure that appears when two things that seemed unrelated suddenly fit together such as the surprise at the end of a joke. Two ribbon structures could do an automatic version because two shapes that looked unrelated could suddenly lock together. The sudden fit would release tension as a quick shudder that settles into an arrangement with less strain.

Doubt in a living body is the hesitation that appears when pieces of information do not agree with one another and the hesitation keeps the body from committing too early. A ribbon structure could do an automatic version because ripples arriving from different directions that do not agree with one another would prevent the ribbon structure from locking onto any single ripple. The ribbon structure would stay loose until one clear pattern arrives because only a clear pattern gives the ribbon structure something to lock onto.

### A way to explore the idea by hand

A hands-on way to explore primitive emotions in structures is to place paper strips with different cuts and linkages together in a bowl and set the bowl on a vibrating pad. The paper structures still intact after long shaking can be compared with the paper structures that fell apart which would show which shapes shrug off bumps and which shapes cling together and which shapes protect a neighbor. Paper in a bowl cannot prove anything about space and yet the bowl can show whether protective behavior can arise from shape alone with no nervous system involved.

## The ladder of emotional logic: from a human down to a ribbon structure

This part of the document steps away from physics and toward biology and evolutionary thinking. Emotional logic is used here as the name for a set of automatic behaviors that protect and build up a structure such as defending the body or moving toward food. The idea being explored is that emotional logic could be one system that tends to increase complexity in whatever structure carries the behaviors whether or not the structure is conscious. This part is an experimental reflection because nothing here claims that a bacterium or a protein or a ribbon structure has feelings.

The comparison is called a ladder because the comparison moves down five steps from a human to a lizard to a bacterium to a protein to a complex non-living structure and each step is simpler than the step before. The same four survival jobs are checked at each step so that a reader can watch which jobs remain as machinery is removed. The four survival jobs are defending a boundary such as skin and avoiding danger such as a predator and seeking resources such as food and recognizing a matching partner such as a member of the same species.

### Step one: a human

Emotional logic is easiest to see in a human because a human can describe the experience in words. Anger rises when a person's body or dignity is damaged and the anger moves the person to defend a boundary. Fear rises before harm arrives and the fear moves the person to prepare or to leave. Hunger moves the person toward food and boredom moves the person toward new growth because both feelings are uncomfortable until the person acts. Loneliness arises when connection is missing and the loneliness moves the person toward other humans while love holds a bond together under strain.

A human nervous system adds prediction on top of the four survival jobs because the emotions of a human forecast what is likely to happen next based on patterns seen before. A human can also reflect on an emotion and choose among several responses which means the human step carries the four survival jobs plus prediction plus reflection.

### Step two: a lizard

A lizard cannot describe an inner experience in words so the lizard step is read from behavior alone. A lizard defends a boundary because many lizards display and chase when another lizard enters a held territory. A lizard avoids danger because a lizard freezes or bolts when a shadow passes overhead and some lizards drop a tail so that the rest of the body escapes. A lizard seeks resources because a lizard hunts insects and moves into sunlight to warm up and into shade to cool down. A lizard recognizes a matching partner because many lizard species use particular head bobs and throat colors that lizards of the same species respond to.

All four survival jobs are still present at the lizard step because each of the four behaviors above can be observed in lizards. Here is a guess about what has thinned out and the guess rests only on watching lizards from the outside: long-range planning and reflection on the emotion appear to be much reduced while the fast link from a detected condition to a protective action remains fully in place.

### Step three: a bacterium

A bacterium is a single living cell with no nerves and no brain and the four survival jobs still get done. A bacterium defends a boundary because a cell wall keeps the inside of the cell separate from the outside and repair machinery patches damage to the wall. A bacterium avoids danger because many bacteria swim away from poisons and some bacteria seal into a tough resting form called a spore when conditions turn harsh. A bacterium seeks resources because many bacteria swim toward places where food is more concentrated. A bacterium recognizes matching partners because many bacteria release and detect chemical signals from bacteria of the same kind and some bacteria pass genetic material to a matching neighbor through direct contact.

The link between condition and response at the bacterium step is made of chemistry alone because a molecule landing on the surface of the cell changes how the swimming tail of the cell spins. The bacterium step shows that emotional logic in the stripped-down sense of a detected condition causing a protective shift needs no nervous system because structure alone can connect harm nearby to moving away.

### Step four: a protein

A protein is a single large molecule made by living cells and a protein is not alive so the protein step is the first step where life has been removed. A protein is one long chain folded into coils and flat pleated sheets and the folded shape is what lets the protein do work.

A protein defends a boundary in a small way because the folded shape springs back after gentle bumps and many proteins tuck the most fragile parts into the middle of the fold. A protein recognizes a matching partner very well because a pocket on the surface of the protein fits one particular molecule and lets every other molecule drift past. A protein gathers resources only by catching because a protein cannot travel toward a target and can only hold the matching molecule when the matching molecule arrives. A protein does the least of the four survival jobs for avoiding danger because a protein cannot move away from heat or acid and can only shift shape a little when conditions change.

A protein adds one ability that matters for the next step because many proteins hold another molecule and change the held molecule in one particular way such as cutting the held molecule in two. Several proteins can also lock together into a team in which each protein does one part of a longer task.

The protein step shows that a single non-living structure can carry pieces of emotional logic through folded shape alone. The protein step also shows a limit because a protein cannot make a copy of the protein without the rest of the cell so the full set of survival jobs only appears when many shaped parts work together.

### Step five: a complex non-living structure

The last step asks whether the four survival jobs could still get done with life removed entirely and a sufficiently complex Mobius strip structure is the example. A ribbon structure could defend a boundary because stored tension would spring an intruding strip back outward. A ribbon structure could avoid danger because early ripples from a violent region could tighten the ribbon structure or push the ribbon structure away before the violent region arrives. A ribbon structure could seek resources because converting nearby flat space into new loops would keep fresh flat space flowing inward. A ribbon structure could recognize a matching partner because a strip seals edge to edge only with a complementary strip and breaks apart from a strip that does not match.

Nothing at the ribbon step senses or chooses because every response would come from shape. The ribbon step is therefore the most stripped-down version of emotional logic because the protective behaviors remain while everything resembling experience has been taken away.

### Below the ladder: a rock and a crystal

A rock sits below the bottom of the ladder because the inside of a rock is arranged the same way in every surrounding so nothing in the rock connects a dangerous condition to a different behavior. A growing crystal sits one small step above the rock because a growing crystal accepts only molecules that fit the pattern of the crystal which is recognition by shape with none of the other three survival jobs. The rock and the crystal show that complexity alone is not enough because a structure has to respond differently to different conditions before emotional logic can begin.

### What stays the same on every step

The same loop shows up on every step in full or in part because a condition that threatens or feeds the structure causes a shift that protects or builds the structure. The machinery carrying the loop changes from a reflecting nervous system in a human to a simpler nervous system in a lizard to chemistry inside a cell in a bacterium to one folded molecule in a protein to bare shape in a ribbon structure.

The four survival jobs also feed one another because a structure that defends a boundary lasts long enough to gain danger avoidance and a structure that finds resources can support the extra parts needed for recognizing a partner. Emotional logic could then be described as one system that tends toward complexity because each survival job protects the complexity already built and makes room for more complexity. Natural selection would favor the loop wherever the loop appears because structures carrying the loop outlast structures without the loop whether the structures are alive or not.

## Ribbon structures as proteins: shaping other strips and selecting for complexity

A sufficiently complex ribbon structure looks a lot like a protein and the resemblance connects the five-step comparison above to the idea that a non-living structure could copy the structure's own form. This part is an experimental reflection like the two parts before because no one has observed ribbon structures in space.

### How a ribbon structure resembles a protein

A protein in a living cell is one long chain that folds into coils and flat pleated sheets and the coils and sheets then pack together into one working shape. Paper Mobius strips cut two or three times fall into shapes with a similar look because the cut strips form spirals and folded bands and loops nested beside each other like puzzle pieces. Ribbons can also join in two ways much as the parts of a protein do. Some ribbons share one continuous band or are chained through each other which is a permanent join while other ribbons only rest against each other and can come apart again which is a temporary join.

### A ribbon structure as a catalyst

A catalyst is something that makes a particular change happen more easily without being used up by the change. A protein that works as a catalyst holds another molecule in a pocket and the fit of the pocket makes one particular change to the held molecule far more likely. A ribbon structure could work as a catalyst in the same way because a ribbon structure with a pocket of the right shape could nestle a passing strip and bend or twist or cut or join the passing strip in one particular way. The simplest case appears in the earlier part of this document about waves and particles where a closed strip works like a mold that helps a traveling ripple close into a new matching strip.

### Teams of several ribbon structures

Proteins in a cell often work in teams because several proteins lock together into one larger unit and each protein in the unit does one part of a longer task. Several ribbon structures could lock together in the same way so that one ribbon structure holds a strip while a second ribbon structure twists the strip and a third ribbon structure seals the strip. A team of ribbon structures could then build shapes that no single ribbon structure could build alone.

### Why selection could lean toward complexity

A complex ribbon structure that does not shape other strips is a dead end because every complex structure slowly decays and a structure that leaves no copies disappears when the structure breaks down. A complex ribbon structure that shapes other strips into more structures of the same kind leaves copies behind and the copies go on shaping further strips. After a long time the ribbon structures still present would mostly be the ribbon structures whose complexity helps reproduce that same complexity. No goal and no intention is needed for complexity to spread in such a way because the spread follows from reproduction alone.

Selection could also favor teams because a group of ribbon structures in which each member helps make another member of the group would rebuild the whole group again and again. A group that rebuilds every member would outlast any lone ribbon structure that cannot make a copy without help.

### What a self-copying ribbon structure would need

Any form that copies the form's own shape needs three things which are a template meaning an existing shape that guides raw material into the same shape and a steady supply of raw material and a way for the finished copy to come free so that the copy can make further copies. The ribbon picture offers all three because a shaping ribbon structure would be the template and inflowing flat space would be the raw material and cutting plus jostling from neighbors would free the copy.

Two questions remain open about a self-copying ribbon structure. The first question is how a complex form could be copied when a simple loop is easy to copy and a braided knot is not. One possibility is a chain of simple loops copied one loop at a time the way a zipper closes one tooth at a time because each single step would only require the easy kind of copying. The second question is how exact the copying has to be because selection needs copies that are mostly the same and occasionally different so that a version that survives better can appear. The same two questions are asked about how non-living chemistry on the early Earth became the first living cell.

## A worked example: a ribbon structure that behaves like a courting lizard

This part of the document imagines one specific complex ribbon structure and follows the ribbon structure through feeding and signaling and traveling and reproducing because a concrete story shows how the survival behaviors described earlier could chain together. The name lizard ribbon structure is used here for a non-living ribbon structure complex enough to copy the signal-and-approach behavior that real lizards show when a lizard advertises readiness to mate and another lizard of the same species moves toward the advertisement. This part is an experimental reflection because nobody has observed any such ribbon structure and nothing here claims that the lizard ribbon structure feels or decides anything.

### Feeding: taking smaller strips into the structure

A lizard ribbon structure could feed by absorbing smaller Mobius strips that drift into contact with the lizard ribbon structure which is similar to how a real lizard eats an insect. A pocket on the lizard ribbon structure would hold a small strip in place and the lizard ribbon structure would then unfold the small strip and join the ribbon of the small strip into the larger body. The motion and tension stored in the small strip would become part of the lizard ribbon structure so the lizard ribbon structure would gain energy and material with every small strip absorbed.

### Signaling: releasing small strips with one particular shape

A lizard ribbon structure that has absorbed more energy than the lizard ribbon structure needs for upkeep could shed the surplus by pinching off small strips from the outer edge. The pinched-off strips would work as signaling strips because every signaling strip from one kind of lizard ribbon structure would carry the same recognizable shape such as a particular number of twists or a particular tightness of spiral. The signaling strips would drift outward in every direction so the space around a well-fed lizard ribbon structure would hold a cloud of signaling strips that is thickest near the lizard ribbon structure and thinner farther away. The cloud would work as an announcement that a lizard ribbon structure with spare energy for reproduction is nearby even though no ribbon intends to announce anything.

### Detecting: a pocket that fits only the matching signal

A second lizard ribbon structure of the same kind could detect the signal because the second lizard ribbon structure would carry a pocket shaped to fit the signaling strip of that kind and no other strip. A signaling strip from a different kind of ribbon structure would have the wrong number of twists so the wrong signaling strip would slide past the pocket without lodging. The fit works like a key in a lock which means the lizard ribbon structure responds only to signals from the same kind of structure and ignores every other strip drifting by.

### Traveling: a spiral tail that turns when a signal lodges

A signaling strip lodging in the pocket could release a held tension inside the second lizard ribbon structure and the released tension could set a spiral tail turning. The turning spiral tail would push the lizard ribbon structure forward the way a corkscrew pushes through thick pudding because the pudding-like resistance of flat space gives the spiral tail something to push against.

The lizard ribbon structure would not need to know where the signal came from because the thickness of the cloud of signaling strips can do the steering. A lizard ribbon structure heading toward the source would meet signaling strips more and more often so the spiral tail would keep turning and the lizard ribbon structure would keep going. A lizard ribbon structure heading away from the source would meet signaling strips less and less often so the spiral tail would slow and the lizard ribbon structure would tumble to a new random heading. Repeating the keep-going and tumble behavior many times would carry the lizard ribbon structure up the cloud toward the source which is the same trick a swimming bacterium uses to find food.

### Meeting: complementary shapes locking together

The traveling behavior raises the odds that two lizard ribbon structures of the same kind bump into each other because both structures end up in the thickest part of the cloud. Two lizard ribbon structures that touch could lock together when the outer edges carry complementary shapes which means a ridge on one structure fits a groove on the other structure. A ribbon structure of a different kind would carry edges that do not fit so the mismatched pair would bounce apart and no energy would be wasted on a partner that cannot help make a copy.

### Reproducing: pooling spare energy to build a new structure

The locked pair of lizard ribbon structures could then pour spare energy into the space between the two structures and the spare energy would twist nearby flat space and nearby small strips into new ribbon. The shapes of the two locked structures would guide the new ribbon the way a mold guides clay so the new ribbon would fold into a third lizard ribbon structure resembling the two parents. Using two parents as the mold would mix features from both parents which could make each new lizard ribbon structure slightly different from either parent.

The new lizard ribbon structure would break free once the new structure is complete because jostling from surrounding strips would work the new structure loose. The two parent structures would separate with less spare energy than before so the two parents would stop shedding signaling strips until feeding builds the surplus back up.

### Why the whole chain would be favored

Every link in the chain raises the number of copies left behind because feeding supplies the energy and signaling plus traveling brings matching structures together and locking plus pooling builds the copy. A kind of ribbon structure missing any single link would leave fewer copies so over a long time the kinds of ribbon structures carrying the whole chain would become the common kinds. The slight differences between each new structure and the parents would give natural selection something to work on because a version that feeds or signals or travels a little better would leave a few more copies than the other versions.

## The whole picture in one paragraph

Space in the ribbon picture began as a smooth flat sheet under tension and the flat sheet has been relaxing into twisted ribbons ever since because a twisted ribbon is a more relaxed shape than a stretched flat sheet. A closed twisted ribbon would be a particle of matter and an open traveling ribbon would be light. One ribbon stretched between two places would be the linked pair of particles that physicists call entangled and several ripples stacked on one ribbon would be the many-states-at-once behavior that physicists call superposition. Flat space flowing toward crowds of ribbons would be gravity and ribbons humming the same note would be magnetism. Simple unknotted loops would fill out the unseen part of the universe while braided knots would make atoms and knots fused into one seamless sphere would make a black hole. Complex ribbon structures that happen to protect and copy the structures' own shape would outlast the ribbon structures that do not which is how survival behaviors resembling primitive emotions could arise without life. Every one of the pictures comes from the same act of twisting a flat strip and joining the ends which is why a strip of paper and a piece of tape are enough to start exploring the whole story.
